Manitoba

Manitoba is in the middle of Canada, with prairie, big lakes and polar bears on Hudson Bay. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital and biggest city: Winnipeg
📅Became a province: 1870
🌷Flower: prairie crocus
🐻‍❄️Churchill: polar bears and beluga whales

The land: the south is prairie farmland. The middle and north are the Canadian Shield, with forests and lakes, and the far north reaches Hudson Bay. Lake Winnipeg is one of the biggest lakes in Canada. The Red and Assiniboine rivers meet in Winnipeg at The Forks.

The people: Cree, Anishinaabe (Ojibwe), Dakota, Dene and Oji-Cree peoples live here, and Manitoba is the homeland of the Métis Nation. More than half of all Manitobans live in Winnipeg. Saint-Boniface in Winnipeg has a large French-speaking community.

The story: the Métis of the Red River area, led by Louis Riel, stood up for their land and rights. This led to the Manitoba Act, and Manitoba became Canada's fifth province in 1870. Its first nickname was the "postage stamp province" because it was so small then.

Water power: big rivers like the Nelson make hydroelectricity, so most of Manitoba's electricity comes from moving water.

Key words

  • The Forks: the place in Winnipeg where the Red and Assiniboine rivers meet
  • hydroelectricity: electricity made by moving water
  • beluga: a small white whale
  • Red River: a river that flows north into Lake Winnipeg
