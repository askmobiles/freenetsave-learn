Alberta

Alberta has the Rocky Mountains, wide prairies and some of the best dinosaur fossils in the world. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital: Edmonton
🏙️Biggest city: Calgary
📅Became a province: 1905
🌹Flower: wild rose

The land: the Rocky Mountains are in the west. Most of Alberta is prairie and parkland, with farms and wide skies. The north is boreal forest, lakes and the oil sands near Fort McMurray. In the southeast are the badlands, with dry hills and fossils.

The people: First Nations such as the Siksika (Blackfoot), Cree, Dene and Tsuut'ina have lived here for thousands of years, and many Métis communities too. Treaties 6, 7 and 8 cover most of Alberta. Settlers came from Ukraine, Britain, the United States and many other places.

The story: Alberta and Saskatchewan both became provinces on September 1, 1905. Banff became Canada's first national park in 1885. In 1947 a big oil find at Leduc changed Alberta, and oil and gas are now its biggest industry.

Chinook winds: warm winds can blow down from the mountains in winter and melt snow in a few hours!

Key words

  • prairie: wide, flat grassland
  • badlands: dry land with bare, steep hills and many fossils
  • oil sands: sand mixed with thick oil under the ground
  • chinook: a warm, dry winter wind from the mountains
