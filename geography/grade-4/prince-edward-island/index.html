Prince Edward Island

Prince Edward Island is Canada's smallest province, with red soil, beaches and potato farms. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital and biggest city: Charlottetown
📅Joined Canada: 1873
🌸Flower: lady's slipper
🌉Confederation Bridge to New Brunswick

The land: PEI is an island in the Gulf of St. Lawrence. It is the smallest province by area and has gentle hills, farms and sandy beaches. The soil is red because it has a lot of iron in it.

The people: the Mi'kmaq call the island Epekwitk, which means "cradled on the waves." Acadians, and families from Scotland, Ireland and England, settled here. Many people farm or fish, and tourism is big in summer.

The story: in 1864 leaders met at the Charlottetown Conference to talk about joining the colonies. That is why PEI is called the "birthplace of Confederation." But PEI did not join right away; it joined Canada in 1873. In 1997 the Confederation Bridge opened, joining the island to New Brunswick.

Potato island: PEI grows a big share of Canada's potatoes in its red soil. It is also famous for lobster and mussels.

Key words

  • Epekwitk: the Mi'kmaw name for PEI, "cradled on the waves"
  • conference: a big meeting to talk and decide things
  • iron: a metal that makes soil red
  • Gulf of St. Lawrence: the sea between PEI, Quebec and the other Atlantic provinces
