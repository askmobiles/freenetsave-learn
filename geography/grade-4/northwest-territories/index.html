Northwest Territories

The Northwest Territories has giant lakes, Canada's longest river and 11 official languages. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital and biggest city: Yellowknife
📅Part of Canada since 1870
🌸Flower: mountain avens
🗣️11 official languages

The land: the south has boreal forest and the north has treeless tundra. Great Bear Lake is the largest lake that is all inside Canada, and Great Slave Lake is the deepest lake in North America. The Mackenzie River is the longest river in Canada.

The people: about half the people are Indigenous: Dene, Inuvialuit, Gwich'in and Métis. The NWT has 11 official languages, including English, French and nine Indigenous languages such as Tłı̨chǫ and Inuvialuktun. The government works by consensus: members are elected without political parties.

The story: Canada took over the huge North-Western Territory and Rupert's Land in 1870. Over time, provinces and Yukon were carved out of it. In 1999 the eastern part became Nunavut, so today's NWT is much smaller than it once was.

Diamonds and ice roads: diamond mines are a big industry. In winter, trucks drive on roads built on frozen lakes!

Key words

  • tundra: cold, flat land with no trees, only small plants
  • consensus government: a government where members work together without parties
  • ice road: a winter road built on frozen lakes or rivers
  • Dene: a group of First Nations in the NWT and nearby
