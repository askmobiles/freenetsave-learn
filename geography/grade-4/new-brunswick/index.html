New Brunswick

New Brunswick is Canada's only officially bilingual province, with forests, rivers and the highest tides in the world. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital: Fredericton
🏙️Biggest cities: Moncton and Saint John
📅One of the first four provinces: 1867
💜Flower: purple violet

The land: most of New Brunswick is covered in forest. The Appalachian Mountains cross the north and middle. The Saint John River flows through the west. The Bay of Fundy is on the south coast.

The people: the Mi'kmaq, Wolastoqiyik (Maliseet) and Peskotomuhkati (Passamaquoddy) are the First Nations of the province. About one in three people speaks French; many are Acadians, whose families have lived here since the 1600s. In 1969 New Brunswick became officially bilingual, in English and French.

The story: after the American Revolution, thousands of Loyalists came here in 1783. New Brunswick was made a separate colony in 1784. It was one of the four first provinces of Canada in 1867.

Huge tides: the Bay of Fundy has the highest tides in the world. At Hopewell Rocks you can walk on the sea floor at low tide and kayak there at high tide!

Key words

  • bilingual: using two languages
  • tide: the daily rise and fall of the ocean
  • Acadians: French-speaking people of the Maritimes whose families came in the 1600s
  • Loyalists: people who stayed loyal to Britain and moved north after the American Revolution
