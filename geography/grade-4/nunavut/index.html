Nunavut

Nunavut is Canada's newest and largest territory, the homeland of the Inuit. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital and biggest city: Iqaluit, on Baffin Island
📅Became a territory: April 1, 1999
🌸Flower: purple saxifrage
🪨The inuksuk is on its flag

The land: Nunavut is the largest province or territory, about one-fifth of Canada. Most of it is Arctic tundra and islands, including Baffin Island, the largest island in Canada. There are no roads between communities, so people travel by plane, by boat in summer and by snowmobile in winter.

The people: about 85% of the people are Inuit. Inuktitut and Inuinnaqtun are spoken along with English and French, and Inuktitut is often written in syllabics. Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit, traditional Inuit knowledge, guides how the government and schools work.

The story: Inuit leaders worked for many years for their own territory. In 1993 the Nunavut Land Claims Agreement was signed. On April 1, 1999, Nunavut was created from the eastern part of the Northwest Territories. Its name means "our land" in Inuktitut.

Living from the land: hunting and fishing for caribou, seals and Arctic char are still important, and country food is shared with family and community.

Key words

  • Inuit: the Indigenous people of the Arctic
  • inuksuk: a stone figure built to guide travellers
  • syllabics: symbols used to write Inuktitut
  • country food: food from hunting, fishing and gathering on the land
