Yukon

Yukon is Canada's westernmost territory, home of Canada's highest mountain and the Klondike Gold Rush. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital and biggest city: Whitehorse
📅Became a territory: 1898
🌸Flower: fireweed
🏔️Mount Logan: Canada's highest mountain

The land: Yukon is next to Alaska. It has mountains, forests, tundra in the north and the long Yukon River. Mount Logan in Kluane National Park is the highest mountain in Canada. In summer the sun barely sets; in winter the days are very short.

The people: 14 First Nations, including the Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in, Champagne and Aishihik, and Vuntut Gwitchin, have lived here for thousands of years. Most of them have signed self-government agreements and run many of their own affairs. Most Yukoners live in Whitehorse.

The story: in 1896 gold was found in the Klondike, near Dawson City. About 100,000 people set out to get rich. Because so many people came, Yukon was made a separate territory in 1898. Later, the Alaska Highway was built through Yukon in 1942.

Northern lights: on dark winter nights the aurora borealis dances across the Yukon sky.

Key words

  • Klondike: the area near Dawson City where gold was found in 1896
  • self-government: when a people makes many of its own laws and decisions
  • midnight sun: when the sun stays up almost all night in summer
  • aurora borealis: the northern lights
