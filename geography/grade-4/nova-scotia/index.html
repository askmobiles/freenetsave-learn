Nova Scotia

Nova Scotia is almost surrounded by the sea, with fishing villages, lighthouses and a long history. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital and biggest city: Halifax
📅One of the first four provinces: 1867
🌸Flower: mayflower
⛵The Bluenose schooner is on the dime

The land: Nova Scotia is a peninsula joined to New Brunswick by a narrow strip of land, plus Cape Breton Island. No place in the province is more than about 70 km from the sea. The Bay of Fundy is on one side and the Atlantic Ocean on the other.

The people: the Mi'kmaq have lived in Mi'kma'ki for thousands of years. Acadians, African Nova Scotians (with roots going back to the 1600s and 1700s), and Gaels from Scotland are important parts of the province. Nova Scotia means "New Scotland" in Latin.

The story: the French founded Port-Royal in 1605, one of the first European settlements in Canada. In 1848 Nova Scotia became the first colony to win responsible government. In 1917 the Halifax Explosion, when two ships collided, destroyed much of the city.

The sea gives work: fishing, lobster, shipbuilding and the port of Halifax have always been important.

Key words

  • peninsula: land with water on three sides
  • Mi'kma'ki: the homeland of the Mi'kmaq
  • African Nova Scotians: Black Nova Scotians whose families have lived there for centuries
  • schooner: a sailing ship with two or more masts
