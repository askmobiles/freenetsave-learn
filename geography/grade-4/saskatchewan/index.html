Saskatchewan

Saskatchewan is famous for wheat fields, big skies and thousands of lakes. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital: Regina
🏙️Biggest city: Saskatoon
📅Became a province: 1905
🌺Flower: western red lily

The land: the south is prairie, flat and good for farming. The north is the Canadian Shield, with forests and about 100,000 lakes. Saskatchewan is the only province where all four borders are straight lines drawn by people, not rivers or coasts.

The people: Cree, Dene, Saulteaux, Dakota, Lakota and Nakota peoples, and many Métis communities, have lived here a long time. The numbered treaties, such as Treaties 4 and 6, cover the province. Settlers came from Ukraine, Germany, Britain, Scandinavia and many other places.

The story: in 1885 the Métis and some First Nations fought Canada at Batoche to protect their land and rights; Louis Riel led the Métis. Saskatchewan became a province in 1905. In 1947 it started public hospital insurance, which helped lead to medicare for all of Canada.

Breadbasket: Saskatchewan grows a huge share of Canada's wheat and is the world's biggest producer of potash, used to help crops grow.

Key words

  • potash: a salty rock mined to make fertilizer for crops
  • fertilizer: something added to soil to help plants grow
  • medicare: health care paid for by the government
  • Métis: a people with First Nations and European roots and their own culture
