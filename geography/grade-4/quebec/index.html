Quebec

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital: Quebec City
🏙️Biggest city: Montreal
📅One of the first four provinces: 1867
💜Flower: blue flag iris

The land: Quebec is the largest province by area. The north is the Canadian Shield, with forests, lakes and tundra. The St. Lawrence River flows through the south, where most people live. The Appalachian Mountains are in the southeast, out to the Gaspé Peninsula.

The people: French is Quebec's official language. First Nations such as the Cree, Innu, Mohawk (Kanien'kehá:ka) and Wendat live here, and the Inuit live in Nunavik in the far north. Montreal is home to people from all over the world.

The story: Samuel de Champlain founded Quebec City in 1608 as a fur trading post for France. It became the centre of New France. Britain took control in 1763, but French language and culture lived on. Quebec was one of the four first provinces of Canada in 1867.

Sweet fact: Quebec makes most of the world's maple syrup, and its big rivers make lots of hydroelectricity.

Key words

  • New France: the French colony in North America before 1763
  • St. Lawrence River: the big river that flows from Lake Ontario to the Atlantic
  • francophone: a person whose main language is French
  • Nunavik: the Inuit homeland in northern Quebec
