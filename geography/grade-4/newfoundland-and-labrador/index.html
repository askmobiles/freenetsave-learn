Newfoundland and Labrador

Newfoundland and Labrador is Canada's most easterly province and the last to join. Learn its land, people and story.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🏛️Capital and biggest city: St. John's
📅Joined Canada: 1949, the last province
🌿Flower: pitcher plant
🐦Bird: Atlantic puffin

The land: the province has two parts: the island of Newfoundland and Labrador on the mainland. It has rocky coasts, cliffs, forests and the Torngat Mountains in northern Labrador. In spring, icebergs float past the coast. Cape Spear is the most easterly point in Canada.

The people: the Innu and Inuit live in Labrador; the Inuit region is called Nunatsiavut. The Mi'kmaq live in Newfoundland. Many families came from England and Ireland to fish. The province has its own time zone, half an hour ahead of the Maritimes.

The story: around the year 1000, Vikings built a camp at L'Anse aux Meadows, the first known European site in North America. For centuries people fished for cod on the Grand Banks. Newfoundland joined Canada in 1949, led by Joey Smallwood. In 1992 the cod fishery was closed because there were too few fish.

Today: fishing, offshore oil, mining and tourism give work. People are known for music, stories and a friendly welcome.

Key words

  • iceberg: a huge piece of ice floating in the sea
  • Grand Banks: shallow ocean areas off Newfoundland, rich in fish
  • Nunatsiavut: the Inuit region of Labrador
  • moratorium: an official stop, like the 1992 stop on cod fishing
