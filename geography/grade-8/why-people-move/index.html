Learn what you need to know

Migration is moving from one place to live in another. Emigrate means leave a country; immigrate means arrive in one.

➡️Push factors: war, persecution, poverty, lack of jobs, natural disasters, climate change
⬅️Pull factors: jobs, safety, freedom, education, health care, family

Migration can be internal (within a country, like from Newfoundland to Alberta) or international. It can be voluntary or forced.

A refugee has fled their country because of a well-founded fear of persecution or war. An internally displaced person has been forced from home but is still in their own country. Over 100 million people worldwide have been forced from their homes.

Intervening obstacles make moving harder: cost, distance, borders, language and immigration rules.

Canada: about 1 in 4 people was born outside Canada, and immigration is the main source of population growth.

Key words

  • migration: moving from one place to live in another
  • emigrate: to leave your country to live elsewhere
  • immigrate: to come into a new country to live
  • intervening obstacle: something that makes migration harder, like cost or borders
