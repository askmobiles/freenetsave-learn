Learn what you need to know

Economic development is how a country's wealth, jobs and living standards grow. Countries are often grouped as more developed or less developed, but every country is somewhere on a range.

💰GDP per capita: the value of goods and services produced per person
🏭Sectors: primary (resources), secondary (manufacturing), tertiary (services), quaternary (information, research)
📊Wealthier countries usually have most workers in the tertiary sector
🌾Less developed countries often have many workers in farming (primary)

Many factors affect development: history (including colonialism), natural resources, education, health, government stability, debt, trade rules and climate.

Ways to promote development include education (especially for girls), clean water, health care, fair trade, microloans and good government.

Careful with labels: "developing" does not mean a place is backward — cultures, cities and innovations exist everywhere.

Key words

  • GDP per capita: the value of goods and services a country produces, divided by its population
  • economic sector: a part of the economy, like primary, secondary or tertiary
  • colonialism: one country taking control of another and its resources
  • microloan: a very small loan to help someone start a business
