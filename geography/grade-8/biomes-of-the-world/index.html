Learn what you need to know

A biome is a large region with a similar climate, plants and animals. Climate (temperature and precipitation) is the main control.

🌴Tropical rainforest: hot and wet all year; most biodiversity on Earth (Amazon, Congo)
🌾Savanna: hot, with wet and dry seasons; grasses and scattered trees (East Africa)
🏜️Desert: under 250 mm of rain a year; hot (Sahara) or cold (Gobi)
🍂Temperate deciduous forest: four seasons; trees lose leaves (southern Ontario)
🌿Grassland: hot summers, cold winters (Canadian Prairies)
🌲Boreal forest (taiga): long cold winters; conifers (most of Canada)
❄️Tundra: very cold, permafrost, no trees (Canada's far North)

Plants and animals are adapted to their biome: cacti store water, conifers shed snow, caribou have wide hooves for snow.

People change biomes: rainforests cleared for cattle, soy and palm oil; grasslands ploughed for farms; boreal forest logged and mined. Protecting biomes protects biodiversity and the climate.

Canada's biomes: tundra, boreal forest, temperate deciduous forest, grassland, temperate rainforest (B.C. coast) and mountain biomes.

Key words

  • biome: a large region with a similar climate, plants and animals
  • biodiversity: the variety of living things in an area
  • permafrost: ground that stays frozen all year
  • boreal forest: a cold northern forest of mostly conifers
