Learn what you need to know

City land is used in different ways. Planners use zoning to decide what can be built where.

🏠Residential: homes
🛍️Commercial: stores, offices
🏭Industrial: factories, warehouses
🏫Institutional: schools, hospitals, places of worship, government
🚆Transportation: roads, rail, airports
🌳Open space and recreation: parks, sports fields

Urban sprawl is low-density growth spreading onto farmland, which increases car travel. Smart growth builds denser, mixed-use neighbourhoods near transit.

Ontario's Greenbelt protects farmland, forests and wetlands around the Golden Horseshoe from sprawl.

Mixed use: shops below, apartments above — people can walk to what they need.

Key words

  • zoning: rules about how land in each area may be used
  • urban sprawl: low-density city growth spreading into the countryside
  • institutional land: land used for schools, hospitals and government
  • mixed use: combining homes, shops and offices in the same area
