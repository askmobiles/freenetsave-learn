Time zones and modern maps

Learn what you need to know

Earth spins 360° in 24 hours, so it turns 15° each hour. The world is divided into about 24 time zones. Canada has six: Pacific, Mountain, Central, Eastern, Atlantic and Newfoundland (which is 30 minutes off).

The Prime Meridian (0° longitude) runs through Greenwich, England. Going east, clocks are later; going west, clocks are earlier. The International Date Line (about 180°) is where the date changes.

🕐Toronto 12:00 noon (Eastern) → Vancouver 9:00 a.m. (Pacific, 3 h earlier)
🕐Toronto 12:00 → Halifax 1:00 p.m. → St. John's 1:30 p.m.
🕐Toronto 12:00 → London 5:00 p.m. (in winter)

Canada's Sandford Fleming proposed worldwide standard time in 1879 so railways could keep schedules.

Modern maps use GPS (satellites tell your exact location), remote sensing (satellite and drone images) and GIS (computer systems that layer data on maps, like flood risk or bus routes).

Rule: east → add hours; west → subtract hours.

Key words

  • time zone: a region that uses the same standard time
  • Prime Meridian: 0° longitude, through Greenwich, England
  • GPS: Global Positioning System: satellites that find your location
  • GIS: Geographic Information System: software that layers data on maps
