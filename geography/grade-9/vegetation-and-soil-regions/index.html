Vegetation and soil regions

Learn what you need to know

Natural vegetation is the plant life that grows without people. It depends mostly on climate, and in turn shapes the soil beneath it.

❄️Tundra: mosses, lichens, low shrubs; permafrost; no trees
🌲Boreal forest: spruce, pine, fir; Canada's largest region
🍁Mixed forest: conifers and deciduous trees together; southern Ontario and Quebec, Maritimes
🌳Deciduous forest: maple, oak, beech; southernmost Ontario (Carolinian zone)
🌾Grassland: tall and short grasses; the southern Prairies
🌲West coast forest: huge cedar, hemlock and Douglas fir; very wet
🏔️Cordilleran vegetation: changes with elevation up the mountains

Key soils: Chernozem (black, rich in humus) under grasslands is Canada's best farmland. Podzol (grey, acidic, leached) under boreal forest is poor for farming. Tundra soils are thin and frozen by permafrost.

Leaching is when water washes nutrients down out of the topsoil. Humus is dark, decayed plant matter that makes soil fertile.

The chain: climate → vegetation → soil → how people can use the land.

Key words

  • natural vegetation: plants that grow naturally without people planting them
  • humus: dark, decayed plant and animal matter in soil
  • leaching: water washing nutrients down out of the topsoil
  • chernozem: black, fertile grassland soil
