Learn what you need to know

Statistics Canada's census gives a portrait of Canadians every five years. Geographers use these numbers to plan schools, transit and hospitals.

🏙️Largest metropolitan areas: Toronto (about 7 million), Montreal (about 4.5 million), Vancouver (about 3 million), Calgary, Edmonton, Ottawa–Gatineau
🏠Over 80% of Canadians live in urban areas
🗣️About 75% speak English most often at home and about 21% French; over 200 languages are spoken in Canada
🌏About 23% of people are immigrants; India, the Philippines and China are top recent sources
🪶First Nations, Métis and Inuit: about 1.8 million people

Ontario has the most people (about 16 million), then Quebec, British Columbia and Alberta. The three territories together have about 130 000 people.

Reading data: a percentage compares a part to the whole; a rate compares to a set number, like per 1000 people. Always check the date and source.

Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

Key words

  • census: an official count of a population and its characteristics
  • metropolitan area: a large city and the surrounding communities linked to it
  • urban: built-up areas such as cities and towns
  • percent change: how much something grew or shrank compared with its starting value

See real-life examples

Brampton

One of Canada's fastest-growing cities, with many newcomers from South Asia.

Languages

Punjabi, Mandarin, Cantonese, Tagalog, Arabic and Tamil are among the most common non-official languages.

Territories

Nunavut, Yukon and the Northwest Territories cover about 40% of Canada but hold well under 1% of its people.
