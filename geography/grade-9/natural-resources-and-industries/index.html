Learn what you need to know

A natural resource is something from nature that people use. Resources are sorted by whether they run out:

♻️Renewable: can regrow or be replaced if managed well (forests, fish, soil)
⛏️Non-renewable: fixed supply that runs out (oil, natural gas, coal, metals)
🌬️Flow: always available, but must be used where and when it happens (wind, sun, flowing water)

Jobs are grouped into four sectors: primary (take resources: farming, fishing, forestry, mining, oil), secondary (make products: factories, construction), tertiary (services: health, retail, teaching) and quaternary (knowledge: research, information technology).

About three-quarters of Canadian workers are in services, but resource industries still bring in a large share of Canada's exports.

Sustainable use: using resources so future generations can meet their needs too — replanting, quotas, recycling and renewable energy.

Key words

  • renewable resource: a resource that can be replaced naturally if managed well
  • non-renewable resource: a resource with a fixed supply that will run out
  • flow resource: a resource like wind or sunlight that must be used as it occurs
  • sustainable: meeting today's needs without harming future generations
