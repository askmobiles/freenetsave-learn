Learn what you need to know

Canada is the second-largest country in the world, about 10 million km². It has the world's longest coastline and touches three oceans: Pacific, Arctic and Atlantic. It spans six time zones.

During the last ice age, huge ice sheets up to 3 km thick covered almost all of Canada. They melted about 10 000 to 12 000 years ago. Glaciers eroded (scraped) the land in some places and deposited sand and gravel in others.

🧊Glacial erosion: scraped the Shield bare, carved U-shaped valleys and lake basins (the Great Lakes)
🪨Glacial deposits: moraines (ridges of rock and gravel like the Oak Ridges Moraine), drumlins, eskers
💧Meltwater: huge lakes like glacial Lake Agassiz left flat clay plains in Manitoba

A drainage basin is all the land drained by one river system into one body of water. A divide is the high land between basins. Canada has five: Hudson Bay (the largest), Arctic, Atlantic, Pacific and a small part draining to the Gulf of Mexico.

Water facts: Canada has about 20% of the world's fresh water and the Mackenzie is its longest river (about 4200 km).

Key words

  • glacier: a large, slow-moving body of ice
  • moraine: a ridge of rock, sand and gravel left by a glacier
  • drainage basin: all the land drained by one river system
  • divide: high land that separates drainage basins
