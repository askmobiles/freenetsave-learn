Liveable communities

Learn what you need to know

A liveable community is a place where people can live well: safe, affordable homes, jobs, transit, green space, clean air and services nearby.

🏘️Urban sprawl: low-density growth spreading onto farmland; car-dependent
🏢Intensification: adding more homes and jobs in already built-up areas
🚶Complete communities: homes, shops, schools, jobs and parks within walking distance
🚆Transit-oriented development: dense, mixed-use growth around stations
🏭Brownfields: old industrial sites that can be cleaned and reused

Cities face problems like traffic congestion, housing costs, the urban heat island effect (paved areas are hotter) and flooding from hard surfaces. Green infrastructure — trees, green roofs, wetlands and permeable pavement — helps.

In Ontario, provincial plans set where cities can grow and protect farmland and nature in the Greenbelt. Decisions involve trade-offs between more housing, keeping farmland and protecting the environment.

Your ecological footprint is bigger in car-dependent sprawl and smaller in compact, walkable neighbourhoods.

Key words

  • liveable community: a place where people can live well with homes, jobs, services and a healthy environment
  • intensification: adding more homes and jobs within existing built-up areas
  • urban heat island: a city area that is hotter than the surrounding countryside because of pavement and buildings
  • brownfield: an old industrial site that can be cleaned up and reused
