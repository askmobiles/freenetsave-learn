Learn what you need to know

🌲Forests: Canada has about 9% of the world's forests; logging methods include clear-cutting and selective cutting; tree planting replaces harvested areas
🛢️Oil and gas: Canada has the third-largest proven oil reserves, mostly in Alberta's oil sands; crude oil is Canada's top export
🐟Fishing: the Atlantic cod collapse led to a moratorium in 1992 and about 30 000 lost jobs; today quotas limit catches
🚜Farming: only about 7% of Canada's land is farmland; the Prairies grow wheat, canola and pulses; Ontario and Quebec have dairy, corn and soybeans

Clear-cutting removes all trees in an area; it is cheaper but causes erosion and habitat loss. Selective cutting removes only some trees. A sustained yield means harvesting no more than grows back.

Oil sands produce valuable oil and many jobs but use large amounts of water and energy, release greenhouse gases and disturb boreal forest and Indigenous lands. Companies must reclaim mined land.

Farmland near cities is being lost to urban sprawl. Southern Ontario has some of Canada's best farmland, right where cities are growing fastest.

Trade-off: resource industries bring jobs and money, but must be balanced with the environment and Indigenous rights.

Key words

  • clear-cutting: cutting down all the trees in an area
  • selective cutting: cutting only some trees and leaving the rest
  • quota: a limit on how much can be caught or harvested
  • oil sands: sand and clay mixed with thick oil called bitumen
