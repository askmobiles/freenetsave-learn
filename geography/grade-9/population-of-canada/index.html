Population of Canada

Learn what you need to know

Canada has about 41 million people. Most live in the south: about three-quarters live within about 150 km of the U.S. border, where the climate is milder and the land is better for farming and cities.

Population density = people ÷ area. Canada's average is only about 4 people per km², but Toronto has thousands per km².

👶Birth rate: births per 1000 people per year
⚰️Death rate: deaths per 1000 people per year
➕Natural increase = birth rate − death rate
✈️Net migration = immigrants − emigrants
📈Population growth = natural increase + net migration

Canada's fertility rate is about 1.3 children per woman, below the replacement rate of 2.1. Most of Canada's growth now comes from immigration.

A population pyramid shows a population by age and sex. Canada's looks more like a column with a wide middle: the baby boom (people born 1946–1965) is now retiring, so the population is aging.

Indigenous population: about 1.8 million (about 5%), younger and growing faster than the overall population.

Key words

  • population density: the number of people per square kilometre
  • natural increase: birth rate minus death rate
  • replacement rate: about 2.1 children per woman, which keeps a population stable without migration
  • baby boom: the high birth rate in Canada from 1946 to 1965
