Landform regions of Canada

Learn what you need to know

A landform region is a large area with similar rock, relief and history. Canada has eight main regions. Their rock was formed at very different times, from about 4 billion years ago to the present.

🪨Canadian Shield: about half of Canada; very old igneous and metamorphic rock; rich in minerals; rocky with many lakes
🏔️Western Cordillera: young fold mountains made by plates colliding; Mount Logan, 5959 m, is Canada's highest peak
🌾Interior Plains: flat to rolling sedimentary rock; farmland, oil and gas
🏞️Great Lakes–St. Lawrence Lowlands: flat, fertile sedimentary land; most Canadians live here
⛰️Appalachians: old fold mountains worn down by erosion
🌊Hudson Bay Lowlands and Arctic Lowlands: flat, sedimentary, swampy or frozen
❄️Innuitian Mountains: young, rugged mountains in the far Arctic

There are three kinds of rock. Igneous rock forms from cooled magma (granite). Sedimentary rock forms from layers of sediment pressed together (sandstone, limestone); it can hold fossils, oil and gas. Metamorphic rock is rock changed by heat and pressure (marble, gneiss).

Remember: old, worn-down mountains = Appalachians; young, high mountains = Cordillera and Innuitians.

Key words

  • landform region: a large area with similar rock, relief and geological history
  • Canadian Shield: the huge region of very old igneous and metamorphic rock around Hudson Bay
  • sedimentary rock: rock made from layers of sediment pressed together
  • fold mountains: mountains formed when layers of rock are pushed up and bent
