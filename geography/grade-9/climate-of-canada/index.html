Climate of Canada

Learn what you need to know

Weather is the condition of the air today. Climate is the average weather over about 30 years. Six factors explain climate. A handy memory aid is LOWERN:

🌐Latitude: farther from the equator = colder
🌊Ocean currents: the warm North Pacific current keeps British Columbia's coast mild
💨Winds and air masses: westerly winds move weather west to east
⛰️Elevation: temperature drops about 6.5 °C for every 1000 m you climb
🗻Relief: mountains force air up, giving rain on the windward side and a dry rain shadow on the leeward side
💧Nearness to water: water heats and cools slowly, so coasts have mild maritime climates and inland areas have extreme continental climates

On a climate graph, a red line shows the average temperature each month and blue bars show precipitation. The temperature range is warmest month minus coldest month. Winnipeg's range is about 36 °C; Vancouver's is only about 14 °C.

Chinook: a warm, dry wind that comes down the Rockies into southern Alberta and can raise winter temperatures quickly.

Key words

  • climate: the average weather of a place over many years
  • maritime climate: a mild climate with a small temperature range, near an ocean
  • continental climate: a climate with hot summers, cold winters and a large temperature range, far from oceans
  • rain shadow: the dry area on the sheltered side of mountains

See real-life examples

Vancouver vs Winnipeg

Both are at a similar latitude, but Vancouver rarely freezes while Winnipeg often drops below −30 °C.

Chinook arch

A band of clear sky over Calgary often signals a warm chinook wind.

Lake-effect snow

Cold air crossing the Great Lakes picks up moisture and dumps snow on places like London and Barrie.
