Protecting land and water

Learn what you need to know

Protected areas such as national and provincial parks limit development to protect ecosystems. Canada has committed to protect 30% of its land and oceans by 2030 ("30 by 30"), matching a global goal agreed at the COP15 meeting in Montréal in 2022.

🏞️Banff (1885) was Canada's first national park; Algonquin (1893) is Ontario's oldest provincial park
🪶Indigenous Protected and Conserved Areas (IPCAs), such as Thaidene Nëné in the Northwest Territories, are led by Indigenous nations
👀Indigenous Guardians programs monitor land and water using traditional knowledge and science
🌳Ontario's Greenbelt and conservation authorities manage watersheds and floodplains

Sustainable development means meeting today's needs without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs (Brundtland Report, 1987). Tools include environmental assessments before big projects, watershed planning and the precautionary principle — if harm is possible, act carefully even before there is full proof.

Stewardship: taking care of land and water for the next generations — an idea central to many Indigenous teachings.

Key words

  • protected area: land or water set aside to protect nature
  • stewardship: responsible care of land and resources for the future
  • sustainable development: meeting today's needs without harming future generations
  • precautionary principle: acting carefully to prevent possible harm even without full proof
