Forests and fisheries

Canada's forests and oceans are renewable — but only if they are managed so they can renew. The cod collapse shows what happens when they are not.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 geography.

Learn what you need to know

Forests: Canada has about 9% of the world's forests, mostly the boreal forest. In Canada, most forests are on public (Crown) land, and logged areas must be regenerated by law. Clear-cutting removes all trees in an area; selective cutting removes only some.

🌲Sustained yield: harvest no more than the forest can regrow
🏷️Certification (e.g., FSC) labels wood from well-managed forests
🔥Climate change brings more fires and insect outbreaks, like the mountain pine beetle in B.C.

Fisheries — the cod collapse: for centuries the Grand Banks off Newfoundland had huge cod stocks. Large trawlers and too-high quotas caused northern cod to collapse. In 1992 the federal government declared a moratorium (a ban on fishing northern cod), and about 30,000–40,000 people lost their jobs — the largest layoff in Canadian history.

Lesson: a renewable resource can be used up if it is harvested faster than it can renew. Science-based limits and local knowledge are needed.

Key words

  • boreal forest: the northern coniferous forest that covers much of Canada
  • sustained yield: harvesting no more than a resource can replace
  • moratorium: an official stop to an activity
  • quota: a limit on how much can be harvested
