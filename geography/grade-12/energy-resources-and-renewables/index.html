Energy resources and the switch to renewables

How Canada makes and uses energy, and the trade-offs of fossil fuels, nuclear and renewable power.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 geography.

Learn what you need to know

🛢️Non-renewable: oil, natural gas, coal, uranium — limited supply
☀️Renewable: hydro, wind, solar, geothermal, biomass — naturally replaced
⚡Canada makes about 60% of its electricity from hydro; Ontario uses mainly nuclear (about 50%) and hydro

Trade-offs: the oil sands in Alberta bring jobs and government income but release greenhouse gases and affect water and land. Nuclear produces almost no emissions but creates long-lasting radioactive waste. Large hydro dams are clean to run but flood land, sometimes on Indigenous territory. Wind and solar are cheap and clean but vary with weather, so they need storage or backup.

Ontario example: Ontario closed its last coal plant in 2014, the largest single cut of greenhouse gases in North America at the time. Smog days dropped sharply.

Energy transition: moving from fossil fuels to low-carbon energy while keeping power reliable and affordable — and supporting workers in fossil fuel industries.

Key words

  • non-renewable resource: a resource that cannot be replaced in a human lifetime
  • renewable resource: a resource that is naturally replaced
  • oil sands: deposits of sand mixed with heavy oil (bitumen) in Alberta
  • energy transition: the shift from fossil fuels to low-carbon energy
