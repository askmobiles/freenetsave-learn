Population change

Learn what you need to know

World population passed 8 billion in November 2022. Growth depends on the birth rate, death rate and migration. The natural increase is births minus deaths.

1️⃣Stage 1: high births, high deaths, little growth
2️⃣Stage 2: deaths fall (clean water, medicine), births stay high → fast growth
3️⃣Stage 3: births fall (education, cities, women's rights) → growth slows
4️⃣Stage 4: low births, low deaths → little growth (Canada)
5️⃣Stage 5 (some countries): births below deaths → population shrinks (Japan, Italy)

This model is the demographic transition model. A total fertility rate of about 2.1 children per woman keeps a population steady (replacement level). Canada's is about 1.3, so most of its growth comes from immigration. Many countries in sub-Saharan Africa, such as Niger, have rates above 5.

Population pyramids: a wide base means many young people (fast growth); a narrow base and wide top means an aging population, with more retirees and fewer workers.

Key words

  • birth rate: births per 1,000 people in a year
  • total fertility rate: average number of children a woman has in her lifetime
  • demographic transition model: a model showing how birth and death rates change as countries develop
  • aging population: a population with a growing share of older people
