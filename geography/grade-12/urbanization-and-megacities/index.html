Urbanization and megacities

More than half of all people now live in cities. Cities bring opportunity — and challenges like housing, traffic and slums.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 geography.

Learn what you need to know

Urbanization is the growing share of people living in towns and cities. In 1950 about 30% of people lived in cities; today it is about 57%, and the UN expects about 68% by 2050. In Canada over 80% of people are urban.

🏙️A megacity has more than 10 million people: Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, Dhaka, São Paulo, Cairo, Lagos
🧲Push factors (rural): few jobs, drought, conflict. Pull factors (city): jobs, schools, hospitals
🏚️About 1 billion people live in informal settlements (slums) without secure housing or services

Challenges: housing costs, traffic, air pollution, waste, heat islands (cities are hotter than surrounding land), and urban sprawl that covers farmland.

Solutions: public transit, mixed-use neighbourhoods, density near transit, green roofs and parks, upgrading slums with water and sanitation, and planning that protects farmland (Ontario's Greenbelt).

Sustainable city: people can live, work and shop nearby, get around without a car, breathe clean air and afford a home.

Key words

  • urbanization: the increase in the share of people living in cities
  • megacity: a city with more than 10 million people
  • urban sprawl: low-density city growth spreading over rural land
  • heat island: a city area that is hotter than nearby rural land
