Global inequality and development

How do we measure whether a country is "developed", and why are some countries much richer than others?⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 geography.

Learn what you need to know

Measures: GDP per capita (the value of goods and services per person) shows wealth. The Human Development Index (HDI) combines life expectancy, education and income, from 0 to 1. Other measures include literacy rate and infant mortality.

🇨🇦Canada: high HDI (about 0.93), life expectancy about 82 years
🌍Countries with low HDI, such as Chad or South Sudan, have life expectancy near 55 years
📉Since 1990, extreme poverty worldwide has fallen sharply, but progress is uneven

Why the gaps? Historians and geographers point to colonialism (resources taken, borders drawn by outsiders), debt, trade rules that favour rich countries, conflict, corruption, climate and lack of infrastructure. Usually several causes work together.

Responses: the United Nations' 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) for 2030 (e.g., end poverty, zero hunger, quality education); foreign aid; fair trade; microloans to small businesses; debt relief.

Better words: many geographers prefer "Global North / Global South" or "higher-income / lower-income" over "developed / underdeveloped", which can sound like judging a culture.

Key words

  • GDP per capita: the value of goods and services produced per person
  • Human Development Index: a UN measure combining health, education and income
  • Sustainable Development Goals: the UN's 17 global goals for 2030
  • fair trade: a system that pays producers in poorer countries a fairer price
