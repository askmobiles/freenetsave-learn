Ecosystems, biodiversity and human impact

Learn what you need to know

An ecosystem is a community of living things interacting with their environment. Biodiversity is the variety of life — genes, species and ecosystems. Ecosystems provide ecosystem services: pollination, clean water, flood control, carbon storage and food.

🏗️Habitat loss (farms, cities, roads) — the biggest threat
🦠Invasive species: zebra mussels and emerald ash borer in Ontario
🎣Overharvesting: overfishing, overhunting
🧪Pollution, and climate change shifting habitats

Canadian examples: woodland caribou are threatened by habitat disturbance from logging and roads; the monarch butterfly is endangered; wetlands in southern Ontario have been reduced by about 70% since European settlement.

Why it matters: losing one species can affect a whole food web — like removing a block from a tower.

Key words

  • ecosystem: a community of living things and their environment
  • biodiversity: the variety of life in an area
  • ecosystem services: benefits nature gives people, like clean water and pollination
  • invasive species: a non-native species that spreads and harms ecosystems
