Waste, plastics and pollution

Where does our garbage go? Canadians produce a lot of waste — and plastic is a big part of the problem.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 geography.

Learn what you need to know

Canadians produce roughly 700 kg of garbage per person each year — among the highest in the world. Most goes to landfills. Only about 9% of plastic in Canada is recycled; the rest is landfilled, burned or leaks into the environment.

🗑️The waste hierarchy: Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover energy → Landfill (least preferred)
🌊Microplastics (pieces smaller than 5 mm) are found in the Great Lakes, fish, salt and even drinking water
🚫Canada began banning several single-use plastics (checkout bags, straws, cutlery) in 2022; the ban has faced court challenges
🏭Extended producer responsibility: companies pay for collecting and recycling their packaging

Other pollution: air pollution from vehicles and industry harms health; e-waste (old phones and computers) contains valuable and toxic metals and should be recycled properly. In Ontario, Grassy Narrows First Nation has suffered for decades from mercury dumped into the English–Wabigoon River in the 1960s.

Best waste is no waste: reducing and reusing come before recycling.

Key words

  • landfill: a site where garbage is buried
  • microplastics: plastic pieces smaller than 5 mm
  • waste hierarchy: the order of best to worst ways to deal with waste
  • e-waste: discarded electronic devices
