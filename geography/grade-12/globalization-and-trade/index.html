Globalization and trade

Learn what you need to know

Globalization is the growing connection of economies and cultures. It sped up after 1990 with container shipping, cheaper flights, the internet and trade agreements.

📦Global supply chains: a product is designed in one country, its parts made in several, and assembled in another
🤝Trade agreements: CUSMA (Canada–U.S.–Mexico, 2020, replaced NAFTA); CETA with the EU; the World Trade Organization sets global rules
🏢Multinational corporations operate in many countries

Benefits: cheaper goods, more jobs in exporting industries, faster spread of technology, and fewer people in extreme poverty in countries like China and Vietnam.

Costs: some factory jobs moved overseas, low wages and unsafe work in some supply chains (the Rana Plaza garment factory collapse in Bangladesh in 2013 killed more than 1,100 workers), pollution from shipping, and fragile supply chains (shortages in the COVID-19 pandemic).

Canada: trade is about two-thirds of Canada's economy, and the United States is its largest trading partner by far.

Key words

  • globalization: the growing connection of the world's economies and cultures
  • supply chain: all the steps and places involved in making and delivering a product
  • free trade agreement: a deal that lowers or removes tariffs between countries
  • tariff: a tax on imported goods
