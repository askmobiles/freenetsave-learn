Conflict, migration and refugees

More than 100 million people have been forced from their homes. Why do people move, and how do countries respond?⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 geography.

Learn what you need to know

Migration is moving from one place to live in another. It can be voluntary (for work, school or family) or forced (by war, persecution or disaster).

🧳Refugee: a person who fled their country because of a well-founded fear of persecution, protected by the 1951 Refugee Convention
🏠Internally displaced person (IDP): forced to flee but still inside their own country
🌐In 2023 the UN refugee agency (UNHCR) reported more than 110 million forcibly displaced people worldwide

Major causes: wars in Syria, Ukraine, Sudan, Afghanistan and Myanmar (Rohingya); persecution; and climate-related disasters. Most refugees stay in neighbouring countries — Türkiye, Iran, Colombia, Germany and Pakistan host some of the largest numbers.

Canada: resettled more than 40,000 Syrian refugees in 2015–2017 and welcomed many Ukrainians after 2022. Canada's private sponsorship program, where community groups sponsor refugee families, is copied by other countries. Canada also welcomes several hundred thousand economic immigrants each year.

Words matter: a refugee has a legal right to protection; an economic migrant chooses to move for a better life. Both deserve to be treated with dignity.

Key words

  • refugee: a person who fled their country because of persecution or war and cannot safely return
  • internally displaced person: someone forced to flee who stays inside their own country
  • asylum: protection a country gives to a refugee
  • private sponsorship: when Canadian groups support a refugee family to settle
