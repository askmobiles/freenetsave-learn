Food and water security

Enough safe food and clean water are basic needs, yet millions of people lack them — including in parts of Canada.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 geography.

Learn what you need to know

Food security means everyone always has access to enough safe, nutritious food. The world grows enough food for everyone, yet hundreds of millions of people face hunger because of poverty, conflict, climate shocks and waste. About one third of all food is lost or wasted.

🚜The Green Revolution (1960s–70s): new seeds, fertilizer and irrigation greatly increased harvests, but used lots of water and chemicals
🌾Conflict can cut food supplies: the war in Ukraine raised world grain prices in 2022
🥫In Canada, food prices in northern communities are far higher; many families in Nunavut face food insecurity

Water: only about 2.5% of Earth's water is fresh, and most of that is frozen. About 2 billion people lack safely managed drinking water. Agriculture uses about 70% of the fresh water people take. Water stress grows with population, pollution and drought.

In Canada: some First Nations have lived under long-term drinking water advisories for years; the federal government has committed to ending them, and many have been lifted, but some remain.

Key idea: hunger and thirst are usually problems of access and fairness, not of the planet running out.

Key words

  • food security: reliable access to enough safe, nutritious food
  • water stress: when demand for water is close to or above the supply
  • irrigation: supplying water to crops by channels, pipes or sprinklers
  • drinking water advisory: a warning that tap water is not safe to drink without boiling or at all
