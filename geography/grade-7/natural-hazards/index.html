Natural hazards

Learn what you need to know

A natural hazard is a natural event that could harm people. It becomes a disaster when it causes serious damage and loss of life.

🌍Earthquakes: plates slip; measured by magnitude (each step is about 32 times more energy)
🌋Volcanoes: magma erupts as lava, ash and gas
🌊Tsunamis: giant waves caused by undersea earthquakes
🌀Hurricanes: huge storms over warm ocean water
🌪️Tornadoes: spinning columns of air from thunderstorms
🔥Wildfires: spread fast in hot, dry, windy weather
💧Floods: heavy rain, snowmelt or storm surges

The same hazard can hurt poorer places much more: they may have weaker buildings, fewer warning systems and less money to rebuild.

Preparing reduces risk: building codes, early warning systems, emergency kits, evacuation plans, and not building on flood plains.

Canada's hazards: earthquakes in B.C., floods in Manitoba and B.C., wildfires across the West and North, tornadoes in Ontario and the Prairies, and hurricanes in Atlantic Canada.

Key words

  • natural hazard: a natural event that could cause harm
  • disaster: a hazard that causes major damage and loss of life
  • magnitude: a measure of the energy released by an earthquake
  • evacuation: moving people out of danger
