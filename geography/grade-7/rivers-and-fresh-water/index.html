Rivers and fresh water

Learn what you need to know

About 97% of Earth's water is salty ocean water. Only about 3% is fresh, and most of that is frozen in ice caps and glaciers or underground. Less than 1% is easy to use.

A river system collects water from a drainage basin (watershed). It starts at a source (headwaters), is joined by tributaries, and ends at a mouth in a lake or sea.

🌊Nile: the world's longest river (about 6650 km)
🌊Amazon: carries the most water
🍁Mackenzie: Canada's longest river system (about 4200 km)
🍁St. Lawrence: links the Great Lakes to the Atlantic

Canada has about 7% of the world's renewable fresh water and many lakes, but much of it flows north, away from most people.

People use rivers for drinking water, farming, industry, transport, hydroelectric power and recreation. Problems include pollution, dams changing ecosystems, and overuse. Water is a shared resource that needs careful management.

Water scarcity: about 2 billion people do not have safely managed drinking water at home.

Key words

  • drainage basin: all the land drained by a river and its tributaries
  • tributary: a smaller river that flows into a larger one
  • mouth: where a river ends, flowing into a lake or sea
  • fresh water: water that is not salty, like most lakes, rivers and groundwater

See real-life examples

The Great Lakes

They hold about 20% of the world's fresh surface water and supply drinking water to over 40 million people.

Boil-water advisories

Some First Nations communities in Canada have lived under drinking-water advisories for years.

Hydroelectricity

Quebec and Manitoba get most of their electricity from dams on rivers.
