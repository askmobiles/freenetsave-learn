What shapes climate

Learn what you need to know

Weather is the day-to-day condition of the air. Climate is the average weather over 30 years or more. Remember the factors with LOWER:

🌐Latitude: near the equator the Sun's rays are more direct, so it is warmer
🌊Ocean currents: warm and cold currents change coastal temperatures
💨Wind and air masses: winds carry warm or cold, wet or dry air
⛰️Elevation: about 6.5 °C cooler for every 1000 m higher
🏞️Relief: mountains block moist air, making a wet side and a dry "rain shadow"
🌊Near water: lakes and oceans make summers cooler and winters milder

Maritime climates near oceans (Vancouver) have mild winters and cool summers. Continental climates far from oceans (Winnipeg) have very cold winters and hot summers.

A climate graph shows average monthly temperature (a line) and precipitation (bars). The temperature range is warmest month minus coldest month.

Lake-effect snow: cold air crossing the warm Great Lakes picks up moisture and dumps heavy snow on places like Barrie and the snowbelt.

Key words

  • climate: the average weather of a place over 30+ years
  • latitude: distance north or south of the equator
  • rain shadow: the dry side of a mountain range
  • continental climate: a climate far from oceans with large temperature differences

See real-life examples

Vancouver vs Winnipeg

Both are near the same latitude, but Vancouver's January average is about +4 °C and Winnipeg's about −16 °C.

Snowbelt

Towns east of Lake Huron and Georgian Bay get huge lake-effect snowfalls.

Mountain tops

Even near the equator, the top of Mount Kilimanjaro has snow.
