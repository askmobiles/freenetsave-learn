Learn what you need to know

A natural resource is something from nature that people use: water, soil, forests, fish, minerals, oil and sunlight.

♻️Renewable: can be replaced naturally in a human lifetime if used carefully (forests, fish, water)
⛏️Non-renewable: limited, takes millions of years to form (oil, natural gas, coal, metals)
☀️Flow resources: always available (sun, wind, tides)

Resources are not spread evenly. Saudi Arabia has huge oil reserves; Canada has oil sands, minerals, forests and fresh water; the Democratic Republic of the Congo has cobalt used in phone and car batteries.

Primary industries take resources from nature (mining, forestry, fishing, farming). Secondary industries make products (factories). Tertiary industries provide services (stores, teachers, banks).

Sustainability means using resources so future generations can meet their needs too. The 3 Rs — reduce, reuse, recycle — help, as do renewable energy and protecting ecosystems.

Even renewables can run out: the Atlantic cod collapsed in 1992 because it was overfished faster than it could reproduce.

Key words

  • natural resource: something from nature that people use
  • renewable resource: a resource that can be replaced naturally
  • non-renewable resource: a limited resource that cannot be replaced in a human lifetime
  • sustainability: meeting today's needs without harming future generations
