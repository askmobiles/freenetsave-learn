Learn what you need to know

Subsistence farming grows food mainly to feed the farmer's family, often on small plots with hand tools. Commercial farming grows crops or animals to sell, often on large farms with machines.

🌾Canadian Prairies: huge commercial wheat, canola and lentil farms
🍚South and East Asia: rice paddies, often small family farms
☕Tropical plantations: coffee, cocoa, bananas, palm oil for export
🐄Ranching: cattle on large grasslands (Alberta, Argentina)
🏞️Shifting cultivation: clear a small area, farm it a few years, move on (some rainforest regions)

What can be grown depends on climate, soil, landforms and water. Only about 7% of Canada's land is good for farming.

Modern farming feeds billions, but can cause soil erosion, water pollution from fertilizers, loss of forests and habitat, and heavy water use. Sustainable methods include crop rotation, no-till farming, cover crops and efficient irrigation.

Food miles: much of what we eat travels thousands of kilometres. Buying local in season can reduce transport emissions.

Key words

  • subsistence farming: growing food mainly for your own family
  • commercial farming: growing crops or animals to sell
  • crop rotation: changing which crop is grown in a field each year
  • irrigation: supplying water to crops artificially
