Scale and contour lines

How map scales show distance, and how contour lines show the shape of the land.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 7 geography.

Learn what you need to know

A map is much smaller than the real world. The scale tells you how much smaller. It can be written three ways:

📏Ratio: 1 : 50 000 (1 cm on the map = 50 000 cm = 500 m)
💬Statement: "1 cm represents 0.5 km"
📐Line (bar) scale: a ruler drawn on the map

Large-scale maps show a small area in lots of detail (a town). Small-scale maps show a big area with little detail (a world map).

Contour lines join points of the same elevation. The difference between lines is the contour interval.

Lines close together mean steep land; lines far apart mean gentle slopes. Circles inside circles show a hill. Lines that bend into a V pointing uphill show a valley with a stream.

Measuring distance: map distance × scale = real distance. 4 cm on a 1 : 50 000 map is 4 × 500 m = 2 km.

Key words

  • scale: the relationship between distance on a map and real distance
  • contour line: a line joining points of equal elevation
  • contour interval: the height difference between neighbouring contour lines
  • elevation: height above sea level
