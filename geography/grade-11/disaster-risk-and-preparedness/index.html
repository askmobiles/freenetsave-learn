Disaster risk and preparedness

Why do some places suffer more from the same hazard? Risk depends on the hazard, who is exposed, and how vulnerable they are.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 geography.

Learn what you need to know

Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability. A hazard is the event (earthquake, flood). Exposure is who and what is in its path. Vulnerability is how easily they are harmed — poor buildings, poverty, age, disability, lack of warnings.

🏚️Haiti 2010: a magnitude 7.0 earthquake killed over 200,000 people, partly because of poorly built concrete buildings
🏢Chile 2010: a much stronger magnitude 8.8 earthquake killed about 500, thanks to strict building codes
📱Early warning systems: tsunami sirens, Alert Ready, earthquake early-warning apps

The disaster cycle: mitigation (reduce risk before) → preparedness (plans, kits, drills) → response (rescue, shelter) → recovery (rebuild — ideally "build back better").

Be ready (Government of Canada advice): know your risks, make a family plan, and keep a kit with water (about 2 litres per person per day), food, a flashlight, radio, batteries, medication and copies of documents for at least 72 hours.

Big idea: natural hazards are unavoidable, but disasters are made worse — or better — by human decisions.

Key words

  • hazard: a natural event that could cause harm
  • vulnerability: how easily people or places are harmed by a hazard
  • exposure: the people and property in the path of a hazard
  • preparedness: plans and supplies made before a disaster
