Sound and form in poetry

Learn what you need to know

Meter is a pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables. The basic unit is a foot.

da-DUMIamb: "a-LONE" — iambic pentameter has five iambs per line
DUM-daTrochee: "GAR-den"
da-da-DUMAnapest: "in-ter-RUPT"
DUM-da-daDactyl: "MER-ri-ly"
🔊Assonance: repeated vowel sounds — "the light of the fire is a sight"
🔉Consonance: repeated consonant sounds — "pitter patter"
🐍Sibilance: repeated s sounds — soft, sinister or soothing
↪️Enjambment: a sentence runs over the line break
⏸️Caesura: a pause in the middle of a line
🔁Refrain: a repeated line

Forms: sonnet (14 lines; Shakespearean ABAB CDCD EFEF GG ends with a couplet; Petrarchan has an octave and sestet with a turn or volta), villanelle (19 lines, two refrains), ode, elegy, ballad, free verse.

Form follows meaning: a volta marks a shift in thought; enjambment can create rush or surprise; caesura slows the reader down.

Key words

  • meter: the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables
  • iambic pentameter: five iambs (da-DUM) per line
  • enjambment: a sentence continuing past the end of a line
  • volta: the turn or shift in thought in a sonnet
