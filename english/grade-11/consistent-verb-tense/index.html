Consistent verb tense

Keep verb tenses consistent, and use the literary present when writing about texts.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 English.

Learn what you need to know

Shifting tense without a reason confuses readers. Choose a main tense and stay with it unless the time actually changes.

❌Romeo sees Juliet and fell in love.
✅Romeo sees Juliet and falls in love.
❌Yesterday I go to the store and bought milk.
✅Yesterday I went to the store and bought milk.

Literary present: when you discuss what happens in a novel, play, poem or film, use the present tense, because the story happens every time someone reads it: "In Hamlet, the prince hesitates."

A tense change is correct when the time changes: "Gatsby believes he can repeat the past because he loved Daisy years ago."

The past perfect (had + past participle) shows an action that happened before another past action: "By the time the ambulance arrived, the crowd had left."

Check: underline every verb in a paragraph. Each change in tense should match a real change in time.

Key words

  • tense: the form of a verb that shows time
  • literary present: using present tense to discuss events in a text
  • past perfect: had + past participle, for an earlier past action
  • tense shift: a change in tense, correct only if the time changes

Essay feedback

Teachers often mark "tense" when students switch between past and present.

News

Reports use past tense for events: "The council voted on Tuesday."

Book reviews

Reviewers write "The narrator discovers…" in the present tense.
