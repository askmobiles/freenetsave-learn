Argument and logical fallacies

Learn what you need to know

A logical fallacy is an error in reasoning that makes an argument seem stronger than it is.

👤Ad hominem: attacking the person, not the argument
🧍Straw man: distorting the other side's view to make it easy to attack
🐑Bandwagon: "Everyone believes it, so it's true"
⚖️False dilemma: presenting only two options when more exist
⛷️Slippery slope: claiming one step will lead to disaster without evidence
🔁Circular reasoning: the conclusion is used as the evidence
🎓Appeal to (false) authority: trusting someone who is not an expert on the topic
🔗Hasty generalization: a big conclusion from too few examples
⏱️Post hoc: assuming that because B followed A, A caused B
🐟Red herring: changing the subject to distract

To avoid fallacies: attack ideas not people, represent opponents fairly, use enough reliable evidence, and show how causes actually work.

Key words

  • logical fallacy: an error in reasoning
  • straw man: misrepresenting an opponent's argument
  • post hoc: assuming that what came first caused what came next
  • red herring: an irrelevant point that distracts from the issue
