Dashes, hyphens and brackets

Use hyphens to join words, dashes to add emphasis or interruptions, and parentheses and brackets correctly.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 English.

Learn what you need to know

-Hyphen: joins words — a well-known author, twenty-one, self-esteem, re-enter
–En dash: ranges — pages 10–20, 1914–1918, the Toronto–Ottawa train
—Em dash: emphasis or interruption — "She had one goal — to win." (Canadian style often uses spaced en dashes for this.)
( )Parentheses: extra information that is less important (like this)
[ ]Square brackets: your words added inside a quotation: "He [the prime minister] agreed."

Compound adjectives before a noun take a hyphen: "a well-known author", but not after the noun: "the author is well known." Don't hyphenate after adverbs ending in -ly: "a highly respected doctor".

Dashes vs parentheses vs commas: dashes add emphasis, commas are neutral, parentheses play the information down.

Don't overuse dashes. One or two per page keeps them powerful.

Key words

  • hyphen: a short mark (-) that joins words or parts of words
  • em dash: a long dash (—) for emphasis or interruption
  • en dash: a medium dash (–) for ranges
  • square brackets: marks [ ] for words added inside a quotation

See real-life examples

Book titles

"A long-awaited sequel" uses a hyphen before the noun.

Timelines

"World War I (1914–1918)" uses parentheses and an en dash.

News quotations

Reporters add [brackets] to make quotes clear.
