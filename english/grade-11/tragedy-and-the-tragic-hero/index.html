Learn what you need to know

A tragedy is a serious drama in which a main character suffers a downfall, usually ending in death or ruin. Aristotle described it more than 2000 years ago.

👑Tragic hero: a person of high status or great qualities whom the audience admires
⚠️Hamartia (tragic flaw): an error or weakness that leads to the downfall — ambition, pride, indecision, jealousy
🦚Hubris: excessive pride, a common tragic flaw
🔄Peripeteia: a reversal of fortune
💡Anagnorisis: the moment the hero recognizes the truth or their mistake
😢Catharsis: the release of pity and fear the audience feels
🗡️Macbeth: ambition
🤔Hamlet: indecision
💚Othello: jealousy and trust in the wrong person
👴King Lear: pride and poor judgement

Why it matters: tragedy shows how one human weakness, unchecked, can destroy a great person — and asks the audience to learn from it.

Key words

  • tragic hero: an admirable character whose flaw leads to downfall
  • hamartia: the tragic flaw or error
  • hubris: excessive pride
  • catharsis: the emotional release the audience feels

See real-life examples

Stratford Festival

Ontario's Stratford Festival regularly stages Shakespeare's tragedies.

Modern films

Many films follow a tragic arc: a gifted character ruined by pride or greed.

Sports stories

Commentators call a star's fall from glory "a tragedy" when a flaw causes it.
