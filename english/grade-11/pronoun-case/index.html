Pronoun case

Choose between I and me, who and whom, and we and us, even in tricky sentences.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 English.

Learn what you need to know

🧑Subject case: I, you, he, she, it, we, they, who
🎯Object case: me, you, him, her, it, us, them, whom
👜Possessive case: my/mine, your/yours, his, her/hers, its, our/ours, their/theirs, whose

Compound test: cover the other person. "The prize went to Sara and (I/me)" → "went to me", so "Sara and me".

Comparisons with than/as: finish the sentence in your head. "She is taller than I (am)." "I like you more than him" (than I like him) vs "more than he" (than he does).

We/us + noun: remove the noun. "We students want change" (We want). "The rule affects us students" (affects us).

Reflexive pronouns (myself, yourself) are only for when the subject acts on itself or for emphasis: ❌ "Send it to myself and Ali." ✅ "Send it to Ali and me."

Gerunds take possessives: "I appreciate your helping me" (formal style).

Key words

  • subject case: pronouns used as subjects: I, he, we, who
  • object case: pronouns used as objects: me, him, us, whom
  • reflexive pronoun: a pronoun ending in -self or -selves
  • compound: two or more items joined, like "Sara and me"

See real-life examples

Formal emails

"Please contact Ms. Lee or me" is correct; "or myself" is a common error.

Speeches

"We Canadians" uses the subject case because "we" is the subject.

Comparisons

"Nobody works harder than she" sounds formal but is grammatically correct.
