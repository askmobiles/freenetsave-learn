Denotation and connotation

Learn what you need to know

Denotation is a word's dictionary meaning. Connotation is the feeling or association it carries: positive, negative or neutral.

😊Positive: slim, thrifty, confident, curious, home
😐Neutral: thin, careful with money, sure, interested, house
😠Negative: skinny, cheap, arrogant, nosy, shack

Writers choose words with the right connotation to shape how readers feel. A protest can be called a "rally", a "demonstration" or a "mob" — the same event, very different feelings.

Euphemisms soften harsh ideas ("passed away" for died). Dysphemisms make things sound worse.

In analysis: quote a word, name its connotation, and explain the effect on the reader.

Key words

  • denotation: the literal dictionary meaning of a word
  • connotation: the emotional or cultural associations of a word
  • euphemism: a mild word used in place of a harsh one
  • loaded language: words chosen for strong emotional effect

See real-life examples

Real estate ads

"Cozy" often means small; "charming" may mean old.

News

"Freedom fighter" vs "rebel" vs "terrorist" shapes opinions.

Food packaging

"Home-style" and "artisan" carry warm, quality connotations.
