Learn what you need to know

📜Primary source: first-hand evidence — diaries, interviews, photos, original data, speeches
📚Secondary source: analysis of primary sources — textbooks, encyclopedias, reviews, documentaries
🔎Search smart: use specific keywords, quotation marks for exact phrases, and library databases

Evaluate sources with CRAAP:

CCurrency: is it recent enough?
RRelevance: does it fit your topic?
AAuthority: who wrote it, and are they qualified?
AAccuracy: is it supported by evidence and other sources?
PPurpose: is it informing, selling or persuading? Is there bias?

Domains offer clues: .gc.ca (Government of Canada), .edu or university sites, and established news outlets are often reliable, but always check. Wikipedia can be a starting point to find sources, not a final source.

Keep track of every source as you go. In MLA format, a Works Cited entry looks like: Author Last, First. "Title of Article." Website or Publication, Day Month Year, URL.

AI tools: can make up facts and sources ("hallucinations"). Always verify with real sources and follow your school's rules.

Key words

  • primary source: first-hand evidence from the time or person involved
  • secondary source: a source that analyses or interprets primary sources
  • credibility: how trustworthy a source is
  • Works Cited: the MLA list of sources at the end of an essay
