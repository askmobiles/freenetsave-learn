Learn what you need to know

Rhetoric is the art of persuasion. Aristotle described three appeals:

🎓Ethos: credibility and character — "As a nurse for 20 years…"
❤️Pathos: emotion — stories, vivid images, loaded words
🧠Logos: logic — facts, statistics, reasons, cause and effect
🔁Repetition and anaphora: repeating words at the start of lines ("We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds…")
❓Rhetorical question: asked for effect, not an answer
3️⃣Rule of three (tricolon): three parallel items for rhythm
↔️Antithesis: contrasting ideas side by side ("Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country")
🔊Hyperbole: deliberate exaggeration
🙋Direct address: "you" and "we" to involve the audience

Analysing a speech: name the device, quote it, and explain its effect on the audience.

Key words

  • ethos: an appeal to credibility or character
  • pathos: an appeal to emotion
  • logos: an appeal to logic and evidence
  • anaphora: repeating a word or phrase at the start of successive lines or clauses

See real-life examples

Terry Fox

His simple words about running "for the people with cancer" used strong ethos and pathos.

Ads

Toothpaste ads use dentists (ethos) and statistics (logos).

Student council speeches

Candidates often use the rule of three: "fair, fun and focused."
