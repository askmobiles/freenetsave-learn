Writing a summary

Learn what you need to know

A summary restates the main ideas of a text in your own words, much shorter than the original — often about one-quarter or less.

1️⃣Read the whole text and identify its purpose
2️⃣Find the main idea of each section or paragraph
3️⃣Cut examples, details, repetition and your own opinions
4️⃣Write it in your own words, in a logical order
5️⃣Check: accurate, shorter, no opinions, source named

Start with a sentence naming the title, author, type of text and main idea: In "The Story of Terry Fox," the writer explains how Terry's Marathon of Hope inspired Canadians to raise money for cancer research.

For stories, use Somebody Wanted But So Then: who, what they wanted, the problem, what they did, how it ended.

A summary is not: a retelling of every detail, a copy of sentences, or your opinion of the text.

Key words

  • summary: a short restatement of the main ideas in your own words
  • main idea: the most important point of a text or section
  • supporting detail: a fact or example that backs up the main idea
  • objective: not influenced by personal opinions
