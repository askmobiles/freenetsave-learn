Punctuating quotations

Place commas, periods, question marks and citations correctly around quotations.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 English.

Learn what you need to know

1️⃣Periods and commas go inside closing quotation marks: She said, "I'm ready."
2️⃣A comma (or colon for a full-sentence lead-in) introduces a quotation: He shouted, "Wait!"
3️⃣Question and exclamation marks go inside only if they belong to the quotation: She asked, "Are you coming?" / Did she really say "no"?
4️⃣Use a colon after a complete sentence that introduces a quotation: Juliet sums up her problem: "My only love sprung from my only hate."

Blend short quotations into your sentence with no comma: Romeo calls Juliet "the sun."

With an in-text citation (MLA), the period goes after the parentheses: Juliet says "What's in a name?" (2.2.43).

Use single quotation marks for a quotation inside a quotation: She said, "My teacher told us, 'Read chapter five.'"

Block quotations (more than four typed lines in MLA) are indented and have no quotation marks.

Dialogue: start a new paragraph each time the speaker changes.

Key words

  • quotation marks: punctuation that shows someone's exact words
  • in-text citation: a short reference in parentheses showing where a quotation came from
  • block quotation: a long quotation set off and indented without quotation marks
  • blended quotation: a short quotation worked smoothly into your own sentence

See real-life examples

Novels

Dialogue changes paragraph every time a new person speaks.

News stories

Reporters write: "We will rebuild," the mayor said.

British vs Canadian

British writers often use single quotation marks first; Canadian writing uses double.
