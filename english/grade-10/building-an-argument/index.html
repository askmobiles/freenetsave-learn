Building an argument

Learn what you need to know

A strong argument has a claim (your position), reasons (why), and evidence (facts, statistics, examples, expert opinion) for each reason.

A counterargument is the strongest point on the other side. A rebuttal explains why your position still holds. Including both makes you more convincing, because it shows you have thought fairly about the issue.

🔁Concede: "It is true that later start times could affect bus schedules…"
💪Rebut: "…however, boards that changed schedules found students were more alert and attended more often."

Avoid logical fallacies: attacking the person instead of the argument (ad hominem), the bandwagon ("everyone does it"), a false choice between only two options, and a slippery slope (claiming one step will lead to disaster without proof).

Evidence + explanation: evidence alone does not persuade; explain how it proves your reason.

Key words

  • claim: the main position you argue
  • counterargument: the opposing side's strongest point
  • rebuttal: your response showing why your claim still holds
  • logical fallacy: an error in reasoning that weakens an argument
