Agreement: the hard cases

Learn what you need to know

A verb must agree with its subject in number: singular subject, singular verb; plural subject, plural verb. The hard part is finding the real subject.

🔍Ignore phrases between: "The box of crayons <b>is</b> on the desk." (box is)
⚖️Indefinite pronouns that are singular: each, every, everyone, anybody, nobody, neither, either — "Everyone <b>has</b> a locker."
🔀Or / nor: the verb agrees with the closer subject — "Neither the coach nor the players <b>were</b> ready."
🔄Inverted sentences: "There <b>are</b> three reasons." (reasons are)
👥Collective nouns (team, class, family) are usually singular in Canadian English: "The team <b>is</b> winning."
🔢Amounts are singular: "Ten dollars <b>is</b> too much."

Pronoun–antecedent agreement: a pronoun must match the noun it replaces. "Each student brought their own lunch" is now widely accepted, since singular "they" is standard for a person whose gender is unknown or nonbinary.

Trick: cross out prepositional phrases ("of the…", "with the…") to find the true subject.

Key words

  • subject–verb agreement: a verb matching its subject in number
  • indefinite pronoun: a pronoun like everyone or nobody that does not name a specific person
  • collective noun: a noun naming a group, like team or family
  • antecedent: the noun a pronoun refers to

See real-life examples

News headlines

"Government announces…" — a collective noun treated as singular.

Sports

"The Raptors are playing tonight" uses a plural team name with a plural verb.

Style guides

Canadian Press style accepts singular "they" when gender is unknown.
