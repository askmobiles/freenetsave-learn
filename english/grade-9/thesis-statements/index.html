Thesis statements

Learn what you need to know

A thesis statement is one or two sentences, usually at the end of the introduction, that state your main argument and often preview your points.

✅Arguable: someone could reasonably disagree
✅Specific: names exactly what you will prove
✅Focused: can be proven in the length of your essay
❌Not a fact, an announcement ("In this essay I will…") or a question
❌Weak: Social media is used by many teens. (fact)
❌Weak: Social media is good and bad. (vague)
✅Strong: Schools should teach students to manage social media because it affects their sleep, focus and self-esteem.

A simple formula: Topic + claim + reasons. For literature: Title, author + insight + how it is shown. Example: "In Romeo and Juliet, Shakespeare shows that haste leads to tragedy through the rushed marriage, Romeo's quick revenge and the hurried plan with the potion."

Test: Can someone argue against it? Does every body paragraph support it? If not, revise.

Key words

  • thesis statement: a sentence stating the main argument of an essay
  • arguable: able to be reasonably disagreed with
  • claim: a position you will prove
  • preview: a brief list of the points the essay will cover
