Organizing an essay

Plan and build an essay with a strong introduction, body paragraphs and conclusion.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 English.

Learn what you need to know

The introduction opens with a hook (a question, fact, short story or quotation), gives context, then ends with the thesis.

Each body paragraph follows a pattern such as PEEL:

PPoint: a topic sentence that supports the thesis
EEvidence: a quotation, fact, statistic or example
EExplanation: analyse how the evidence proves the point
LLink: connect back to the thesis or lead to the next paragraph

The conclusion restates the thesis in new words, sums up the main points, and ends with a "so what?" — why it matters.

Use transitions to connect ideas: first, furthermore, in addition, however, in contrast, as a result, finally. Order paragraphs logically: most to least important, chronologically, or saving the strongest point for last.

Plan first: a quick outline (thesis + three points + evidence) saves time and keeps the essay focused.

Key words

  • hook: an opening that grabs the reader's attention
  • topic sentence: the first sentence of a paragraph that states its main point
  • transition: a word or phrase that connects ideas
  • outline: a plan of an essay's points and evidence before writing
