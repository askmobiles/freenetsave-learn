Characters and narrators

Learn what you need to know

The protagonist is the main character. The antagonist is the character or force in conflict with them. A foil is a character who contrasts with another to highlight their traits.

🔄Dynamic character: changes in an important way
🧱Static character: stays the same
🎨Round character: complex, with many traits
📄Flat character: simple, one or two traits

Characterization is how an author reveals a character. Direct: the author tells you ("Jay was greedy"). Indirect: you infer from what the character says, does, thinks and looks like, and how others react (STEAL: Speech, Thoughts, Effect on others, Actions, Looks).

1️⃣First person: "I" tells the story; we know only what the narrator knows
3️⃣Third person limited: "she/he/they", following one character's thoughts
👁️Third person omniscient: an all-knowing narrator shares many characters' thoughts
❓Unreliable narrator: a narrator we cannot fully trust

Ask: Who is telling the story? What do they know, and what might they be hiding or missing?

Key words

  • protagonist: the main character of a story
  • antagonist: the character or force in conflict with the protagonist
  • indirect characterization: revealing a character through words, actions, thoughts and others' reactions
  • unreliable narrator: a narrator whose account cannot be fully trusted

See real-life examples

Anne of Green Gables

Anne is a dynamic character who matures over the novel, set on Prince Edward Island.

Diary novels

Books written as diaries use first-person narrators with limited knowledge.

Mystery stories

An unreliable narrator can hide the truth until a final twist.
