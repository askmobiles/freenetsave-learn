Quoting and paraphrasing

Use other people's words and ideas correctly: quoting, paraphrasing, summarizing and giving credit.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 English.

Learn what you need to know

❝Quote: copy the exact words inside quotation marks
🔄Paraphrase: restate one passage in your own words and sentence structure, about the same length
📝Summarize: restate only the main ideas, much shorter
🏷️All three need a citation: say where it came from

Use a quotation when the exact wording matters: a memorable line, a key definition or language you are analysing. Otherwise, paraphrasing often shows better understanding.

Integrate quotations; never drop them in alone. Use a signal phrase: According to…, As the author explains…, or blend a short phrase into your own sentence.

✅Juliet asks "What's in a name?", suggesting that family labels should not decide who we love.
❌"What's in a name?" This shows love.
✂️Ellipsis (…) shows words you removed; square brackets [ ] show words you changed or added for clarity.

Plagiarism means presenting someone else's words or ideas as your own, including changing only a few words (patchwriting) or copying from AI or websites without credit.

Paraphrase method: read, cover the source, write it from memory in your own words, then check and cite.

Key words

  • paraphrase: restating a passage in your own words and structure
  • summary: a short restatement of only the main ideas
  • signal phrase: words that introduce a quotation, such as "According to"
  • plagiarism: presenting someone else's words or ideas as your own
