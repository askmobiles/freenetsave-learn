Media literacy

Analyse media messages: who made them, why, how they persuade, and how to spot bias and false information.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 English.

Learn what you need to know

Media literacy is the ability to access, analyse, evaluate and create media. Every media text is constructed: someone chose what to include, leave out and emphasize.

❓Who made it and who paid for it?
🎯Who is the target audience?
💬What is the message, and what is left out?
🧠What techniques are used to persuade?
🔎How might different people read it differently?
⭐Testimonial / celebrity endorsement
🐑Bandwagon: "Everyone is buying it"
😨Fear appeal
❤️Emotional appeal and music
📊Statistics and "experts"
🧩Glittering generalities: "the best", "natural", "new and improved"

Bias is favouring one side. Look for loaded words, missing viewpoints, and photos chosen to create a feeling. Misinformation is false information shared by mistake; disinformation is spread on purpose.

Check claims with lateral reading: open new tabs and see what other trusted sources say about the site and claim. Watch for edited or AI-generated images and videos.

Before sharing: Stop. Check the source. Find other coverage. Trace the claim to its origin.

Key words

  • target audience: the group a media text is designed to reach
  • bias: favouring one side or viewpoint unfairly
  • disinformation: false information spread on purpose
  • lateral reading: checking a source by seeing what other trusted sources say about it
