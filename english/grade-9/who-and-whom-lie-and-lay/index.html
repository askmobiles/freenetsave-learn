Who and whom, lie and lay

Choose correctly between who and whom, lie and lay, and other tricky pairs.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 English.

Learn what you need to know

Who is a subject (does the action). Whom is an object (receives the action or follows a preposition).

🔁The he/him test: answer the question. If the answer is "he", use who. If it is "him", use whom.
✅Who called? → He called. → Who
✅Whom did you call? → I called him. → Whom
✅To whom should I send it? → to him → whom

Lie means to rest or recline; it takes no object. Lay means to put or place something; it needs an object.

😴lie, lay, has lain: I lie down now. Yesterday I lay down. I have lain here for hours.
📦lay, laid, has laid: Lay the book on the desk. Yesterday I laid it there. I have laid it down.
⚠️Tricky: "lay" is also the past tense of "lie".

Other pairs: sit (rest) vs set (place something); rise (go up) vs raise (lift something).

Object test: if you are putting, placing, setting or lifting something, use lay, set or raise.

Key words

  • subject pronoun: a pronoun that does the action, such as who, he, she
  • object pronoun: a pronoun that receives the action, such as whom, him, her
  • lie: to rest or recline (no object)
  • lay: to put or place something (needs an object)

See real-life examples

Formal letters

"To whom it may concern" is a classic correct use of whom.

Doctor's office

"Please lie down on the bed" — you are resting yourself, so lie.

Bread dough

Dough rises; you raise a flag.
