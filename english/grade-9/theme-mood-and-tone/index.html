Theme, mood and tone

Learn what you need to know

The topic is what a text is about in a word or two (friendship). The theme is the message or insight about life the text suggests, written as a full sentence (True friendship means standing up for someone even when it costs you).

🔍Look at how the main character changes and what they learn
🔍Look at the title, repeated images and the ending
🔍Ask: what does the author want readers to understand about people or life?
⚠️A theme is not a moral command ("Always be kind") and does not name characters

Mood is the feeling the text creates in the reader (tense, peaceful, eerie). Tone is the author's or speaker's attitude toward the subject (sarcastic, admiring, serious).

Writers build mood and tone through diction (word choice), imagery, setting, sentence length and punctuation. Short, choppy sentences build tension; long, flowing ones feel calm.

Memory trick: Mood = Me (how I, the reader, feel). Tone = the Teller's attitude.

Key words

  • theme: the message or insight about life that a text suggests
  • mood: the feeling a text creates in the reader
  • tone: the author's or speaker's attitude toward the subject
  • diction: an author's choice of words

See real-life examples

Movie trailers

Music and quick cuts set a suspenseful or joyful mood before you know the plot.

Restaurant reviews

A reviewer's tone can be enthusiastic, disappointed or sarcastic.

Remembrance Day poems

They often use a solemn, respectful tone.
