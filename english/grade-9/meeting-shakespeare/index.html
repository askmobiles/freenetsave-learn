Learn what you need to know

William Shakespeare (1564–1616) was an English playwright and poet. He wrote about 37 plays and 154 sonnets, many performed at the Globe Theatre in London.

🎭Tragedy: ends in death or downfall (Romeo and Juliet, Macbeth, Hamlet)
😂Comedy: confusion, disguises, ends in marriages (A Midsummer Night's Dream, Twelfth Night)
👑History: English kings (Henry V, Richard III)

Plays are divided into acts and scenes. Many lines are in blank verse (unrhymed iambic pentameter: ten syllables, da-DUM × 5). Servants and comic characters often speak in prose.

🗣️Soliloquy: a character alone on stage speaks their thoughts
🤫Aside: a short comment to the audience that other characters do not hear
😮Dramatic irony: the audience knows something the characters do not
📜Prologue: an opening speech; Romeo and Juliet's is a sonnet

Old words: thou/thee = you, thy/thine = your, art = are, doth = does, wherefore = why. In "Wherefore art thou Romeo?" Juliet asks why he has to be a Montague, not where he is.

Reading tip: read to the punctuation, not the end of each line, and paraphrase each speech in your own words.

Key words

  • soliloquy: a speech in which a character alone on stage speaks their thoughts
  • aside: a brief remark to the audience that other characters do not hear
  • dramatic irony: when the audience knows something a character does not
  • iambic pentameter: a line of ten syllables with five unstressed–stressed beats

Stratford Festival

Stratford, Ontario holds one of North America's biggest Shakespeare festivals every year.

Everyday sayings

"Break the ice", "wild-goose chase" and "heart of gold" all appear in Shakespeare's plays.

Movies

Many films retell his plots, such as modern versions of Romeo and Juliet.
