Parallel structure and modifiers

Learn what you need to know

Parallel structure means using the same grammatical form for items that are joined together in a list or comparison.

❌She likes reading, to swim and hikes.
✅She likes reading, swimming and hiking.
❌The coach told us to stretch, warm up, and that we should drink water.
✅The coach told us to stretch, warm up and drink water.

Watch pairs like not only … but also, either … or and both … and: the words after each part should match.

A modifier is a word or phrase that describes something. A misplaced modifier sits next to the wrong word. A dangling modifier describes something that is not in the sentence at all.

❌Walking to school, the rain soaked me. (The rain was not walking.)
✅Walking to school, I got soaked by the rain.
❌She almost ate the whole pizza. vs She ate almost the whole pizza.
💡Put words like only, almost, just and nearly right before the word they limit.

Test: read each list item on its own after the shared start ("She likes… reading / swimming / hiking"). Each should sound right.

Key words

  • parallel structure: using the same grammatical form for joined items
  • modifier: a word or phrase that describes another word
  • misplaced modifier: a modifier placed next to the wrong word
  • dangling modifier: a modifier with nothing in the sentence for it to describe

See real-life examples

Famous speeches

"Government of the people, by the people, for the people" uses parallel structure for power and rhythm.

Job ads

Lists of duties read better when every item starts with a verb: "greet customers, answer phones, stock shelves."

Funny headlines

Misplaced modifiers make jokes like "I saw a moose driving to Algonquin."
