Active and passive voice

Who is doing the action? Learn when to use active voice and when passive voice makes sense.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 7 English.

Learn what you need to know

💪Active voice: the subject does the action — "Leo kicked the ball."
📦Passive voice: the subject receives the action — "The ball was kicked by Leo."
🔍Passive clue: a form of "be" (is, was, were, been) + a past participle (kicked, eaten, written)

Active voice is usually clearer and shorter, so use it most of the time.

Passive voice is useful when the doer is unknown ("My bike was stolen"), not important ("The school was built in 1960"), or when you want to focus on what happened.

Zombie test: if you can add "by zombies" after the verb and it makes sense, the sentence is passive. "The cake was eaten (by zombies)."

Key words

  • active voice: the subject does the action
  • passive voice: the subject receives the action
  • past participle: a verb form like eaten, written or kicked
  • doer: the person or thing doing the action

See real-life examples

News headlines

"Bank robbed downtown" uses passive voice because the robber is unknown.

Science reports

"The water was heated" focuses on the experiment, not the scientist.

Sports

Commentators use active voice for energy: "She shoots, she scores!"
