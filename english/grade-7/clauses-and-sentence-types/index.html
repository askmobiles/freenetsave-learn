Learn what you need to know

A clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb. A main (independent) clause can stand alone as a sentence. A subordinate (dependent) clause cannot — it starts with a word like because, when, if, although, since or after.

1️⃣Simple: one main clause — "The puppy slept."
2️⃣Compound: two main clauses joined by and, but, or, so — "The puppy slept, but the cat played."
3️⃣Complex: a main clause + a subordinate clause — "The puppy slept because it was tired."
4️⃣Compound-complex: two main clauses + at least one subordinate clause

Comma tip: when a subordinate clause comes first, put a comma after it: "When the bell rang, we left."

Key words

  • clause: a group of words with a subject and a verb
  • main clause: a clause that can stand alone as a sentence
  • subordinate clause: a clause that cannot stand alone
  • complex sentence: a main clause plus a subordinate clause
