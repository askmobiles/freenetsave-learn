Academic honesty and citing sources

Learn what you need to know

Plagiarism is presenting someone else's words, ideas, images or work as your own. It includes copying without quotation marks, paraphrasing too closely, and submitting work written by another person or an AI tool without permission.

✍️Quote: exact words in quotation marks, with a citation
🔄Paraphrase: the idea in your own words and structure, with a citation
📌Summarize: the main idea, much shorter, with a citation
🧠Common knowledge (e.g. Ottawa is the capital) does not need a citation
MLAIn-text: (Author page) — (Smith 42). List: Works Cited
APAIn-text: (Author, year, p.) — (Smith, 2021, p. 42). List: References
🌐Web sources: include author, title, site, date and URL

When in doubt, cite. Citing shows your research and gives credit.

Key words

  • plagiarism: presenting someone else's work or ideas as your own
  • paraphrase: restating an idea in your own words and structure
  • in-text citation: a short reference in the sentence showing the source
  • common knowledge: widely known facts that need no citation
