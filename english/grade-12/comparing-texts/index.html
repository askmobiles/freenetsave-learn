Learn what you need to know

A comparative essay shows how two texts are similar and different, and — most importantly — what the comparison reveals.

🔗Find a basis for comparison: a shared theme, character type, technique or issue
🎯Thesis: name both texts and the insight the comparison gives
📑Block structure: discuss all of Text A, then all of Text B
🔀Point-by-point structure: each paragraph compares both texts on one point
↔️Comparison words: similarly, likewise, both, in contrast, whereas, however, unlike

Point-by-point is usually stronger in senior essays because it keeps the texts in conversation.

Avoid: two separate mini-essays joined by "In conclusion, they are similar and different."

Key words

  • basis for comparison: the shared idea used to compare two texts
  • block structure: discussing one text fully, then the other
  • point-by-point structure: comparing both texts on each point
  • whereas: a word that shows contrast
