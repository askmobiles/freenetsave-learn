Learn what you need to know

;Semicolon: joins two complete sentences that are closely related — "The storm ended; the cleanup began."
;Semicolon with a conjunctive adverb — "I studied; however, the test was hard."
;Semicolons in a complex list — "Halifax, Nova Scotia; Regina, Saskatchewan; and Victoria, B.C."
:Colon: after a complete sentence, to introduce a list, a quotation or an explanation — "She had one goal: to finish."
,Comma splice: two sentences joined only by a comma — fix with a period, semicolon or conjunction

Restrictive vs non-restrictive clauses: "Students who study do better" (no commas — it tells which students). "My sister, who studies law, lives in Ottawa" (commas — extra information).

Never put a colon between a verb and its object: ❌ "The tools are: a hammer and a saw." ✅ "The tools are a hammer and a saw."

Test for a semicolon: could each side stand alone as a sentence? If yes, a semicolon works.

Key words

  • semicolon: a mark (;) that joins closely related complete sentences
  • comma splice: two complete sentences joined only by a comma
  • non-restrictive clause: extra information set off with commas
  • conjunctive adverb: a linking word such as however, therefore or moreover
