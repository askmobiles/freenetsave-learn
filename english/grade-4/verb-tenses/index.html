Verb tenses

Learn what you need to know

A verb is an action word or a being word: run, eat, think, is, was. The tense of a verb tells us when the action happens.

⏪Past: it already happened. Yesterday I <b>walked</b> to school.
▶️Present: it happens now or often. I <b>walk</b> to school every day.
⏩Future: it will happen later. Tomorrow I <b>will walk</b> to school.

Most verbs make the past tense by adding -ed: jump → jumped, play → played. If the verb ends in e, just add -d: bake → baked.

Some verbs are irregular. They change in their own way: go → went, see → saw, eat → ate, run → ran, is → was. You have to learn these by heart.

For the future, put will in front of the verb: I will swim, she will read.

Remember: look for clue words. Yesterday, last week → past. Now, today, every day → present. Tomorrow, next year → future.

Key words

  • verb: a word that shows an action or a state of being
  • tense: the form of a verb that shows when something happens
  • past tense: shows an action that already happened
  • irregular verb: a verb that does not add -ed for the past tense

See real-life examples

A diary

Diaries are mostly written in the past tense: "Today we went to the lake and I caught a fish."

A recipe

Recipes use the present tense to give steps: "Mix the flour. Add two eggs."

A weather forecast

Forecasts use the future tense: "It will snow tonight, and tomorrow will be sunny."
