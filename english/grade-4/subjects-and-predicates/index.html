Subjects and predicates

Every sentence has two parts: who or what (subject) and what happens (predicate).⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 4 English.

Learn what you need to know

A complete sentence has two parts.

👤The subject tells who or what the sentence is about.
🎬The predicate tells what the subject does or is. It always has a verb.

My little brother | splashed in the puddle. Who? My little brother (subject). Did what? Splashed in the puddle (predicate).

The subject is usually at the start, but not always: In the morning, the birds sing.

If a group of words is missing a subject or a predicate, it is a fragment, not a sentence. "Ran to the bus." Who ran? It needs a subject.

Ask two questions: Who or what? (subject) · What happened? (predicate)

Key words

  • subject: the part of a sentence that tells who or what
  • predicate: the part of a sentence that tells what the subject does or is
  • fragment: a group of words missing a subject or predicate
  • complete sentence: a sentence with a subject and a predicate

See real-life examples

Sports news

"The Raptors won the game." Subject: the Raptors. Predicate: won the game.

Text messages

"Coming soon!" is a fragment. "I am coming soon" is a complete sentence.

Science notes

"Water freezes at 0 °C." Subject: water. Predicate: freezes at 0 °C.
