Similes and metaphors

Learn what you need to know

Writers compare two different things to help us picture them. There are two main ways.

🔍A <b>simile</b> compares using <b>like</b> or <b>as</b>: "Her smile was <b>like</b> sunshine." "He is <b>as</b> busy <b>as</b> a bee."
🪄A <b>metaphor</b> says one thing <b>is</b> another: "The classroom <b>was</b> a zoo."

The classroom was not really a zoo. The metaphor means it was loud and wild.

Similes and metaphors are a kind of figurative language: words that do not mean exactly what they say. They make writing more interesting.

Some are so common they are clichés: as cold as ice, as light as a feather. Try making up your own fresh comparisons.

Quick test: see like or as? Simile. Says one thing is another? Metaphor.

Key words

  • simile: a comparison using like or as
  • metaphor: a comparison that says one thing is another
  • figurative language: words that do not mean exactly what they say
  • cliché: a comparison used so often it is no longer fresh

See real-life examples

Songs and poems

Poets use metaphors all the time: "The moon was a silver coin."

Sports talk

"That goalie is a brick wall!" means nothing gets past her.

Everyday talk

"I slept like a log" is a simile that means you slept very deeply.
