Learn what you need to know

Homophones are words that sound the same but are spelled differently and mean different things. Spell-check often misses them, so you need to know them.

🔢two (2) · to (toward) · too (also, very)
📍there (a place) · their (belongs to them) · they're (they are)
🐻bear (animal) · bare (nothing on)
👀see (look) · sea (ocean)
🌸flower (plant) · flour (for baking)
🏃right (correct) · write (with a pencil)

Use the meaning of the sentence to choose the right spelling. Read the sentence and ask: which meaning fits?

Memory tricks help: their has heir in it (someone who owns things). There has here in it (a place). Too has too many o's.

Remember: homophones are checked by meaning, not by sound.

Key words

  • homophone: a word that sounds like another word but has a different spelling and meaning
  • meaning: what a word stands for
  • spelling: the letters in a word, in order
  • context: the words around a word that help show its meaning

See real-life examples

Baking

A recipe needs flour, not flower. Mixing them up would make a strange cake!

A party invitation

"Their party is at two o'clock. We're going there too." Five homophones in two sentences.

Signs at the park

"Do not feed the bears" — not "bares". The meaning changes completely.
