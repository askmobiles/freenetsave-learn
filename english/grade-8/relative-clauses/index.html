Relative clauses

Use who, whom, whose, which and that to add information about a noun.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 English.

Learn what you need to know

A relative clause adds information about a noun. It starts with a relative pronoun.

🧑who — people (subject): "the girl who won"
🙋whom — people (object): "the coach whom we thanked"
👜whose — possession: "the boy whose bike was stolen"
📦which — things, often with commas: "My bike, which is red, is new."
📦that — people or things, no commas: "the book that I borrowed"

Essential (restrictive) clauses tell us WHICH one; no commas: "Students who finish early may read." Non-essential clauses add extra information; use commas: "My sister, who is 16, drives."

In most Canadian writing, use that for essential clauses about things and which (with commas) for extra information.

whose vs who's: whose = possession; who's = who is.

Key words

  • relative clause: a clause that describes a noun, starting with who, which, that, etc.
  • relative pronoun: who, whom, whose, which, that
  • essential clause: information needed to identify the noun (no commas)
  • non-essential clause: extra information set off by commas

Book blurbs

"A girl who can talk to animals must save her town."

Wanted posters

"The person who took my lunch, please return it."

Wikipedia
