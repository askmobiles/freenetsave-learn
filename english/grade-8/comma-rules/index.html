Comma rules

Use commas correctly in lists, after introductions, with joining words and around extra information.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 English.

Learn what you need to know

📋Lists: "We bought apples, bread and milk." (A comma before "and" — the Oxford comma — is optional; be consistent.)
🚪After an introduction: "After school, we played soccer." "However, it rained."
🔗Before FANBOYS joining two sentences: "I was tired, so I went to bed."
🧩Around extra (non-essential) information: "My brother, who is twelve, loves hockey."
🗣️Direct address: "Maya, please sit down."
📅Dates and places: "Toronto, Ontario" "July 1, 1867"

Don't put a comma between a subject and its verb: ❌ "My best friend, lives in Calgary."

Don't join two full sentences with only a comma (comma splice): ❌ "It snowed, school closed." ✅ "It snowed, so school closed."

Extra-information test: if you can remove the words and the sentence still makes sense, put commas around them.

Key words

  • comma splice: two sentences joined by only a comma
  • introductory element: a word or phrase at the start of a sentence
  • non-essential clause: extra information that can be removed
  • Oxford comma: a comma before "and" at the end of a list

See real-life examples

Grandma's message

"Let's eat, Grandma!" vs "Let's eat Grandma!" — commas save lives.

Addresses

"Brampton, Ontario" uses a comma between city and province.

Letters

"Dear Ms. Singh," starts a friendly letter with a comma.
