Learn what you need to know

Poems are built from lines grouped into stanzas. Poets choose every word for sound and meaning.

🔊Rhyme and rhyme scheme (ABAB, AABB)
🥁Rhythm: the beat of stressed and unstressed syllables
🐍Alliteration: repeated first sounds — "slippery slithering snake"
💥Onomatopoeia: sound words — buzz, crash, sizzle
🔁Repetition of words or lines for emphasis
🦁Simile: compares using like or as — "brave as a lion"
🌊Metaphor: says one thing IS another — "the classroom was a zoo"
🌳Personification: gives human traits to things — "the wind whispered"
🏔️Hyperbole: exaggeration — "I've told you a million times"
👁️Imagery: words that appeal to the senses

Forms: haiku (3 lines: 5-7-5 syllables), free verse (no set rhyme or rhythm), limerick (5 funny lines, AABBA), sonnet (14 lines), concrete poem (shaped like its subject).

Analysing a poem: name the device, quote it, and explain the effect or feeling it creates.

Key words

  • stanza: a group of lines in a poem, like a paragraph
  • rhyme scheme: the pattern of rhymes, shown with letters like ABAB
  • free verse: poetry without a set rhyme or rhythm
  • figurative language: words used beyond their literal meaning to create images
