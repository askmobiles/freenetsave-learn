Semicolons and colons

Use semicolons to join closely related sentences and colons to introduce lists, explanations and quotations.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 English.

Learn what you need to know

A semicolon (;) joins two complete sentences that are closely related: "It was snowing; the roads were icy."

🔗Before words like however, therefore, instead: "I studied; however, the test was hard."
📋In complicated lists that already contain commas: "We visited Halifax, Nova Scotia; Moncton, New Brunswick; and Charlottetown, PEI."

A colon (:) comes after a complete sentence to introduce what follows.

📋A list: "Bring three things: a pencil, an eraser and a ruler."
💡An explanation: "There was one problem: nobody had a key."
💬A quotation: "My grandma gave me one rule: 'Always be kind.'"
⏰Times and ratios: 3:45, 2:1

Don't use a colon after a verb or preposition: ❌ "My favourite sports are: hockey and soccer." ✅ "My favourite sports are hockey and soccer."

Test: a semicolon needs a full sentence on BOTH sides. A colon needs a full sentence BEFORE it.

Key words

  • semicolon: punctuation ; that joins related sentences
  • colon: punctuation : that introduces a list or explanation
  • independent clause: a group of words that can stand alone as a sentence
  • conjunctive adverb: a word like however or therefore that links ideas
