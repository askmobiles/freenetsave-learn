Verbals

Gerunds, participles and infinitives: verb forms that do the job of nouns, adjectives and adverbs.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 English.

Learn what you need to know

A verbal looks like a verb but does a different job in the sentence.

🏊Gerund: -ing word used as a NOUN. "Swimming is fun." "I enjoy reading."
🔥Participle: -ing or -ed word used as an ADJECTIVE. "The barking dog" "a broken window"
🎯Infinitive: to + verb, used as a noun, adjective or adverb. "I want to win." "She studied to pass."

Test for a gerund: can you replace it with "it" or "something"? "Swimming is fun" → "It is fun." Test for a participle: does it describe a noun? "the barking dog" → which dog? the barking one.

A verbal phrase is the verbal plus its words: "Running for the bus, Maya dropped her phone." Make sure the phrase sits next to the noun it describes.

Remember: a verbal is never the main verb. "Running to class" is not a sentence; "I was running to class" is.

Key words

  • gerund: an -ing verb form used as a noun
  • participle: a verb form (-ing or -ed) used as an adjective
  • infinitive: to + the base form of a verb
  • verbal phrase: a verbal with the words that go with it
