Modifiers in the right place

Put describing words and phrases right next to what they describe, so your sentences say what you mean.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 English.

Learn what you need to know

A modifier is a word or phrase that describes something. If it is in the wrong place, the sentence can say something silly or confusing.

😂Misplaced: "I saw a bear driving to the cottage." (Was the bear driving?)
✅Fixed: "Driving to the cottage, I saw a bear."
😂Dangling: "Running to class, my books fell." (Books can't run.)
✅Fixed: "Running to class, I dropped my books."

Limiting words like only, just, almost, nearly, even change meaning depending on where they go: "Only I ate cake" (no one else) vs "I ate only cake" (nothing else).

Rule: put the modifier as close as possible to the word it describes. After an opening -ing phrase, name the person doing the action.

Key words

  • modifier: a word or phrase that describes another word
  • misplaced modifier: a modifier next to the wrong word
  • dangling modifier: a modifier with nothing in the sentence to describe
  • limiting modifier: a word like only or just whose position changes meaning

See real-life examples

Newspaper bloopers

"Lost: dog by owner with three legs" makes readers laugh for the wrong reason.

Instructions

"Only take one cookie" vs "Take only one cookie".

Ads

"For sale: piano by a lady with carved legs."
