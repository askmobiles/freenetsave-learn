Who is telling the story?

Point of view: first person, second person and third person — and how the narrator changes what we know.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 6 English.

Learn what you need to know

🙋First person: the narrator is a character and uses I, me, we — "I opened the door."
👉Second person: speaks to the reader as "you" — "You open the door."
👀Third person: the narrator is outside the story and uses he, she, they — "She opened the door."
🔦Third person limited: knows one character's thoughts
🌐Third person omniscient: knows every character's thoughts

A first-person narrator can only tell us what they see, hear and think — so they might not know everything, or might not be telling the whole truth!

Look for pronouns outside of dialogue to find the point of view.

Key words

  • narrator: the voice telling the story
  • point of view: who is telling the story and how much they know
  • first person: told by a character using I and me
  • third person omniscient: an all-knowing narrator outside the story

See real-life examples

Diaries

Diary stories are written in first person.

Choose-your-own-adventure books

Often use second person: "You decide…"

Fairy tales

Usually third person: "Once upon a time, there was…"
