Learn what you need to know

Around 1800, many different groups lived in what is now Ontario and Quebec. Some had been here for thousands of years; others had just arrived.

🪶First Nations: such as the Anishinaabe and Haudenosaunee, here for thousands of years
⛪Canadiens: French-speaking settlers along the St. Lawrence River
🧳Loyalists: people loyal to Britain who left the United States after the American Revolution (1783)
✊🏿Black Loyalists and other Black settlers, some escaping slavery

In 1791 the land was split into Upper Canada (now Ontario) and Lower Canada (now Quebec). The town of York (now Toronto) became the capital of Upper Canada in 1796.

Haudenosaunee people led by Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea) settled along the Grand River after the American Revolution. Today this is Six Nations of the Grand River.

Remember: early Canada was home to First Nations, Canadiens, Loyalists, Black settlers and newcomers from Britain.

Key words

  • Loyalist: a person who stayed loyal to Britain and moved north after 1783
  • Canadien: a French-speaking settler in early Canada
  • settler: a person who comes to live and farm on new land
  • Upper Canada: the old name for most of what is now Ontario
