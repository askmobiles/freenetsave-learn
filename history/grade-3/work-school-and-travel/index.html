Learn what you need to know

In the early 1800s, children worked hard: fetching water, feeding animals, gathering firewood, weeding and helping with younger children.

🏫One-room school: all ages with one teacher, slates and chalk
🌾Grist mill: ground grain into flour using water power
🔨Blacksmith: made horseshoes, nails and tools from iron
🏪General store: sold tea, sugar, cloth and tools, and held the mail

Travel was slow. Roads were dirt trails that turned to mud. Corduroy roads were made of logs laid side by side. People also travelled by canoe, bateau (flat boat), sleigh in winter, and later stagecoach and steamboat.

The Rideau Canal (opened 1832) linked Kingston and Bytown (now Ottawa) by water. Today it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a famous skating rink in winter.

Remember: children worked and went to one-room schools; travel was slow on mud, logs and water.

Key words

  • grist mill: a mill that grinds grain into flour
  • blacksmith: a worker who shapes hot iron into tools
  • corduroy road: a bumpy road made of logs
  • canal: a waterway dug by people for boats
