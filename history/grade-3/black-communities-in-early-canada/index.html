Black communities in early Canada

Learn what you need to know

Black people have lived in Canada for over 400 years. Some were enslaved here in early times. Others came as free people or escaped slavery in the United States.

⚓Birchtown, Nova Scotia (1783): one of the largest free Black communities of its time, founded by Black Loyalists
🌾Oro Township, Ontario (from 1819): Black settlers given land near Lake Simcoe
🏡Buxton (Elgin Settlement), near Chatham (1849): a farming community with its own school
⭐Dawn Settlement, near Dresden: started by Josiah Henson

In 1793, Upper Canada passed a law that stopped bringing enslaved people into the colony. In 1834, slavery ended across the British Empire, including Canada.

The Underground Railroad was a secret network of people and safe houses that helped thousands of enslaved people escape to Canada, where they could be free. Harriet Tubman led many to freedom and lived for a time in St. Catharines, Ontario.

Remember: Black communities helped build early Canada. Many people found freedom here through the Underground Railroad.

Key words

  • enslaved: forced to work without pay or freedom
  • Underground Railroad: a secret network that helped people escape slavery
  • freedom: being free to make your own choices
  • settlement: a place where people make a new community
