Building a home in the forest

Learn what you need to know

Most of Upper Canada was thick forest. A settler family first had to clear the land by cutting trees with axes, then pull or burn the stumps.

🪵Log cabin: walls of logs, gaps filled with mud and moss
🔥Fireplace: for heat, light and cooking
🐂Oxen: strong animals to pull stumps and ploughs
🧺Potash: ash from burned trees, sold to make soap and glass

Neighbours helped each other at bees: a barn-raising bee to build a barn in one day, a logging bee to clear land, or a quilting bee to make quilts. After the work, there was often food and dancing.

The first home was often a small one-room log cabin. Later, families built bigger frame houses with boards from a sawmill.

Remember: settlers cleared forest, built log cabins and worked together at bees.

Key words

  • settler: a person who moves to new land to live and farm
  • bee: a gathering where neighbours work together
  • log cabin: a small house built from logs
  • potash: a powder made from wood ash
