First Nations, Inuit and Métis

Learn what you need to know

Indigenous peoples are the first peoples of the land now called Canada. There are three main groups: First Nations, Inuit and Métis.

🪶First Nations: many nations with different languages, like the Anishinaabe, Haudenosaunee, Cree and Mi'kmaq
🧊Inuit: live in the Arctic; hunted seals and caribou; built igloos in winter
🎻Métis: a people with First Nations and European roots, with their own culture and language, Michif
🌽The Three Sisters: corn, beans and squash grown together by the Haudenosaunee

The Haudenosaunee lived in villages of longhouses. The Anishinaabe travelled by birchbark canoe and lived in wigwams. Inuit used kayaks and dog sleds (qamutiit).

Indigenous peoples used the land carefully and took only what they needed. Many teachings speak of caring for the land for the next seven generations.

Remember: First Nations, Inuit and Métis are three distinct peoples, each with many communities, languages and traditions.

Key words

  • Indigenous: the first peoples of a land
  • longhouse: a long wooden home shared by several Haudenosaunee families
  • igloo: a snow house built by Inuit
  • Métis: a distinct people with First Nations and European roots

See real-life examples

Nunavut

Nunavut, Canada's newest territory (1999), has an Inuit majority and Inuktitut as an official language.

The canoe

The birchbark canoe, made by First Nations, became the main way to travel Canada's rivers.

Louis Riel Day

Manitoba honours Métis leader Louis Riel on the third Monday of February.
