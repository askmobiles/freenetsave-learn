Remembrance Day: how we remember

Learn what you need to know

Remembrance Day is on November 11. We remember the Canadians who served and died in wars and peacekeeping, and those who serve today.

The First World War ended with an agreement (an armistice) at the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month in 1918. That is why we pause at 11 a.m.

🌺The red poppy: poppies grew on battlefields in Flanders, Belgium
📝"In Flanders Fields": a poem by Canadian doctor John McCrae (1915)
🤫Two minutes of silence at 11 a.m.
🎺The Last Post: a bugle call played at ceremonies

Ceremonies are held at cenotaphs (war memorials) in towns across Canada. The National War Memorial in Ottawa has the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier.

Remember: 11 a.m., November 11. We wear a poppy and stay silent to honour those who served.

Key words

  • armistice: an agreement to stop fighting
  • veteran: a person who served in the armed forces
  • cenotaph: a monument to people who died in war
  • peacekeeper: a soldier who helps keep peace in another country
