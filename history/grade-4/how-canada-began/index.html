Learn what you need to know

Before 1867, there were separate British colonies in North America. Their leaders met to talk about joining together. This joining is called Confederation.

🏝️1864: meetings in Charlottetown (PEI) and Quebec City
📜1867: the British North America Act creates the Dominion of Canada
🎉July 1, 1867: Canada's birthday
🗺️First 4 provinces: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick

The leaders who planned Confederation are called the Fathers of Confederation. Sir John A. Macdonald became Canada's first Prime Minister. George-Étienne Cartier brought French Canada on board.

Why join? To build a railway, to grow trade, and to be stronger against the United States. Other provinces and territories joined later; Nunavut was the last, in 1999.

First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples were not invited to the Confederation meetings, even though the land was theirs first.

Remember: Confederation, July 1, 1867. Four provinces. First Prime Minister: John A. Macdonald.

Key words

  • Confederation: the joining of colonies to form Canada in 1867
  • colony: a place ruled by another country
  • Prime Minister: the leader of Canada's government
  • province: one of the large areas that make up Canada
