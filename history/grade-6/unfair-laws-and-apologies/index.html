Unfair laws and apologies

Times when Canadian governments treated communities unfairly, and the apologies that followed.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 6 history.

Learn what you need to know

Canada has not always treated everyone fairly. Some past laws targeted people because of their race or culture. Learning about them helps make sure they are not repeated.

💵Chinese head tax (1885–1923): a fee only Chinese immigrants had to pay, raised to $500 in 1903; then the 1923 Exclusion Act banned almost all Chinese immigration. Apology: 2006
🚢Komagata Maru (1914): a ship of 376 passengers, mostly Sikhs from India, was turned away at Vancouver. Apology in the House of Commons: 2016
🏕️Japanese Canadian internment (1942): about 22 000 people were removed from the B.C. coast and their property sold. Redress: 1988
🏫Residential schools (1880s–1996): about 150 000 First Nations, Inuit and Métis children were taken from their families. Apology: 2008

An apology admits a wrong was done. Redress tries to repair it, for example with payments, education funds or memorials.

In 2015 the Truth and Reconciliation Commission released 94 Calls to Action to help repair the harm of residential schools. September 30 is the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation.

Why it matters: survivors and their families still feel these effects. Listening to their stories is part of reconciliation.

Key words

  • head tax: a fee charged only to Chinese immigrants entering Canada
  • internment: forcing people to live in camps, usually during a war
  • redress: actions to make up for a past wrong
  • reconciliation: rebuilding respectful relationships after harm

Family stories
