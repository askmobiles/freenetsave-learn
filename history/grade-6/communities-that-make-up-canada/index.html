Learn what you need to know

Canada is made up of many communities: groups of people who share a place, a language, a culture, a religion or a history.

🪶First Nations, Inuit and Métis: the first peoples of this land, with over 50 languages
⚜️French Canadians: mostly in Quebec, also Acadians in New Brunswick and Franco-Ontarians
🇬🇧British communities: English, Scottish, Irish and Welsh settlers
🌏Newcomers: people from Asia, Africa, the Caribbean, Europe and Latin America

The Métis are a distinct Indigenous people whose culture grew from First Nations and European (often French or Scottish) fur-trade families, especially in the Red River area.

In 1971 Canada became the first country to adopt an official multiculturalism policy. It says all cultures are welcome and can keep their traditions while sharing Canadian values.

Today: about one in four people in Canada was born in another country.

Key words

  • community: a group of people who share something, such as a place or culture
  • Métis: a distinct Indigenous people with mixed First Nations and European roots
  • multiculturalism: a policy that values and supports many cultures in one country
  • heritage: traditions and history passed down from earlier generations

Festivals

Diwali, Lunar New Year, Eid, Caribana and Saint-Jean-Baptiste Day are celebrated across Canada.

Two official languages

Labels and federal services are in English and French.
