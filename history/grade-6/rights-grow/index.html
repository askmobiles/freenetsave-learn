Rights grow

Learn what you need to know

At Confederation in 1867, only men who owned property could vote. Over time, more and more people won rights through hard work and protest.

🗳️1916–1918: most women win the vote (Manitoba first in 1916; federally 1918)
👩‍⚖️1929: Persons Case — women are "persons" and can become senators
🎭1946: Viola Desmond refuses to leave a whites-only section of a Nova Scotia theatre
🗳️1947–1948: Chinese and South Asian Canadians win the right to vote; Japanese Canadians in 1949
🪶1960: First Nations people can vote federally without giving up status
📜1982: Charter of Rights and Freedoms

The Famous Five — Emily Murphy, Nellie McClung, Irene Parlby, Louise McKinney and Henrietta Muir Edwards — won the Persons Case.

Viola Desmond now appears on Canada's $10 bill. She was pardoned in 2010.

Pattern: rights usually grew because ordinary people spoke up, organized and challenged unfair rules.

Key words

  • suffrage: the right to vote
  • Persons Case: the 1929 decision that women are legal "persons"
  • segregation: keeping people apart by race
  • activist: a person who works for social or political change
