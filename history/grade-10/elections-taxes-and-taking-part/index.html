Elections, taxes and taking part

Learn what you need to know

🗳️First-past-the-post: the candidate with the most votes in a riding wins, even without a majority
📅Federal elections must happen at least every five years; fixed-date laws usually set them every four
🏛️Majority government: one party wins more than half the seats; minority: the governing party has fewer than half
🧾Taxes: income tax, GST/HST and property tax pay for health care, schools, roads and more
🙋Taking part: voting, volunteering, contacting MPs, petitions, joining groups, peaceful protest

Canadian citizens aged 18 or older can vote in federal elections. Elections Canada runs federal elections and is independent of the government.

Being an active citizen means more than voting — it includes staying informed and helping your community.

Key words

  • first-past-the-post: an electoral system where the candidate with the most votes wins
  • majority government: a government whose party holds more than half the seats
  • petition: a written request signed by many people asking for action
  • GST/HST: sales taxes on most goods and services
