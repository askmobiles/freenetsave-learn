The Great Depression

The economic crash of the 1930s: causes, hardship across Canada, and how governments and people responded.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 history.

Learn what you need to know

The Great Depression began with the stock market crash of October 1929 and lasted through the 1930s. At its worst, about one in four Canadian workers was unemployed.

📉Causes: risky buying of stocks on credit, overproduction, falling world trade and high tariffs
🌾The prairies were hit hardest: drought, dust storms and grasshoppers ruined crops (the "Dirty Thirties")
🍲"Relief" (welfare) was small and hard to get; people lined up at soup kitchens
🚂Single unemployed men were sent to relief camps paying 20 cents a day
🚩The On-to-Ottawa Trek (1935) ended in the Regina Riot

Responses: Prime Minister R.B. Bennett first raised tariffs, then proposed a "New Deal." New parties formed, like the CCF (which later became the NDP) and Social Credit. The Bank of Canada was created in 1934.

Legacy: the Depression convinced many Canadians that governments should help people in hard times — leading later to unemployment insurance (1940) and other social programs.

Key words

  • depression: a long, severe drop in economic activity
  • relief: government help for people in need during the Depression
  • tariff: a tax on imported goods
  • stock market: a place where shares in companies are bought and sold
