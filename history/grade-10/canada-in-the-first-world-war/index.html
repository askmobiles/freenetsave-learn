Canada in the First World War

How Canada went to war in 1914, what Canadians did at the front and at home, and how the war changed the country.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 history.

Learn what you need to know

When Britain declared war on Germany on August 4, 1914, Canada was automatically at war because it was part of the British Empire. More than 600,000 Canadians enlisted, and about 61,000 were killed.

🪖Second Battle of Ypres (1915): Canadians held the line during one of the first poison gas attacks
🌲Somme (1916): huge losses; the Newfoundland Regiment was nearly wiped out at Beaumont-Hamel on July 1
⛰️Vimy Ridge (April 1917): all four Canadian divisions fought together and captured the ridge
🌧️Passchendaele (1917): fighting in deep mud for a small gain
💯Hundred Days (1918): the Canadian Corps led a series of victories at the end of the war

On the home front: factories made shells and supplies, women took on new jobs, and the government introduced the first federal income tax (1917) as a "temporary" war measure.

Hard and unfair parts: the War Measures Act (1914) led to the internment of thousands of Ukrainian Canadians and others labelled "enemy aliens." The Conscription Crisis of 1917 divided English and French Canada. The Halifax Explosion (December 6, 1917) killed about 2,000 people.

Why it matters: Canada's achievements, like Vimy Ridge, increased its pride and led to its own signature on the Treaty of Versailles (1919) — a step toward independence.

Key words

  • conscription: forcing people by law to join the military
  • internment: holding people in camps during wartime without a criminal charge
  • home front: the war effort by people who stayed in their own country
  • War Measures Act: a 1914 law giving the federal government emergency powers in wartime
