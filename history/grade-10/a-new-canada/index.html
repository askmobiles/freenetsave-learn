A new Canada

Canada from 1945 to 1982: the baby boom, the Cold War, new symbols, social programs and the Quiet Revolution.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 history.

Learn what you need to know

👶Baby boom (1946–1965): a huge rise in births changed schools, suburbs and the economy
🧊Cold War: Canada joined NATO (1949) and NORAD (1958) and fought in the Korean War (1950–1953)
🕊️Peacekeeping: Lester B. Pearson won the Nobel Peace Prize (1957) for his work during the Suez Crisis
🍁New symbols: Canadian citizenship (1947), the Maple Leaf flag (1965), Expo 67
🏥Social programs: universal medicare, the Canada Pension Plan (1965) and more

Newfoundland joined Canada in 1949. The Canadian Bill of Rights (1960) was passed, and First Nations people living on reserves gained the federal vote in 1960.

Quebec: the Quiet Revolution (1960s) modernized Quebec and strengthened Québécois identity. The October Crisis (1970) led to the War Measures Act being used in peacetime. The 1980 referendum on sovereignty-association was defeated.

Immigration: the 1967 points system removed race-based rules, and multiculturalism became official policy in 1971.

Big idea: after 1945, Canada built a new identity: more independent, more diverse and with a stronger social safety net.

Key words

  • baby boom: a sharp rise in birth rates after the Second World War
  • Cold War: the long rivalry between the US and USSR after 1945
  • Quiet Revolution: rapid change in Quebec in the 1960s
  • multiculturalism: a policy that supports many cultures living together
