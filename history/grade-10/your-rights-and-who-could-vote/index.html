Your rights and who could vote

Your rights under the Charter, and the long struggle over who could vote in Canada.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 history.

Learn what you need to know

🗣️Fundamental freedoms (s.2): religion, expression, peaceful assembly, association
🗳️Democratic rights (s.3): every citizen can vote and run for office
🧳Mobility rights (s.6): enter, leave and move within Canada
⚖️Legal rights (s.7–14): life, liberty and security; fair trials
🤝Equality rights (s.15): equal protection without discrimination
🗨️Language rights: English and French as official languages

Reasonable limits (s.1): rights can be limited if the limit is reasonable in a free and democratic society — for example, hate speech laws.

1918Most women won the federal vote
1947–1948Chinese, South Asian and later Japanese Canadians (1948) regained or won the vote
1960First Nations people living on reserves gained the federal vote without giving up status
1982The Charter guaranteed the vote to every citizen 18 and over

Remember: the right to vote was won step by step — many groups had to fight for it.

Key words

  • Charter of Rights and Freedoms: the part of the Constitution that protects Canadians' rights
  • reasonable limits: limits on rights that are justified in a free and democratic society
  • enfranchise: to give the right to vote
  • discrimination: unfair treatment based on things like race, religion or sex
