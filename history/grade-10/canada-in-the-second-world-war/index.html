Canada in the Second World War

Canada's role from 1939 to 1945: battles, the home front, and injustices such as the internment of Japanese Canadians.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 history.

Learn what you need to know

Canada declared war on Germany on September 10, 1939 — a week after Britain, showing Canada now decided for itself. More than 1 million Canadians served, and about 45,000 were killed.

🌊Battle of the Atlantic (1939–1945): the Royal Canadian Navy protected supply convoys from German submarines
🏝️Hong Kong (1941): Canadian soldiers were killed or captured by Japan
⚓Dieppe (1942): a disastrous raid on France; most Canadians were killed, wounded or captured
🇮🇹Italy (1943–1945): the long, hard Italian Campaign, including Ortona
🏖️D-Day (June 6, 1944): Canadians landed at Juno Beach
🌷Netherlands (1944–1945): Canadians helped liberate the Dutch, who still send tulips to Ottawa

Home front: factories built ships, planes and vehicles; women joined the armed forces and industry; food and gas were rationed. The British Commonwealth Air Training Plan trained over 130,000 air crew in Canada.

Injustices: after Pearl Harbor, more than 21,000 Japanese Canadians were forced from the BC coast, and their property was sold. Canada also refused Jewish refugees (the MS St. Louis, 1939).

Results: Canada ended the war with a strong economy, a large military and a new confidence on the world stage.

Key words

  • convoy: a group of ships travelling together for protection
  • rationing: limiting how much of something each person can buy
  • internment: forcing people into camps without a trial
  • liberation: freeing a place from enemy control
