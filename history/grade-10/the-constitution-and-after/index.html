The Constitution and after

Learn what you need to know

On April 17, 1982, Queen Elizabeth II signed the Constitution Act, 1982. Canada could now change its own constitution without Britain — this is called patriation. It included the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms.

⚖️Charter (1982): freedoms of expression and religion, equality rights, language rights and more
🪶Section 35: recognizes and affirms Aboriginal and treaty rights
⚜️Quebec did not sign the 1982 agreement; the Meech Lake (1987) and Charlottetown (1992) accords failed
🗳️1995 Quebec referendum: "No" won by about 50.6% to 49.4%
🌍Since then: Nunavut created (1999), same-sex marriage legalized (2005), residential schools apology (2008), Truth and Reconciliation report (2015)

Other events: the Oka Crisis (1990) brought land rights to national attention; the North American Free Trade Agreement (1994) changed trade; Canada stayed out of the 2003 Iraq War.

Why it matters: the Charter changed how Canadians understand their rights; courts can strike down laws that break it.

Key words

  • patriation: bringing the constitution under Canada's full control
  • Charter: the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, part of the Constitution
  • referendum: a direct vote by citizens on a question
  • reconciliation: rebuilding respectful relationships, especially with Indigenous peoples
