Rights and change after the war

The 1920s in Canada: votes for women, the Winnipeg General Strike, the Persons Case, new technology — and who was left out.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 history.

Learn what you need to know

🗳️Women's vote: many women gained the federal vote in 1917–1918; Agnes Macphail became the first woman MP in 1921
✊Winnipeg General Strike (1919): about 30,000 workers walked off the job for better pay and the right to bargain
👩‍⚖️Persons Case (1929): the "Famous Five" won the ruling that women are "persons" under the law
🚗Roaring Twenties: cars, radios, movies and jazz; a booming economy for some
🌾The prairies and Maritimes did not share equally in the boom

Who was left out? Many Indigenous peoples, Asian Canadians and others still could not vote. The Chinese Immigration Act (1923) almost completely stopped Chinese immigration. The Indian Act was changed to require residential school attendance and to ban some ceremonies.

Steps to independence: the Halibut Treaty (1923) was the first treaty Canada signed on its own, and the Balfour Report (1926) said the dominions were equal to Britain.

Remember: change in the 1920s was real but uneven — progress for some, exclusion for others.

Key words

  • suffrage: the right to vote
  • general strike: a strike by workers in many industries at once
  • Persons Case: the 1929 ruling that women are legally "persons"
  • autonomy: the power to govern yourself
