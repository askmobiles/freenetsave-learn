Learn what you need to know

Canada is a constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary democracy. The King is head of state; the prime minister is head of government.

👑The Crown: the King, represented by the Governor General (federal) and lieutenant governors (provinces)
🏛️Parliament: the King, the elected House of Commons and the appointed Senate
🗳️Prime minister: usually the leader of the party with the most seats in the House of Commons
⚖️Courts: independent judges interpret laws; the Supreme Court of Canada is the highest court
🇨🇦Federal: national defence, citizenship, money, Indigenous affairs, criminal law
🗺️Provincial/territorial: education, health care delivery, highways, natural resources
🏙️Municipal: garbage, local roads, parks, libraries, water, public transit
🪶Indigenous governments: First Nations, Inuit and Métis self-government in many communities

How a bill becomes law: introduced → three readings in the House of Commons → three readings in the Senate → royal assent.

Key words

  • constitutional monarchy: a system where a monarch is head of state but powers are limited by a constitution
  • federalism: dividing power between national and provincial governments
  • royal assent: the final approval that makes a bill a law
  • riding: an electoral district that elects one Member of Parliament
