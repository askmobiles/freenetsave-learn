Learn what you need to know

The first Europeans known to reach North America were the Norse (Vikings), around the year 1000. Their camp at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

⛵1497: John Cabot (sailing for England) reaches Newfoundland; reports huge fish stocks
🧭1534: Jacques Cartier (France) lands at Gaspé and raises a cross; meets Donnacona's people
🏘️1535: Cartier sails up the St. Lawrence to Stadacona and Hochelaga
🗺️1608: Samuel de Champlain founds Quebec

Why did they come? Europeans wanted a faster route to Asia for spices and silk, valuable resources like fish and furs, land, and to spread Christianity.

First Nations guided explorers, shared food and knowledge, and traded. They showed Cartier's sick crew how to cure scurvy with a drink made from a tree (likely white cedar).

Contact also brought harm: Europeans carried diseases such as smallpox that killed many Indigenous people, and they claimed land that was already home to others.

Point of view: Europeans called it "discovery," but the land was already known and lived on by many nations.

Key words

  • explorer: a person who travels to unfamiliar places to learn about them
  • Northwest Passage: a sea route through North America to Asia that Europeans searched for
  • scurvy: a disease caused by lack of vitamin C
  • contact: the first meetings between Indigenous peoples and Europeans
