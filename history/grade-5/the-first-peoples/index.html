Learn what you need to know

Long before Europeans arrived, the land now called Canada was home to many First Nations and the Inuit. Each nation had its own language, government, beliefs and way of life, shaped by the land where they lived.

🌲Mi'kmaq (Atlantic coast): fishing, hunting, birchbark canoes, wigwams
🌽Haudenosaunee (south of Lake Ontario): farmed corn, beans and squash; longhouses
🛶Wendat (near Georgian Bay): farmers and traders; longhouses in villages
🦌Anishinaabe (Great Lakes): hunting, fishing, wild rice; canoes and wigwams
❄️Inuit (Arctic): hunted seals, whales and caribou; kayaks, igloos in winter

Corn, beans and squash are called the Three Sisters. They were planted together and helped each other grow.

The Haudenosaunee Confederacy united five (later six) nations under the Great Law of Peace. Leaders were chosen by clan mothers, so women had real power.

Nations traded with each other over long distances, using rivers and lakes as highways. Wampum belts made of shell beads recorded agreements.

Remember: there was no single "Indigenous way of life." Each nation was different, and many of these nations are still here today.

Key words

  • First Nations: the many Indigenous nations south of the Arctic, each with its own culture
  • Inuit: Indigenous people of the Arctic
  • Three Sisters: corn, beans and squash grown together
  • wampum: shell-bead belts used to record agreements and history

See real-life examples

Place names

Canada comes from "kanata," a Haudenosaunee (Iroquoian) word for village. Ontario, Ottawa and Toronto also come from Indigenous words.

Lacrosse

Canada's national summer sport was first played by Indigenous nations, including the Haudenosaunee.

Canoes today

People still paddle canoes and kayaks, inventions from Indigenous peoples.
