Learn what you need to know

Canada is a constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary democracy. The King (represented by the Governor General) is head of state; the Prime Minister leads the government.

🇨🇦Federal (Ottawa): Prime Minister and MPs — army, money, passports, citizenship
🏛️Provincial (Toronto, Queen's Park): Premier and MPPs — schools, hospitals, highways
🏙️Municipal: Mayor and councillors — garbage, local roads, parks, transit, water

Parliament has three parts: the King (Governor General), the Senate (appointed) and the House of Commons (elected). A bill must pass both houses and get royal assent to become law.

In a federal election, each riding elects one Member of Parliament (MP). The party with the most seats usually forms the government, and its leader becomes Prime Minister.

First Nations have their own governments too, with chiefs and councils, and treaty rights.

Who do I call? Pothole on your street → city. School funding → province. Passport → federal.

Key words

  • federal government: the national government in Ottawa
  • MP: Member of Parliament, elected to the House of Commons
  • premier: the leader of a provincial government
  • riding: an area that elects one representative
