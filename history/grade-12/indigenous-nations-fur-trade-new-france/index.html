Indigenous nations, the fur trade and New France

Long before Confederation, Indigenous nations and French and British newcomers built relationships of trade, alliance and conflict.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

Indigenous peoples have lived in what is now Canada for at least 12,000 years, in hundreds of nations with their own languages, governments and economies — from the Haudenosaunee and Wendat farming villages to the Anishinaabe, Cree, Inuit and the Pacific Coast nations.

🦫The fur trade depended on Indigenous knowledge, trappers, guides and trading partners
⚜️New France: Samuel de Champlain founded Québec in 1608; the seigneurial system shaped farms along the St. Lawrence
🏢The Hudson's Bay Company (1670) was granted Rupert's Land by the English king — without asking the Indigenous peoples who lived there
🤝The Great Peace of Montréal (1701) brought peace between New France and about 40 Indigenous nations

Britain conquered New France in the Seven Years' War (Battle of the Plains of Abraham, 1759; Treaty of Paris, 1763). The Royal Proclamation of 1763 recognized that Indigenous peoples held rights to their lands and that only the Crown could acquire land through treaties — a basis for treaty rights today.

Métis: the Métis Nation grew from the families of fur traders and First Nations women, developing its own culture and language (Michif), especially in the Red River area.

Key words

  • seigneurial system: the land system of New France with long, narrow farms along rivers
  • Royal Proclamation of 1763: the British decree recognizing Indigenous land rights and requiring treaties
  • Métis: a distinct Indigenous people with mixed First Nations and European ancestry and their own culture
  • Rupert's Land: the huge territory granted to the Hudson's Bay Company in 1670
