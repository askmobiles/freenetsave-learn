The Scientific Revolution and the Enlightenment

New ways of testing ideas changed how people understood nature, then thinkers used reason to question kings and the Church.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

The Scientific Revolution replaced reliance on ancient authorities with observation, experiments and math — the scientific method.

☀️Copernicus (1543): the Sun, not Earth, is at the centre (heliocentric model)
🔭Galileo used a telescope to support Copernicus and was put on trial by the Church in 1633
🍎Isaac Newton (1687): laws of motion and universal gravitation

The Enlightenment (1700s) applied reason to society and government.

📜John Locke: natural rights to life, liberty and property; government needs the consent of the governed
⚖️Montesquieu: separation of powers (legislative, executive, judicial)
🗣️Voltaire: freedom of speech and religion
🤝Rousseau: the social contract and the general will
👩Mary Wollstonecraft (1792): education and rights for women

Why it matters: Enlightenment ideas shaped the American and French Revolutions and modern constitutions, including Canada's Charter of Rights and Freedoms.

Key words

  • heliocentric: with the Sun at the centre
  • scientific method: testing ideas through observation and experiment
  • natural rights: rights people have simply by being human
  • separation of powers: dividing government into branches that check each other
