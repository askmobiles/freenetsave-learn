Imperialism and the world wars

European empires took control of most of Africa and much of Asia, and rivalries between powers led to two world wars.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

Imperialism is when a powerful country takes control of other lands. In the Scramble for Africa, European powers met at the Berlin Conference (1884–1885) and divided Africa among themselves without any Africans present. By 1914, only Ethiopia and Liberia stayed independent. Britain ruled India directly from 1858.

💰Motives: raw materials, markets, national pride, strategic bases, and racist ideas of "civilizing" others
⛓️Effects: borders drawn without local input, forced labour (e.g., Congo Free State), loss of land and culture

First World War (1914–1918): long-term causes are remembered as MAIN — Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism. The spark was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in 1914. About 17 million people died.

Second World War (1939–1945): after the harsh Treaty of Versailles and the Great Depression, dictators rose: Hitler in Germany, Mussolini in Italy and militarists in Japan. Germany invaded Poland in 1939. Around 70–85 million people died, including six million Jews murdered in the Holocaust.

Connection: imperial rivalry helped cause the First World War, and the unfair peace that ended it helped lead to the Second.

Key words

  • imperialism: when a powerful country takes control of other lands and peoples
  • nationalism: strong loyalty to one's nation, sometimes above others
  • genocide: the deliberate killing of a people because of who they are
  • appeasement: giving in to an aggressor's demands to avoid war
