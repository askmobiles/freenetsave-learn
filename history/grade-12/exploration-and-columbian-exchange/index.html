Exploration and the Columbian Exchange

European voyages connected the world, moving plants, animals, people and diseases — with terrible costs for Indigenous peoples and enslaved Africans.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

Europeans wanted spices and gold from Asia without going through Ottoman and Italian middlemen. New ships (the caravel), the compass and better maps helped. Portugal reached India by sea (Vasco da Gama, 1498). Christopher Columbus reached the Caribbean in 1492. Magellan's expedition sailed around the world (1519–1522).

🌽To Europe, Africa and Asia: maize, potatoes, tomatoes, cacao, tobacco
🐴To the Americas: horses, cattle, pigs, wheat, sugar cane
🦠Diseases such as smallpox killed a very large share of Indigenous peoples, who had no immunity

Conquest: Hernán Cortés defeated the Aztecs (1521) and Francisco Pizarro the Inca (1530s). Spain used the encomienda system of forced Indigenous labour.

The transatlantic slave trade: to work sugar and other plantations, European traders forcibly took about 12 million Africans across the Atlantic between the 1500s and 1800s. Millions died during the Middle Passage.

Two sides: the Columbian Exchange connected the world and changed diets everywhere, but it also brought disease, conquest and slavery.

Key words

  • Columbian Exchange: the transfer of plants, animals, people and diseases between the Americas and the rest of the world after 1492
  • Middle Passage: the voyage of enslaved Africans across the Atlantic
  • immunity: the body's ability to resist a disease
  • encomienda: a Spanish system that forced Indigenous people to work for colonists
