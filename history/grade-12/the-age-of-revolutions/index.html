The age of revolutions

Between 1775 and 1825, revolutions in America, France, Haiti and Latin America challenged kings and empires in the name of rights and freedom.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

🇺🇸American Revolution (1775–1783): thirteen colonies rejected British taxes without representation; Declaration of Independence (1776)
🇫🇷French Revolution (1789–1799): storming of the Bastille (July 14, 1789), Declaration of the Rights of Man, end of the monarchy, the Reign of Terror
🇭🇹Haitian Revolution (1791–1804): enslaved people led by Toussaint Louverture defeated France and created the first Black republic
🌎Latin American independence (1810s–1820s): leaders such as Simón Bolívar ended most Spanish rule

Causes they shared: Enlightenment ideas, unfair taxes, debts from wars, unequal social classes and, in Haiti, the brutality of slavery.

France: society was divided into three estates; the Third Estate (most people) paid most taxes. After the revolution turned violent, Napoleon Bonaparte took power (1799) and later crowned himself emperor, but his Napoleonic Code spread legal equality across Europe.

Canada link: about 40,000 to 50,000 Loyalists, who stayed loyal to Britain, moved north after the American Revolution, helping create Upper Canada (Ontario) and New Brunswick.

Key words

  • revolution: a fast, major change in government or society
  • estates: the three legal classes of pre-revolutionary France
  • Loyalists: colonists who stayed loyal to Britain and many of whom moved to Canada
  • republic: a country with no monarch, led by elected officials

See real-life examples

Ontario

Many towns along the St. Lawrence and Lake Ontario were founded by Loyalists.

Holidays

July 14 (Bastille Day) is France's national holiday.

Rights

Ideas from these revolutions appear in many constitutions, including Canada's Charter.
