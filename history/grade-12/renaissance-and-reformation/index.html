Renaissance and Reformation

A rebirth of art and learning in Europe, the printing press, and a split in Western Christianity that changed politics for centuries.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

The Renaissance ("rebirth") began in Italian city-states such as Florence in the 1300s–1400s. Rich families like the Medici paid artists and scholars. Humanism focused on human potential and on studying Greek and Roman texts.

🎨Artists: Leonardo da Vinci (Mona Lisa), Michelangelo (Sistine Chapel ceiling), Raphael
🖨️Johannes Gutenberg's printing press (about 1450) made books cheaper and spread ideas fast
📐Artists used perspective and studied anatomy to make art realistic

The Reformation: in 1517 Martin Luther criticized the Catholic Church, especially the sale of indulgences, in his 95 Theses. Printing spread his ideas. Protestant churches formed (Lutheran, Calvinist, and the Church of England under Henry VIII in 1534). The Catholic Church answered with the Counter-Reformation (Council of Trent, Jesuits).

Religious wars followed, ending with the Thirty Years' War (1618–1648). The Peace of Westphalia (1648) helped create the idea of independent, sovereign states.

Chain of causes: printing press → ideas spread quickly → Reformation spreads → religious wars → modern idea of sovereign states.

Key words

  • humanism: a Renaissance movement focused on human potential and classical learning
  • indulgence: a payment to the Church believed to reduce punishment for sins
  • Protestant: a Christian who broke away from the Catholic Church in the Reformation
  • sovereignty: a state's full control over its own territory
