The Industrial Revolution

Machines, factories and steam power transformed how people worked and lived, starting in Britain around 1760.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

The Industrial Revolution began in Britain around 1760. Britain had coal and iron, capital to invest, colonies for raw materials and markets, and new farming methods that freed workers to move to cities.

🧵Textiles: the spinning jenny and water frame moved cloth-making into factories
🚂James Watt improved the steam engine (1760s–1770s); railways spread from the 1820s
🏭Factories replaced home workshops; cities like Manchester grew very fast

Life for workers: long hours (12–16 per day), low wages, dangerous machines, child labour and crowded, polluted housing. In response came trade unions, factory laws, and new ideas: capitalism (Adam Smith, 1776) and socialism, including Karl Marx's Communist Manifesto (1848).

A Second Industrial Revolution (about 1870–1914) brought steel, electricity, the telephone, oil and chemicals.

Big picture: industrialization raised living standards over time, but at first it caused great hardship, and it began large-scale burning of fossil fuels linked to today's climate change.

Key words

  • industrialization: the change to making goods with machines in factories
  • urbanization: the movement of people from the countryside to cities
  • trade union: a group of workers who join together to bargain for better pay and conditions
  • capitalism: an economic system where private owners run businesses for profit

Climate
