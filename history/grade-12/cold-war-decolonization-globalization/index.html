Cold War, decolonization and globalization

After 1945 two superpowers competed for influence, empires ended, and the world became more connected than ever.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 history.

Learn what you need to know

The Cold War (1947–1991): the United States (capitalist democracy) and the Soviet Union (communist one-party state) competed without fighting each other directly. Europe was divided by the "Iron Curtain"; the Berlin Wall stood from 1961 to 1989.

☢️Nuclear arms race; Cuban Missile Crisis (1962) brought the world close to nuclear war
🛡️Alliances: NATO (1949, Canada a founding member) and the Warsaw Pact (1955)
🔥Proxy wars: Korea (1950–1953), Vietnam (1955–1975), Afghanistan (1979–1989)
🚀Space race: Sputnik (1957), Moon landing (1969)

Decolonization: India and Pakistan gained independence in 1947; Ghana led sub-Saharan Africa in 1957; about 17 African countries became independent in 1960 alone. Leaders included Gandhi (non-violent resistance) and Kwame Nkrumah. Some independence came through long wars (Algeria, Vietnam).

Globalization since the 1990s: more trade, travel, migration and the internet connect the world, but also spread economic shocks, pandemics and inequality.

Canada's role: Lester B. Pearson won the 1957 Nobel Peace Prize for proposing UN peacekeeping during the Suez Crisis.

Key words

  • Cold War: the 1947–1991 rivalry between the U.S. and the Soviet Union without direct war between them
  • proxy war: a war where superpowers support opposing sides instead of fighting each other
  • decolonization: colonies gaining independence from empires
  • globalization: the growing connection of the world's economies and cultures
