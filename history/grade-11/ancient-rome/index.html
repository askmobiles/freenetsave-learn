Ancient Rome

Rome grew from a small city into a republic and then an empire that shaped law, language and government in Europe and beyond.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 history.

Learn what you need to know

According to tradition Rome was founded in 753 BCE. In 509 BCE Romans overthrew their king and created a republic: two elected consuls led the state, and the Senate advised them. Ordinary citizens (plebeians) struggled with the rich families (patricians) for more rights; the Twelve Tables (about 450 BCE) wrote laws down for all to see.

⚔️Rome defeated Carthage in the Punic Wars (264–146 BCE) and took control of the Mediterranean
🗡️Julius Caesar became dictator and was killed in 44 BCE
👑Augustus became the first emperor in 27 BCE; the Pax Romana (Roman peace) lasted about 200 years
✝️Christianity spread; Emperor Constantine supported it in the 300s CE

Legacy: Roman law (innocent until proven guilty), Latin (the root of French, Spanish, Italian), roads, aqueducts, concrete, arches and domes. The western empire fell in 476 CE; the eastern (Byzantine) empire lasted until 1453.

Republic vs empire: in the republic, power was shared among elected officials; in the empire, one emperor held most of the power.

Key words

  • republic: a state where leaders are elected instead of inheriting power
  • Senate: the council of leading Romans that advised the consuls
  • Pax Romana: about 200 years of relative peace in the Roman Empire
  • aqueduct: a channel or bridge that carried water to cities
