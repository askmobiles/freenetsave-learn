Ancient Greece

Learn what you need to know

Ancient Greece was made of independent city-states (poleis). Mountains and the sea separated them. The two most famous were Athens and Sparta.

🗳️Athens: direct democracy from about 508 BCE — male citizens voted on laws in the Assembly
🛡️Sparta: a military state where boys trained as soldiers from age 7
🚫Women, enslaved people and foreigners could not vote in Athens
⚔️Persian Wars (490–479 BCE): Greeks defeated Persia at Marathon and Salamis; Peloponnesian War (431–404 BCE): Sparta defeated Athens

Ideas: philosophers Socrates, Plato and Aristotle asked how we should live and how we know things. Greeks also developed theatre (tragedy and comedy), history writing (Herodotus), geometry (Euclid) and the Olympic Games (from 776 BCE).

Alexander the Great (356–323 BCE) of Macedonia conquered an empire from Greece to Egypt and India, spreading Greek culture. This mix of Greek and local cultures is called the Hellenistic age.

Legacy with limits: Athens invented democracy, but only a small share of the people living there could take part.

Key words

  • polis: a Greek city-state
  • democracy: rule by the people
  • philosophy: the study of big questions about life, knowledge and right and wrong
  • Hellenistic: the mix of Greek and other cultures after Alexander's conquests
