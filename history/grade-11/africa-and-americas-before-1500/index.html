Africa and the Americas before 1500

Rich kingdoms and empires grew in Africa and the Americas long before European ships arrived.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 history.

Learn what you need to know

West Africa: the empires of Ghana, Mali and Songhai grew rich from the trans-Saharan trade in gold and salt. Mali's ruler Mansa Musa made a famous pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324, giving away so much gold that its value fell in Cairo. Timbuktu had mosques, libraries and scholars.

🏰Great Zimbabwe (about 1100–1450): a stone city in southern Africa that traded gold and ivory to the Indian Ocean coast
⛪Aksum and later Ethiopia: an early Christian kingdom in East Africa
⛵Swahili city-states such as Kilwa traded with Arabia, India and China

The Americas: the Maya (strongest about 250–900 CE) built cities with pyramids and developed writing, astronomy, a calendar and the zero. The Aztecs built Tenochtitlan on a lake in 1325 (today Mexico City). The Inca ruled a huge empire in the Andes with about 40,000 km of roads and kept records on knotted strings called quipu.

North of Mexico: Cahokia (near today's St. Louis) was a large Mississippian city with earth mounds. In what is now Canada and the U.S., the Haudenosaunee Confederacy united nations under the Great Law of Peace.

Myth to avoid: Africa and the Americas were not "empty" or "without history" before 1500. They had cities, trade, governments and science.

Key words

  • trans-Saharan trade: trade across the Sahara Desert by camel caravans
  • pilgrimage: a religious journey to a holy place
  • quipu: Inca knotted strings used to keep records
  • confederacy: a union of nations that agree to work together
