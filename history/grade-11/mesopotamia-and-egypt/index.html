Mesopotamia and ancient Egypt

Two of the world's first civilizations grew up beside great rivers. Compare their writing, rulers, laws and beliefs.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 history.

Learn what you need to know

Mesopotamia ("land between the rivers") grew between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers. The Sumerians built city-states such as Ur and Uruk, each with a temple tower called a ziggurat. They invented cuneiform, one of the first writing systems, around 3200 BCE.

📜Hammurabi of Babylon (about 1750 BCE) wrote one of the earliest law codes, with punishments based on "an eye for an eye" and on social class
🛞Mesopotamian inventions: the wheel for carts, the plough, a number system based on 60 (60 minutes, 360 degrees)
⚔️Empires such as Akkad, Babylon and Assyria fought to control the region

Ancient Egypt grew along the Nile. Yearly floods left rich black soil. Upper and Lower Egypt were united around 3100 BCE. The pharaoh was seen as a god-king. Egyptians wrote with hieroglyphs on papyrus, built the pyramids at Giza (around 2500 BCE) and mummified the dead because they believed in an afterlife.

Big difference: Mesopotamia was open to invasion and often divided into rival city-states; Egypt was protected by deserts and stayed united and stable for very long periods.

Key words

  • cuneiform: Mesopotamian wedge-shaped writing on clay tablets
  • hieroglyphs: Egyptian writing using picture signs
  • pharaoh: the god-king who ruled ancient Egypt
  • ziggurat: a stepped temple tower in Mesopotamian cities
