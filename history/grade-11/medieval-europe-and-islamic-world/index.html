Medieval Europe and the Islamic world

After Rome fell, Europe was organized by feudalism and the Church, while the Islamic world became a centre of science and trade.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 history.

Learn what you need to know

Medieval Europe (about 500–1500): with no strong central government, Europe used feudalism. Kings gave land (fiefs) to lords; lords gave land to knights in return for military service; peasants and serfs worked the land and could not leave it. The Roman Catholic Church was the most powerful institution, and monasteries kept learning alive.

⚔️The Crusades (1096–1291): Christian armies tried to take Jerusalem and the Holy Land
📜Magna Carta (1215): English barons forced King John to accept that the king is under the law
☠️The Black Death (1347–1351) killed about one third of Europe's people and weakened feudalism

The Islamic world: Islam began with the Prophet Muhammad in Arabia in the 600s CE. Within about a century, Muslim states stretched from Spain to India. Under the Abbasids, Baghdad became a centre of learning: the House of Wisdom translated Greek, Persian and Indian works. Scholars such as al-Khwarizmi (algebra) and Ibn Sina (medicine) made major advances.

Connection: Greek and Indian knowledge preserved and expanded in the Islamic world later reached Europe and helped spark the Renaissance.

Key words

  • feudalism: a system where land was exchanged for loyalty and military service
  • serf: a peasant tied to a lord's land
  • Magna Carta: the 1215 charter limiting the English king's power
  • caliphate: a state ruled by a caliph, a successor to Muhammad
