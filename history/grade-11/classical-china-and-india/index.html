Classical China and India

Great empires in China and India built strong governments, new beliefs and inventions that spread across Asia.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 history.

Learn what you need to know

China: the Zhou rulers claimed the Mandate of Heaven — the idea that heaven gives a just ruler the right to rule and takes it away from a bad one. Confucius (551–479 BCE) taught respect for family, education and good government. The Qin dynasty united China in 221 BCE, standardized writing and money, and began the Great Wall. The Han dynasty (206 BCE–220 CE) used exams based on Confucian ideas to choose officials and opened the Silk Road.

📄Chinese inventions: paper (Han), later printing, the compass and gunpowder
🐫The Silk Road linked China with India, Persia and Rome, carrying silk, spices, ideas and religions

India: the Indus Valley cities, such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (about 2500 BCE), had planned streets and drains. Later, Hinduism developed, and Buddhism began with Siddhartha Gautama (about 500 BCE). The Maurya emperor Ashoka (about 268–232 BCE) turned to Buddhism after a bloody war. The Gupta empire (about 320–550 CE) is known as a golden age: mathematicians used the decimal system and the zero.

Our numbers: the digits 0–9 we use were developed in India, then spread through the Islamic world to Europe.

Key words

  • dynasty: a series of rulers from the same family
  • Mandate of Heaven: the Chinese belief that heaven gives a just ruler the right to rule
  • Confucianism: teachings of Confucius about family, respect and good government
  • Silk Road: trade routes linking China to the Mediterranean
