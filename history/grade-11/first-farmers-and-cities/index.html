The first farmers and the first cities

How people moved from hunting and gathering to farming, and how farming led to villages, cities and the first civilizations.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 history.

Learn what you need to know

For most of human history, people lived as hunter-gatherers, moving to follow animals and seasonal plants. Starting about 10,000 BCE, people in the Fertile Crescent (today's Iraq, Syria and Türkiye) began to plant wheat and barley and to raise sheep and goats. This change is called the Neolithic Revolution.

🌾Farming began separately in several places: the Fertile Crescent, China (rice, millet), Mesoamerica (maize), the Andes (potatoes) and New Guinea
🏘️Farming produced a food surplus, so villages grew and some people could do other jobs
🏺New jobs: potters, weavers, metal workers, priests, soldiers and rulers
📈Populations grew, but so did inequality, disease and war over land

A civilization is usually described as having cities, a government, organized religion, social classes, specialized jobs, writing and public works such as walls and temples. Early cities include Çatalhöyük (a large farming town in Türkiye) and Uruk in Mesopotamia.

Why it matters: almost everything that came later — cities, writing, laws, trade and empires — depended on farmers growing more food than they needed.

Key words

  • Neolithic Revolution: the change from hunting and gathering to farming and settled villages
  • surplus: extra food or goods beyond what is needed
  • civilization: a complex society with cities, government, writing and specialized jobs
  • domestication: taming plants and animals so people can raise them
