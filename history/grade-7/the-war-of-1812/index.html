Learn what you need to know

The War of 1812 was fought between the United States and Britain (with its colonies and First Nations allies) from 1812 to 1814. Much of the fighting happened in Upper Canada.

Causes: the British navy stopped American ships and took sailors; Americans believed Britain was helping First Nations resist settlement; and some Americans wanted to take over British North America.

🎖️Isaac Brock: British general who captured Detroit; killed at Queenston Heights (October 1812)
🪶Tecumseh: Shawnee leader of a First Nations alliance; killed at the Battle of the Thames (1813)
🚶‍♀️Laura Secord: walked about 30 km in 1813 to warn of an American attack (Beaver Dams)
🔥1813: Americans burned York (Toronto); 1814: the British burned Washington, including the White House

Black soldiers such as Richard Pierpoint's "Coloured Corps" also fought to defend Upper Canada.

The Treaty of Ghent (signed December 24, 1814) ended the war with no land changing hands. But it gave Upper Canada a sense of shared identity, while First Nations allies lost their hope of an independent homeland.

Who won? Each side claims something. Canada stayed British, the US kept its land, and First Nations lost the most.

Key words

  • invasion: an army entering another country's territory to attack it
  • ally: a partner who fights on the same side
  • Treaty of Ghent: the 1814 treaty that ended the War of 1812
  • militia: ordinary citizens trained to fight when needed
