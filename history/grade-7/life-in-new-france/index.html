Life in New France

Learn what you need to know

The Treaty of Utrecht (1713) gave Britain Acadia (mainland Nova Scotia), Newfoundland and the Hudson Bay lands. France kept Île Royale (Cape Breton) and built the huge fortress of Louisbourg there.

🌾Habitants farmed long river lots and paid dues to the seigneur
⛪The Catholic Church ran parishes, schools and hospitals
🏰Louisbourg: a fortified fishing and trading town
🛶The fur trade pushed west to the Prairies (La Vérendrye, 1730s–1740s)

Life was hard work: clearing land, long cold winters, and planting and harvesting by hand. Families were large, and children helped on the farm.

Slavery existed in New France. Both Indigenous people (called panis) and people of African descent were enslaved. In 1734 Marie-Joseph Angélique, an enslaved Black woman, was accused of setting a fire that burned part of Montreal and was executed.

France relied on alliances with First Nations such as the Wendat, Algonquin and Mi'kmaq for trade and defence. The Great Peace of Montreal (1701) brought peace between France and about 40 First Nations.

Perspective: history looks different from the view of a seigneur, a habitant, an enslaved person, or a First Nations ally.

Key words

  • Treaty of Utrecht: the 1713 treaty that gave Britain Acadia, Newfoundland and Hudson Bay
  • Louisbourg: a French fortress town on Cape Breton Island
  • alliance: an agreement to work together
  • Great Peace of Montreal: the 1701 peace treaty between France and about 40 First Nations
