Britain takes New France

Learn what you need to know

Britain and France fought for control of North America. In 1755, British officials began forcing Acadians from their homes — about 10 000 by 1764 — because they would not swear an unconditional oath to the British king. This is the Acadian Expulsion (Le Grand Dérangement).

⚔️1756–1763: Seven Years' War between Britain and France
🏰1758: Britain captures Louisbourg
🗻1759: Battle of the Plains of Abraham — Wolfe and Montcalm both die; Quebec falls
📜1763: Treaty of Paris — France gives New France to Britain

The Royal Proclamation of 1763 set rules for the new British colony. It said Indigenous lands west of the colonies could only be bought by the Crown through treaties. It is still part of Canada's Constitution.

The British first tried to make Quebec English and Protestant. The Quebec Act of 1774 changed course: it allowed the Catholic religion, French civil law and the seigneurial system, and enlarged Quebec.

Why the Quebec Act mattered: it kept French Canadians loyal when the American colonies rebelled in 1775.

Key words

  • Acadian Expulsion: the forced removal of Acadians by the British starting in 1755
  • Plains of Abraham: the 1759 battle where Britain captured Quebec
  • Royal Proclamation: the 1763 British document organizing new lands and recognizing Indigenous land rights
  • Quebec Act: the 1774 law protecting French language, Catholic religion and civil law in Quebec
