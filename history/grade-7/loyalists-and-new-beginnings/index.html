Loyalists and new beginnings

Learn what you need to know

During the American Revolution (1775–1783), people who stayed loyal to Britain were called Loyalists. After the war, about 40 000–50 000 moved north to British North America.

🇬🇧Most went to Nova Scotia; New Brunswick was created for them in 1784
✊🏿About 3000 Black Loyalists, promised freedom, settled in Nova Scotia (Birchtown)
🪶Haudenosaunee Loyalists led by Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea) settled on the Grand River
🏞️Others settled along the upper St. Lawrence and Lake Ontario

Black Loyalists faced racism and were often given poor land or none. In 1792, about 1200 left Nova Scotia for Sierra Leone in Africa.

The Haldimand Proclamation (1784) granted the Six Nations land six miles on each side of the Grand River. Much of it was later lost, and Six Nations land claims continue today.

With many English-speaking settlers arriving, the Constitutional Act of 1791 split Quebec into Upper Canada (mostly English, later Ontario) and Lower Canada (mostly French, later Quebec).

Loyalists changed the map: New Brunswick (1784) and Upper Canada (1791) were created because of them.

Key words

  • Loyalist: a colonist who stayed loyal to Britain during the American Revolution
  • Black Loyalists: people of African descent who supported Britain in exchange for a promise of freedom
  • Haldimand Proclamation: the 1784 land grant to the Six Nations along the Grand River
  • Constitutional Act: the 1791 law that created Upper and Lower Canada
