Settlers, canals and freedom

Learn what you need to know

After 1815, many settlers came from England, Scotland and Ireland, pushed by poverty, crowded cities and, later, the Irish Potato Famine (1845–1849). In 1847 thousands arrived sick with typhus; many died at the quarantine station on Grosse Île.

🛶Lachine Canal (1825): around the rapids at Montreal
🌊Welland Canal (1829): ships bypass Niagara Falls
🏗️Rideau Canal (1832): built by Colonel John By, linking Kingston and Bytown (Ottawa) — now a UNESCO site

Canals moved goods and people faster and safer, and helped defend the colony away from the American border.

Freedom: Upper Canada's 1793 Act to Limit Slavery (Simcoe) stopped new enslaved people being brought in. The British Empire abolished slavery in 1834. Thousands of freedom seekers escaped the US by the Underground Railroad, a secret network of routes and safe houses, to towns like St. Catharines, Chatham and Amherstburg.

Harriet Tubman guided people north and lived in St. Catharines in the 1850s. Josiah Henson founded the Dawn Settlement near Dresden, Ontario.

Remember: freedom in Canada was not equality — Black settlers still faced racism and segregated schools.

Key words

  • canal: a human-made waterway for boats
  • Underground Railroad: a secret network that helped enslaved people escape to freedom
  • abolition: officially ending something, such as slavery
  • famine: a severe shortage of food
