Rebellions and responsible government

Learn what you need to know

In Upper and Lower Canada, an elected assembly could pass laws, but the governor and appointed councils had the real power. Small groups of wealthy friends controlled them.

🏛️Family Compact: the ruling elite of Upper Canada
🏛️Château Clique: the ruling elite of Lower Canada (mostly English-speaking)
🔥1837: William Lyon Mackenzie leads a rebellion near Toronto (Montgomery's Tavern)
🔥1837–38: Louis-Joseph Papineau and the Patriotes rebel in Lower Canada

Both rebellions were quickly defeated. Some rebels were hanged or sent away. Britain sent Lord Durham to investigate.

The Durham Report (1839) recommended uniting the two Canadas and giving responsible government. He also wanted French Canadians to be assimilated into English culture, which angered them.

The Act of Union (1840, in force 1841) created the Province of Canada. Reformers Robert Baldwin and Louis-Hippolyte LaFontaine worked together across language lines. Responsible government came in 1848 — first in Nova Scotia (led by Joseph Howe), then in the Province of Canada.

Responsible government means the cabinet must have the support of the elected assembly — like today's government needing support in the House of Commons.

Key words

  • Family Compact: the small elite that controlled Upper Canada's government
  • rebellion: an armed uprising against a government
  • Durham Report: Lord Durham's 1839 report recommending union and responsible government
  • responsible government: when the government must keep the support of the elected assembly
