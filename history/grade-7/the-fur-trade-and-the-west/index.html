The fur trade and the West

Learn what you need to know

Two companies competed for furs in the West: the Hudson's Bay Company (HBC, from 1670, based on Hudson Bay) and the Montreal-based North West Company (NWC, from 1779), whose voyageurs paddled huge distances.

🏢HBC: owned Rupert's Land; traders waited at forts on the bay
🛶NWC: went inland to meet First Nations trappers; explorers like Alexander Mackenzie and David Thompson mapped the West
🥩Pemmican: dried bison meat mixed with fat, made by the Métis — fuel for the trade
🏘️Red River Colony (1812): Lord Selkirk's settlement near today's Winnipeg

The Métis grew as a nation at Red River, with their own language (Michif), culture and the buffalo hunt.

The Pemmican Proclamation (1814) tried to stop pemmican leaving Red River, angering the Métis and NWC. In 1816, at the Battle of Seven Oaks, Métis led by Cuthbert Grant clashed with colonists; about 21 colonists and one Métis died.

In 1821 the two companies merged under the HBC name. Many voyageurs lost jobs, and Red River grew as a Métis and settler community.

Indigenous partners: the fur trade depended on First Nations and Métis trappers, guides, food and knowledge.

Key words

  • Hudson's Bay Company: a British fur-trade company founded in 1670
  • North West Company: a Montreal fur-trade company that competed with the HBC
  • pemmican: dried meat mixed with fat, a long-lasting travel food
  • Rupert's Land: the huge area draining into Hudson Bay, granted to the HBC
