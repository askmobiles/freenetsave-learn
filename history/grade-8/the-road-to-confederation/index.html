Learn what you need to know

In the 1860s the British North American colonies faced big problems. Uniting seemed like a solution.

🔒Political deadlock: the Province of Canada's English and French halves could not agree, and governments kept falling
🇺🇸Fear of the US: after the American Civil War, a large US army and Fenian raids (1866) threatened the colonies
🚂Railways: a railway to the Atlantic was needed but too costly for one colony
💰Trade: the US ended the Reciprocity Treaty in 1866, so colonies needed to trade with each other
🇬🇧Britain wanted the colonies to pay for their own defence

Charlottetown Conference (September 1864): the Maritimes met to discuss union; delegates from Canada joined and proposed a larger union. Quebec Conference (October 1864): the 72 Resolutions. London Conference (1866): final details.

The British North America Act took effect on July 1, 1867, creating the Dominion of Canada with four provinces: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. John A. Macdonald became the first prime minister.

Not everyone agreed: many in Nova Scotia opposed it (Joseph Howe), and PEI and Newfoundland stayed out at first. First Nations were not consulted.

The Fathers of Confederation included John A. Macdonald, George-Étienne Cartier, George Brown, Charles Tupper and Samuel Leonard Tilley.

Key words

  • Confederation: the union of the colonies to form Canada in 1867
  • BNA Act: the British North America Act, Canada's founding law (now the Constitution Act, 1867)
  • deadlock: a situation where no progress can be made because sides cannot agree
  • Fenians: Irish-American raiders who attacked British North America in the 1860s
