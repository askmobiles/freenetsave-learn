Learn what you need to know

Canada wanted farmers, but immigration rules favoured people from Britain, the US and Northern Europe. Others faced barriers:

💵Chinese head tax raised to $500 in 1903
🚢1908 continuous journey rule blocked most South Asian immigrants; Komagata Maru turned away 1914
🇯🇵1908 agreement limited Japanese immigrants
✊🏿Around 1910–1911, Black settlers from Oklahoma were discouraged, and an order-in-council to ban them was approved but never used

Industrialization: factories grew in cities like Toronto, Montreal and Hamilton. People moved from farms to cities (urbanization). Many workers, including children, worked long hours for low pay in unsafe places. Workers formed unions to demand better conditions.

Women worked in factories, as teachers, nurses and domestic workers. Reformers like Nellie McClung and groups like the Woman's Christian Temperance Union fought for women's right to vote and other changes. Emily Stowe was the first woman to practise medicine in Canada.

Reform movements wanted safer factories, public health, an end to child labour, women's suffrage and limits on alcohol (temperance).

Key words

  • industrialization: the growth of factories and machine production
  • urbanization: people moving from the countryside to cities
  • union: a group of workers who join together to improve pay and conditions
  • continuous journey rule: a 1908 rule requiring immigrants to come by a non-stop trip from their home country, which blocked most South Asians
