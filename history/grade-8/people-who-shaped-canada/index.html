Learn what you need to know

Many people shaped Canada between 1850 and 1914. Their legacies are often complex.

🎩John A. Macdonald: first PM, led Confederation and the CPR; also responsible for policies that harmed Indigenous peoples, including residential schools
⚜️George-Étienne Cartier: brought Quebec into Confederation
🦬Louis Riel: Métis leader, founder of Manitoba, hanged 1885
🐎Gabriel Dumont: Métis military leader in 1885
🪶Crowfoot (Isapo-Muxika): Siksika chief who signed Treaty 7 and kept his people out of the 1885 conflict
🪶Big Bear (Mistahimaskwa): Cree chief who held out for a better Treaty 6 deal
🇨🇦Wilfrid Laurier: first francophone PM; "the 20th century belongs to Canada"
🩺Emily Stowe: first woman to practise medicine in Canada, women's rights leader

Historians use evidence and multiple perspectives. For example, Macdonald's statue in some places has been removed or debated because of his role in harming Indigenous peoples, while others value his role in building Canada.

Judging the past: understand people in their time, but also be honest about the harm their choices caused.

Key words

  • legacy: what a person leaves behind that affects later generations
  • perspective: a point of view shaped by someone's experience
  • evidence: information used to support a conclusion
  • Treaty 7: the 1877 treaty signed by Blackfoot Confederacy nations and others
