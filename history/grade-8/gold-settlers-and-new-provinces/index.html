Gold, settlers and new provinces

Learn what you need to know

In 1896, gold was found in the Klondike in the Yukon (by Skookum Jim Mason, Dawson Charlie and George Carmack, with Kate Carmack). About 100 000 people set out in 1897–1898; only about 30 000–40 000 reached Dawson City.

The NWMP under Sam Steele required each gold seeker to bring about a year's supplies (around a tonne) over the Chilkoot or White Pass. The Yukon Territory was created in 1898.

Under Prime Minister Wilfrid Laurier (1896–1911), minister Clifford Sifton advertised the Prairies as "the Last, Best West." The Dominion Lands Act offered 160 acres (65 hectares) of land for $10 if settlers built a home and farmed it.

🌾Settlers came from Britain, the US and Eastern Europe (Ukrainians, Germans, Poles, Doukhobors)
🏠First homes were often sod houses on the treeless prairie
🌾Marquis wheat (1909) ripened faster, so wheat could grow farther north
🗺️1905: Alberta and Saskatchewan become provinces

Population boom: Canada grew from about 5.4 million people in 1901 to about 7.2 million in 1911.

Key words

  • gold rush: a rush of people to a place where gold has been found
  • Dominion Lands Act: an 1872 law offering cheap homestead land to settlers
  • homestead: land given to a settler who agrees to live on and farm it
  • Last, Best West: the slogan used to attract settlers to the Prairies
