The railway and 1885

Learn what you need to know

The Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR) was built to keep Macdonald's promise to British Columbia and to bring settlers west. Its last spike was driven at Craigellachie, B.C., on November 7, 1885.

The hardest section, through the B.C. mountains, was built largely by about 15 000 Chinese workers. They were paid less than white workers and given the most dangerous jobs; hundreds died. When the railway was finished, Canada brought in the head tax on Chinese immigrants.

Meanwhile, on the Prairies, the bison had almost disappeared, First Nations faced hunger, and treaty promises were not kept. The Métis at Batoche worried about their land.

⚔️1885 North-West Resistance: Métis led by Louis Riel and Gabriel Dumont
🪶Cree leaders Big Bear (Mistahimaskwa) and Poundmaker (Pîhtokahanapiwiyin) were also involved
🚂The new railway rushed troops west in days
⚖️Riel was found guilty of treason and hanged on November 16, 1885

After 1885, the government used the pass system and other controls on First Nations. Poundmaker was exonerated by the federal government in 2019.

Riel is still debated: to many he is a hero who defended Métis and French rights; at the time, many in Ontario saw him as a traitor.

Key words

  • CPR: Canadian Pacific Railway, completed in 1885
  • last spike: the final spike completing the CPR, at Craigellachie, B.C.
  • North-West Resistance: the 1885 armed conflict between the Métis, some First Nations and Canada
  • treason: the crime of betraying one's country
