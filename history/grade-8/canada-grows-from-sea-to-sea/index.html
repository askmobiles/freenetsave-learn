📘 Ontario Grade 8 · History: Creating Canada, 1850–1890

Learn what you need to know

Macdonald wanted Canada to stretch "from sea to sea." The new country grew quickly.

🗺️1869–1870: Canada acquires Rupert's Land and the North-Western Territory from the HBC (the North-West Territories)
🦬1870: Manitoba joins after the Red River Resistance
🚂1871: British Columbia joins after Canada promises a railway within 10 years
🦞1873: PEI joins to get help with its railway debts and land problems
❄️1880: Britain transfers the Arctic islands to Canada

The HBC sale was made without asking the people who lived there — First Nations and the Métis. At Red River, the Métis led by Louis Riel formed a provisional government (1869–1870) and negotiated the Manitoba Act, which protected French and English language rights and promised land to the Métis.

The North-West Mounted Police (NWMP) were created in 1873 to keep order in the West. They later became the RCMP.

Later: Yukon (1898), Alberta and Saskatchewan (1905), Newfoundland and Labrador (1949) and Nunavut (1999) came later.

Key words

  • Rupert's Land: the HBC's huge territory, sold to Canada in 1869–1870
  • Manitoba Act: the 1870 law creating Manitoba, negotiated by the Red River provisional government
  • provisional government: a temporary government set up until a permanent one is formed
  • NWMP: North-West Mounted Police, created in 1873; later the RCMP
