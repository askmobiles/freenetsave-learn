Canadian places with surprising stories

Learn what you need to know

🛶L'Anse aux Meadows, N.L.: Norse camp from about 1021; UNESCO site since 1978
🏰Old Quebec: founded 1608, the only walled city north of Mexico; UNESCO 1985
🦬Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump, Alberta: used by the Blackfoot and other Plains peoples for nearly 6,000 years
🪵SG̱ang Gwaay, Haida Gwaii: Haida village with poles left to return to the earth; UNESCO 1981
🌾Grand-Pré, Nova Scotia: heart of Acadia; deportation of about 10,000 Acadians began in 1755
⛏️The Klondike, Yukon: gold found in 1896 by Keish (Skookum Jim), Kaa Goox, George Carmack and Shaaw Tláa; Yukon created in 1898
🚢Pier 21, Halifax: almost one million immigrants arrived 1928–1971
📡Signal Hill, St. John's: Marconi's transatlantic wireless signal, December 12, 1901
☢️The Diefenbunker near Ottawa: a secret four-storey underground Cold War shelter finished in 1961
⚔️Batoche, Saskatchewan: the Métis and their Cree and Dakota allies fought government troops in May 1885

Places as evidence: historic sites let us see and touch the past, and the way they are presented changes as we learn more.

Key words

  • national historic site: a place officially recognized for its importance to Canada's history
  • deportation: forcing people to leave a country or region
  • gold rush: a sudden rush of people to an area where gold was found
  • Acadians: descendants of French settlers in the Maritimes

Research for this lesson comes from 10 Canadian Places with Surprising Stories on Canada Quiz, our sister site with free Canadian history quizzes and citizenship test practice.
