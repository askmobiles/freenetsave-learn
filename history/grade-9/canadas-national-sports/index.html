Hockey, lacrosse and Canada's national sports

Canada has two national sports. One came from Indigenous peoples, and the other became part of almost every Canadian winter.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

🥍Lacrosse is Canada's national summer sport; it comes from Indigenous peoples, especially the Haudenosaunee, who played it long before Europeans arrived
🏒Ice hockey is Canada's national winter sport

The Stanley Cup: Lord Stanley, the Governor General, donated a silver bowl in 1892; the first winners in 1893 were the Montreal Hockey Club. Today the NHL plays for it, and every winning player's name is engraved on it.

🇨🇦Seven NHL teams are Canadian: Toronto, Montreal, Ottawa, Winnipeg, Calgary, Edmonton and Vancouver
⏱️A game has three 20-minute periods; each team has six players on the ice, including the goalie
🎩A hat trick is three goals by one player in a game
📺Hockey Night in Canada has been a Saturday tradition for generations, first on radio and then TV

For everyone: backyard rinks, outdoor community rinks, ball hockey and strong women's teams make hockey part of community life — not only a pro sport.

Key words

  • national sport: a sport officially named as a symbol of a country
  • period: one of the three 20-minute parts of a hockey game
  • hat trick: three goals by one player in one game
  • Stanley Cup: the NHL championship trophy, first won in 1893
