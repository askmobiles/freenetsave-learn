Canada's trailblazers: famous firsts

Someone always has to go first. Meet Canadians who broke barriers in politics, science, space and sport.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

🗳️1916: Manitoba was the first province where women could vote, after campaigns by Nellie McClung
⚖️1916: Emily Murphy became a magistrate in Edmonton; she and four other women won the Persons Case in 1929
🏛️1921: Agnes Macphail became the first woman elected to the House of Commons
✊1968: Lincoln Alexander became the first Black MP; 1993: Jean Augustine became the first Black woman MP and later led the motion for Black History Month
💉1923: Frederick Banting shared Canada's first Nobel Prize for the discovery of insulin
🛰️1962: Alouette 1 made Canada the third country to design and build its own satellite
🚀1984: Marc Garneau became the first Canadian in space; 1992: Roberta Bondar the first Canadian woman
🛸2013: Chris Hadfield became the first Canadian to command the International Space Station

More firsts: Jeanne Sauvé became the first woman Governor General (1984); Mary Simon, an Inuk leader, became the first Indigenous Governor General (2021). The first Stanley Cup was won in 1893, and in 1954 Marilyn Bell, 16, was the first person to swim across Lake Ontario.

Not everyone at once: most women won the federal vote in 1918, but many Asian Canadian women only in 1947–1948, Inuit in 1950, and First Nations people (without losing status) in 1960.

Key words

  • trailblazer: a person who is the first to do something and opens the way for others
  • Persons Case: the 1929 decision that women are "persons" who can be appointed to the Senate
  • magistrate: a judge who handles lower-court cases
  • suffrage: the right to vote
