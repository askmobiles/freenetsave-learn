How Canada grew: provinces and territories

Canada began with four provinces in 1867 and reached ten provinces and three territories in 1999. Learn when and how each joined.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

1867Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick (Confederation)
1870Manitoba and the Northwest Territories
1871British Columbia (promised a railway)
1873Prince Edward Island
1898Yukon (Klondike gold rush)
1905Alberta and Saskatchewan
1949Newfoundland and Labrador, after two referendums
1999Nunavut, created from the Northwest Territories after an Inuit land claim

Size vs people: Nunavut is the largest (about 2.1 million km²) but has the fewest people (about 43,000). Prince Edward Island is the smallest province. Ontario and Quebec hold about three of every five Canadians.

Province or territory? The difference is how they are governed, not their size: provinces get their powers from the Constitution; territories get theirs from the federal government.

Key words

  • province: a region with powers set out in the Constitution
  • territory: a region whose powers come from the federal government
  • land claim: an agreement about Indigenous rights to land and resources
  • referendum: a direct vote by citizens on one question
