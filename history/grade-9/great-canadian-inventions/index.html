Great Canadian inventions

Insulin, basketball, the Canadarm and the garbage bag — Canadians invented things the whole world uses.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

💉Insulin (1921–22): Banting, Best, Collip and Macleod at the University of Toronto
❤️The pacemaker (1950s): John Hopps at the National Research Council
♿The electric wheelchair: George Klein, to help injured veterans after the Second World War
✈️The G-suit: Wilbur Franks' suit that stops pilots from blacking out
📞The telephone: Alexander Graham Bell made the first long-distance call between Brantford and Paris, Ontario, in 1876
📻The walkie-talkie: Donald Hings, late 1930s
🎬IMAX: a Canadian team, first shown at Expo 70
📱The BlackBerry: built in Waterloo, Ontario
🏀Basketball (1891): James Naismith, born near Almonte, Ontario, used two peach baskets and 13 rules
🛷The snowmobile: Joseph-Armand Bombardier of Quebec
🗑️The green garbage bag (1950s): Harry Wasylyk and Larry Hansen
🔩The Robertson screw: P. L. Robertson's square-drive screw

The Canadarm: built by Spar Aerospace and partners, it first flew on the space shuttle Columbia on November 13, 1981, flew 90 missions over 30 years, and was followed by Canadarm2 (2001), which helped build the International Space Station, and the robot Dextre (2008).

Why it matters: many of these inventors were newcomers or children of newcomers — a country of about 41 million people can still change the world.

Key words

  • invention: something new that someone creates
  • pacemaker: a device that keeps the heart beating in a steady rhythm
  • Canadarm: Canada's robot arm used on the space shuttles
  • National Research Council: Canada's main government science and research agency
