Canada's flag and national symbols

The maple leaf flag is younger than many grandparents. Learn how Canada chose it, and what the beaver, the anthem and other symbols mean.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

For a long time Canada used flags with the British flag in the corner, such as the Red Ensign. In 1964 the government asked Canadians for ideas, and thousands of designs arrived — beavers, hockey sticks, stars and many maple leaves.

🎓History professor George Stanley suggested two red bands, a white square and one red maple leaf
✏️Artist Jacques St-Cyr drew the final leaf, which has 11 points
🗳️MPs debated from June to December 1964; the final vote came in the middle of the night
🍁The flag was first raised on Parliament Hill on February 15, 1965 — now National Flag of Canada Day

Other symbols: red and white became Canada's official colours in 1921. The beaver recalls the fur trade; Sandford Fleming put a beaver on Canada's first postage stamp in 1851. The national anthem is O Canada. The coat of arms carries the motto "From Sea to Sea" (A Mari usque ad Mare). The loonie and toonie are the one- and two-dollar coins.

Head of state: Canada is a constitutional monarchy. The King is head of state and is represented in Canada by the Governor General.

Key words

  • Red Ensign: a red flag with the British flag in the corner, used by Canada before 1965
  • coat of arms: an official emblem with symbols and a motto
  • motto: a short phrase that sums up an idea or belief
  • constitutional monarchy: a country with a monarch as head of state whose powers are set by a constitution
