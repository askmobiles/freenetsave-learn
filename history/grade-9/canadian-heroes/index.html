Canadian heroes who changed the world

A runner, a doctor, an engineer and a businesswoman — four Canadians whose actions still matter today.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

Terry Fox: after losing part of his leg to cancer, he began the Marathon of Hope on April 12, 1980, dipping his artificial leg in the Atlantic at St. John's. He ran about 42 km a day for 143 days and 5,373 km, until cancer returned near Thunder Bay. Terry Fox Runs have raised more than $800 million for cancer research.

Insulin: in 1921–1922 Frederick Banting, Charles Best, James Collip and John Macleod at the University of Toronto discovered how to purify insulin. They sold the patent to the university for $1 each so no one could profit unfairly from it.

⏰Sandford Fleming proposed world time zones after missing a train in 1876; the 1884 conference chose Greenwich as the starting line
✊Viola Desmond refused to leave the whites-only section of a Nova Scotia theatre in 1946; she appears on the $10 bill
🏒Wayne Gretzky, "The Great One", set many NHL records

What heroes share: courage, persistence, and acting for others, not only for themselves.

Key words

  • Marathon of Hope: Terry Fox's 1980 run across Canada for cancer research
  • insulin: a hormone that controls blood sugar, used to treat diabetes
  • patent: the legal right to control an invention
  • segregation: separating people by race
