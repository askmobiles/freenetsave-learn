Canada and the United States: trade through history

For 170 years Canada has wanted the American market but worried about American power. Each trade deal mixed money with sovereignty.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

1854Reciprocity Treaty: free trade in natural products like grain, timber and fish
1866The U.S. cancelled it — one reason the colonies joined in Confederation to build an east–west economy
1879Macdonald's National Policy: tariffs, a railway and western settlement
1911Laurier's free trade deal lost the election — the slogan was "no truck nor trade with the Yankees"
1965The Auto Pact: car tariffs removed if carmakers kept building in Canada
1989The Canada–U.S. Free Trade Agreement took effect after the 1988 "free trade election"
1994NAFTA added Mexico
2020CUSMA replaced NAFTA, with a review every six years

Since 2025: the United States put new tariffs on many Canadian goods, Canada answered with counter-tariffs, and many Canadians responded with "Buy Canadian" campaigns. Softwood lumber has been a separate dispute since the 1980s.

The pattern: Canada opens up to trade, something goes wrong, Canada protects itself, and a generation later it opens up again.

Key words

  • reciprocity: an agreement where both sides lower trade barriers
  • tariff: a tax on imported goods
  • sovereignty: a country's full control over its own decisions
  • protectionism: using tariffs to protect local industries from foreign competition
