Surprising true stories from Canada's past

A bear from Winnipeg, a Norse village in Newfoundland, a secret spy school in Ontario — real stories behind Canada's history.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 history.

Learn what you need to know

🛶About the year 1021: Norse sailors built a settlement at L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland — the only confirmed Norse site in North America (UNESCO, 1978)
🏘️1535: the name "Canada" likely comes from kanata, a Huron-Iroquois word for village
🐻1914: Lieutenant Harry Colebourn bought a bear cub at White River, Ontario, named her Winnie after Winnipeg; she inspired Winnie-the-Pooh
🕵️1941: Camp X, a secret spy school, opened between Whitby and Oshawa, Ontario
🦫2007: the world's longest beaver dam, about 850 metres, was found on satellite images in Wood Buffalo National Park
🛸1967: St. Paul, Alberta, built a UFO landing pad as a Centennial project, with a time capsule to open in 2067

How historians know: the Norse date of 1021 came from tree rings in wooden logs at the site, marked by a solar storm in the year 993. Good history depends on evidence — artifacts, documents, photos and science.

Remember: Indigenous peoples had lived in all these places for thousands of years before any of these stories.

Key words

  • archaeologist: a scientist who studies the past through objects people left behind
  • artifact: an object made or used by people in the past
  • UNESCO World Heritage Site: a place recognized worldwide for its cultural or natural importance
  • evidence: facts or objects that show something is true
