The prime ministers of Canada

Learn what you need to know

The prime minister is the head of government: usually the leader of the party that can win the support of most members of the House of Commons. Canada has had 24 prime ministers, but 30 ministries, because four people served more than once.

1️⃣Sir John A. Macdonald was first, from July 1, 1867
🇫🇷Sir Wilfrid Laurier was the first francophone PM (1896) and served 15 years without a break
⏳William Lyon Mackenzie King served the longest in total: about 21 years over three terms
⚡Sir Charles Tupper served the shortest: 68 days in 1896
👩Kim Campbell is the only woman PM so far (1993, 132 days)
🍁Mark Carney became the 24th PM on March 14, 2025

Interesting facts: Lester B. Pearson won the Nobel Peace Prize (1957). John Diefenbaker gave First Nations people the federal vote without losing their status in 1960. Pierre Elliott Trudeau brought home the Constitution with the Charter in 1982.

Remember: Canadians do not vote for the prime minister directly. They elect MPs, and the party leader who has the confidence of the House becomes prime minister.

Key words

  • prime minister: the head of the federal government
  • ministry: a period of government led by one prime minister
  • confidence of the House: support from a majority of MPs needed to govern
  • francophone: a person whose first language is French
