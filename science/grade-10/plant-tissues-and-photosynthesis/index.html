Plant tissues and photosynthesis

Learn what you need to know

Plants have specialized cells and tissues too:

🌱Meristematic tissue: undifferentiated cells at root and shoot tips that divide for growth
🧱Dermal tissue: the outer layer; makes a waxy cuticle and guard cells
🌿Ground tissue: photosynthesis (palisade and spongy mesophyll), storage and support
🚰Vascular tissue: xylem carries water and minerals up from roots; phloem carries sugars to all parts

Plant organs are roots (anchor, absorb water), stems (support, transport) and leaves (photosynthesis). In a leaf, palisade cells are packed with chloroplasts near the top. Stomata on the underside, opened and closed by guard cells, let CO₂ in and O₂ and water vapour out.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Water lost through stomata (transpiration) helps pull water up the xylem. Factors affecting photosynthesis rate: light intensity, CO₂ concentration and temperature.

Remember: xylem = water up; phloem = food everywhere; palisade cells do most photosynthesis.

Key words

  • xylem: vascular tissue that carries water and minerals upward
  • phloem: vascular tissue that carries sugars throughout the plant
  • guard cells: cells that open and close stomata
  • transpiration: loss of water vapour through leaves

See real-life examples

Maple syrup

In spring, sugar stored in maple roots moves up in sap. Farmers tap trees to collect it and boil it into syrup.

Celery and food colouring

Put celery in coloured water and the xylem tubes turn coloured, showing the path of water.

Greenhouses add CO₂

Some Ontario greenhouses add extra CO₂ to the air so vegetables photosynthesize and grow faster.
