Learn what you need to know

Most reactions in Grade 10 fit five patterns. Spotting the pattern helps you predict the products.

🔗Synthesis: A + B → AB. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl
💥Decomposition: AB → A + B. 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂
🔄Single displacement: A + BC → AC + B. Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
🔁Double displacement: AB + CD → AD + CB. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃
🔥Combustion: fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

In a single displacement, a more reactive element pushes a less reactive one out of its compound. Zinc is more reactive than copper, so it takes copper's place.

A double displacement often makes a precipitate (a solid that forms in a solution), a gas, or water. A neutralization (acid + base → salt + water) is a double displacement.

Count the reactants: one reactant → decomposition. Two elements → synthesis. Element + compound → single displacement. Two compounds → double displacement. Fuel + oxygen → combustion.

Key words

  • synthesis: two or more substances combine into one
  • decomposition: one compound breaks into simpler substances
  • precipitate: a solid formed when two solutions react
  • combustion: a fast reaction of a fuel with oxygen, giving heat and light

See real-life examples

Rusting

Iron + oxygen → iron oxide is a synthesis reaction that slowly ruins bikes and cars.

Airbags

Sodium azide decomposes in a split second to make nitrogen gas that fills the airbag.

Barbecue

Propane burning in a barbecue is a combustion reaction making CO₂ and water.
