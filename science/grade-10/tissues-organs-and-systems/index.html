Tissues, organs and systems

Cell specialization, the four animal tissue types, and organ systems. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Biology: Tissues, Organs, and Systems

Learn what you need to know

All cells in your body have the same DNA, but they become different by switching different genes on. This is cell specialization (cell differentiation).

Specialized cells form tissues. Animals have four main tissue types:

🧱Epithelial: covers and lines surfaces (skin, lining of the gut)
🦴Connective: supports and connects (bone, cartilage, blood, fat)
💪Muscle: contracts to move (skeletal, smooth, cardiac)
⚡Nervous: sends electrical signals (neurons)

Different tissues work together in an organ. The stomach has epithelial tissue lining it, muscle tissue to churn food, nervous tissue to control it, and connective tissue holding it together.

Organs work together in organ systems, like the digestive, circulatory, respiratory, nervous and excretory systems. All the systems make up the organism.

Remember: cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism.

Key words

  • cell specialization: cells becoming different to do specific jobs
  • epithelial tissue: tissue that covers and lines body surfaces
  • connective tissue: tissue that supports and connects body parts
  • organ: a structure made of several tissues working together

See real-life examples

Skin

Skin is an organ: epithelial tissue on the outside, connective tissue below, nerves to feel and muscles that raise hairs.

Heart muscle

Cardiac muscle never gets tired like your arm muscles. It contracts about 100 000 times a day.

Blood is a tissue

Blood is a connective tissue. Its cells float in a liquid called plasma.
