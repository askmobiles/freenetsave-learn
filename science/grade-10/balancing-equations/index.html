Balancing equations

Using coefficients to balance chemical equations and show conservation of mass. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Chemistry: Chemical Reactions

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

A chemical equation uses formulas to show a reaction. Because atoms are never created or destroyed (law of conservation of mass), the number of each type of atom must be the same on both sides.

We balance equations by changing coefficients (numbers in front of formulas). Never change subscripts, because that changes the substance: H₂O is water, H₂O₂ is hydrogen peroxide.

1️⃣Write the correct formulas
2️⃣Count atoms of each element on each side
3️⃣Add coefficients, one element at a time; leave H and O for last
4️⃣Treat polyatomic ions as a unit if they appear on both sides
5️⃣Check every atom and use the lowest whole numbers

Example: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O. Oxygen: 2 on the left, 1 on the right, so put 2 in front of H₂O: H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Now hydrogen: 2 on the left, 4 on the right, so put 2 in front of H₂: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

Example: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. Check: C 1 = 1; H 4 = 4; O 4 = 2 + 2.

Remember: change coefficients, never subscripts.

Key words

  • coefficient: the number in front of a formula in an equation
  • subscript: the small number in a formula showing atoms in a molecule
  • balanced equation: an equation with equal numbers of each atom on both sides
  • law of conservation of mass: mass is not created or destroyed in a reaction

See real-life examples

Rocket fuel

The Space Shuttle's main engines burned hydrogen and oxygen: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, releasing huge energy and only water vapour.

Car exhaust

Gasoline (like octane) burns as 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O. Balancing shows how much CO₂ a car makes.

Airbags

Airbags inflate in milliseconds when sodium azide decomposes: 2NaN₃ → 2Na + 3N₂.
