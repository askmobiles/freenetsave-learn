Curved mirrors

Concave and convex mirrors, ray diagrams and the images they make.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 science.

Learn what you need to know

A concave mirror curves inward like a cave. A convex mirror bulges outward. Light still follows the law of reflection at every point.

🎯Principal axis: the line through the centre of the mirror
🔴Focus (F): where parallel rays meet after reflecting
🟣Centre of curvature (C): twice as far as F
📏Focal length: distance from mirror to F

Ray diagram rules (concave): a ray parallel to the axis reflects through F; a ray through F reflects parallel; a ray to the vertex reflects at an equal angle. Where reflected rays meet is the image.

Object beyond C → image is smaller, upside down (inverted) and real. Object between F and the mirror → image is bigger, upright and virtual (like a makeup mirror). A convex mirror always gives a smaller, upright, virtual image with a wide view.

SALT: describe an image by Size, Attitude (upright/inverted), Location and Type (real/virtual).

Key words

  • concave mirror: a mirror that curves inward; it can focus light
  • convex mirror: a mirror that bulges outward; it spreads light and shows a wide view
  • focal point: the point where parallel rays meet after reflecting
  • virtual image: an image formed where light only seems to come from; it cannot be shown on a screen

See real-life examples

Car side mirror

"Objects in mirror are closer than they appear": convex mirrors give a wide view but make things look smaller.

Makeup mirror

A concave mirror held close makes your face look larger and upright.

Satellite dish

A curved dish reflects signals to a receiver at its focus.
