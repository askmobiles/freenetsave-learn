The eye and colour

How the eye forms images, vision problems, and how we see colour. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Physics: Light and Geometric Optics

Learn what you need to know

The eye works like a camera. Light enters through the cornea (which does most of the bending), passes through the pupil (size controlled by the iris), and the lens focuses it onto the retina. The image on the retina is real and inverted; the brain flips it.

The retina has rods (dim light, black and white) and cones (colour, bright light). The optic nerve carries signals to the brain. Where it leaves the eye there are no receptors: the blind spot.

👓Near-sightedness (myopia): far objects blurry; image forms in front of the retina; corrected with a diverging lens
🔍Far-sightedness (hyperopia): near objects blurry; image forms behind the retina; corrected with a converging lens
👵Presbyopia: the lens stiffens with age; reading glasses help

Colour: white light is a mix of all colours (ROYGBIV), as a prism shows. Objects reflect some colours and absorb others: a red apple reflects red. The additive primary colours of light are red, green and blue (RGB), used in screens. Red + green light = yellow; all three = white.

Remember: cornea and lens focus light on the retina; cones see colour; screens mix RGB.

Key words

  • retina: the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye
  • cones: retina cells that detect colour
  • myopia: near-sightedness
  • additive colour: mixing coloured light, as in screens

See real-life examples

Laser eye surgery

Laser eye surgery reshapes the cornea so it bends light correctly, reducing the need for glasses.

Phone screens

Zoom very close into a phone screen and you will see tiny red, green and blue dots that mix to make every colour.

Colour blindness

About 1 in 12 males has some colour vision deficiency, usually difficulty telling red from green.
