Climate change

The greenhouse effect, evidence of climate change, impacts and solutions. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Earth and Space Science: Climate Change

Learn what you need to know

Weather is short-term; climate is the average pattern over 30 years or more.

The greenhouse effect is natural: gases like carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide and water vapour absorb heat (infrared radiation) given off by Earth and keep the planet about 33 °C warmer than it would be. Without it, Earth would be frozen.

Since the Industrial Revolution, burning fossil fuels and cutting forests have raised CO₂ from about 280 ppm to over 420 ppm. This enhanced greenhouse effect traps more heat, causing global warming of about 1.2 °C so far.

🌡️Rising average temperatures
🧊Melting glaciers, sea ice and permafrost
🌊Rising sea levels
🔥More heat waves and wildfires
🌧️Heavier storms and changing rain patterns
🐻‍❄️Stress on species like polar bears

Canada's Arctic is warming about three to four times faster than the global average. Solutions include mitigation (cutting emissions: renewable energy, efficiency, electric vehicles, protecting forests) and adaptation (preparing: flood defences, heat plans, fire-smart communities).

Remember: more greenhouse gases → more trapped heat → climate change.

Key words

  • greenhouse effect: gases in the atmosphere trapping heat
  • ppm: parts per million, a unit of concentration
  • mitigation: reducing the causes of climate change
  • adaptation: preparing for the effects of climate change

See real-life examples

Wildfire smoke

Recent summers brought record wildfires in Canada, sending smoke across Ontario and the US.

Permafrost

In the North, thawing permafrost damages roads and buildings and releases methane.

Ice cores

Air bubbles trapped in Antarctic ice show CO₂ levels for the past 800 000 years. Today's levels are the highest in that record.
