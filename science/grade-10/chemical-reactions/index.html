Chemical reactions

Types of chemical reactions and how to recognize them. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Chemistry: Chemical Reactions

Learn what you need to know

In a chemical reaction, reactants turn into new products. Atoms are rearranged, never created or destroyed.

Common reaction types:

🔗Synthesis: A + B → AB (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO)
💥Decomposition: AB → A + B (2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂)
🔄Single displacement: A + BC → AC + B (Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu)
🔀Double displacement: AB + CD → AD + CB, often making a precipitate
🔥Combustion: fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O (for hydrocarbons)
⚖️Neutralization: acid + base → salt + water

In a single displacement, a more reactive metal replaces a less reactive one. The activity series ranks metals: K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, (H), Cu, Ag, Au. A metal can only replace one below it.

Evidence of a reaction: colour change, gas bubbles, a precipitate, energy change (heat or light), or a new odour.

Remember: synthesis builds, decomposition breaks, displacement swaps, combustion burns.

Key words

  • synthesis reaction: two or more substances combining into one
  • decomposition reaction: one substance breaking into simpler ones
  • displacement reaction: one element or ion replacing another
  • activity series: a list of metals from most to least reactive

See real-life examples

Rusting

Iron reacting with oxygen and water to form rust is a slow synthesis-type reaction.

Copper in silver nitrate

A copper wire in silver nitrate solution grows silver crystals. Copper is more reactive, so it displaces silver.

Furnaces

Natural gas furnaces burn methane: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. This combustion heats many Canadian homes.
