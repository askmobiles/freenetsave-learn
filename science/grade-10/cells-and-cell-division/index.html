Cells and cell division

The cell cycle, mitosis, and what happens when cell division goes wrong. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Biology: Tissues, Organs, and Systems

Learn what you need to know

Cells divide so organisms can grow, repair damage and replace worn-out cells. Before dividing, a cell copies its DNA.

The cell cycle has two main parts:

📈Interphase: the cell grows, does its normal job and copies its DNA (the longest stage)
➗Mitosis: the nucleus divides
✂️Cytokinesis: the cytoplasm splits into two daughter cells

Mitosis has four phases, remembered as PMAT:

1️⃣Prophase: chromosomes condense and become visible; nuclear membrane breaks down
2️⃣Metaphase: chromosomes line up in the middle
3️⃣Anaphase: sister chromatids are pulled to opposite ends
4️⃣Telophase: two new nuclei form

The result is two identical daughter cells with the same number of chromosomes as the parent (46 in humans).

Checkpoints stop the cycle if DNA is damaged. When control fails, cells can divide out of control, forming a tumour. Malignant tumours (cancer) can spread. Risk factors include smoking, UV radiation and some chemicals (carcinogens).

Remember: interphase → PMAT → cytokinesis gives two identical cells.

Key words

  • interphase: the stage when a cell grows and copies its DNA
  • mitosis: division of the nucleus into two identical nuclei
  • cytokinesis: division of the cytoplasm into two cells
  • carcinogen: something that can cause cancer

See real-life examples

Onion root tips

Onion root tips grow quickly, so under a microscope you can see cells in every stage of mitosis.

Sunscreen

UV rays can damage skin cell DNA. Sunscreen and shade lower the risk of skin cancer.

Healing bones

A broken bone heals because bone cells divide by mitosis to fill the gap.
