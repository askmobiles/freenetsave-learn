Stem cells and medical imaging

Stem cells and their uses, plus X-ray, CT, MRI and ultrasound imaging. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Biology: Tissues, Organs, and Systems

Learn what you need to know

Stem cells are unspecialized cells that can divide and become specialized cells.

👶Embryonic stem cells: can become almost any cell type
🦴Adult (tissue) stem cells: found in bone marrow, skin and other tissues; become a limited set of cells
🔬Induced pluripotent stem cells: adult cells reprogrammed to act like embryonic stem cells

Uses include bone marrow transplants for leukemia, growing skin for burn patients, and research on diabetes, spinal injuries and heart disease. Stem cells were first identified in 1961 by Canadian scientists James Till and Ernest McCulloch in Toronto. Some uses raise ethical questions that society debates.

Medical imaging lets doctors see inside the body without surgery:

🦴X-ray: shows bones; uses small doses of radiation
🌀CT scan: many X-rays combined into cross-section images
🧲MRI: strong magnets and radio waves; great for soft tissue like the brain; no radiation
🔊Ultrasound: high-frequency sound; safe for checking babies before birth
☢️PET scan: tracks a radioactive tracer to show activity

Remember: stem cells can become specialized cells; imaging lets doctors see inside safely.

Key words

  • stem cell: an unspecialized cell that can become specialized cells
  • bone marrow: soft tissue inside bones that makes blood cells
  • MRI: imaging that uses magnets and radio waves
  • ultrasound: imaging that uses high-frequency sound waves
