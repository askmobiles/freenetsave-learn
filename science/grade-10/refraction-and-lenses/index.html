Refraction and lenses

Learn what you need to know

Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one medium into another, because its speed changes.

index of refraction n = c ÷ v (c = speed of light in a vacuum, v = speed in the medium)

Water n = 1.33, glass about 1.50, diamond 2.42. Light going from a fast medium (air) into a slower one (water) bends toward the normal; going into a faster medium, it bends away from the normal. Snell's law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.

Total internal reflection: when light goes from a slower medium to a faster one at an angle greater than the critical angle, all of it reflects back. This is how optical fibres carry internet signals and why diamonds sparkle.

🔍Converging (convex) lens: thicker in the middle; brings rays together; used in magnifying glasses, cameras, the eye
👓Diverging (concave) lens: thinner in the middle; spreads rays out; used to correct near-sightedness

Thin lens equation: 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ, magnification M = −dᵢ/dₒ (same as for mirrors).

Remember: slower medium → bends toward the normal; fibres use total internal reflection.

Key words

  • refraction: the bending of light as it enters a new medium
  • index of refraction: how much a medium slows light: n = c ÷ v
  • critical angle: the angle beyond which total internal reflection happens
  • converging lens: a lens that brings light rays together

See real-life examples

Optical fibre internet

Fibre-optic cables carry light pulses for thousands of kilometres by total internal reflection.

Mirages

On hot roads, light bends through layers of hot air, making a "puddle" that is really sky.

Eyeglasses

Diverging lenses help near-sighted people see far; converging lenses help far-sighted people see close.
