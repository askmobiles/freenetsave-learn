Light and reflection

Learn what you need to know

Light travels in straight lines called rays, at about 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum. Light sources can be incandescent (hot, like the Sun), fluorescent, LED, bioluminescent (fireflies) and more.

Laws of reflection: 1) the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection (both measured from the normal, a line at 90° to the surface); 2) the incident ray, reflected ray and normal are in the same plane.

A plane (flat) mirror makes an image that is the same size, upright, virtual (behind the mirror), the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front, and left–right reversed.

🥄Concave mirror (curves in): can form real, inverted images or magnified virtual images; used in flashlights, makeup mirrors, telescopes
🔮Convex mirror (curves out): always smaller, upright, virtual images with a wide view; used in store security and car side mirrors

Mirror equation: 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ, and magnification M = hᵢ/hₒ = −dᵢ/dₒ.

Remember: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.

Key words

  • normal: a line at 90° to a surface where a ray hits
  • virtual image: an image formed where light only seems to come from
  • real image: an image formed where light rays actually meet
  • focal point: the point where parallel rays meet after reflection

See real-life examples

Car mirrors

Passenger side mirrors are convex. They show a wider area, which is why they say "objects in mirror are closer than they appear."

Flashlights

A concave mirror behind the bulb reflects light into a strong, straight beam.

Ambulance

AMBULANCE is printed backward on the front so drivers read it correctly in their mirrors.
