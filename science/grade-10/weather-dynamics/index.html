Weather dynamics

Why the weather changes: energy from the Sun, air pressure, fronts, storms, and how forecasters predict the weather.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 science.

Learn what you need to know

Weather is driven by uneven heating of Earth by the Sun. The tropics get more energy than the poles, so heat moves toward the poles by winds and ocean currents.

🔼High pressure: sinking air; usually clear, calm weather.
🔽Low pressure: rising air that cools and forms clouds; often rain or snow.
🌬️Wind blows from high pressure to low pressure. The Coriolis effect, from Earth's spin, turns it to the right in the Northern Hemisphere.

Air masses and fronts: an air mass is a huge body of air with similar temperature and moisture, like cold, dry Arctic air. Where two meet is a front. A cold front pushes under warm air fast and brings short, heavy storms. A warm front slides over cold air and brings longer, steady rain.

Water and energy: when water vapour condenses into clouds it releases latent heat, which powers thunderstorms and hurricanes. Oceans store heat; that is why coastal places have milder weather. In Canada, the jet stream steers storms from west to east.

Forecasting: weather stations, balloons, radar and satellites collect data. Computer models use it to predict the weather. Environment and Climate Change Canada issues warnings for tornadoes, blizzards and heat waves.

Weather vs climate: weather is the conditions now; climate is the average over 30 years or more.

Key words

  • air mass: a large body of air with similar temperature and humidity
  • front: the boundary where two air masses meet
  • jet stream: a fast band of wind high in the sky that steers storms
  • Coriolis effect: the turning of winds and currents caused by Earth's spin
