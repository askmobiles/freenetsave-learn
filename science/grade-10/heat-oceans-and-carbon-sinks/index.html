Heat, oceans and carbon sinks

How Earth balances heat, how oceans move heat, and what stores carbon.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 science.

Learn what you need to know

Earth receives energy from the Sun and radiates heat back to space. The balance between incoming and outgoing energy decides Earth's temperature. Albedo is how much sunlight a surface reflects: snow and ice reflect up to 80–90%, oceans and forests absorb most of it.

Positive feedback loop: as ice melts, darker ocean and land are exposed, absorbing more heat, which melts more ice. This is one reason the Arctic is warming so fast.

Oceans store enormous amounts of heat because water has a high specific heat capacity. Ocean currents, like the Gulf Stream and the global thermohaline circulation, move warm and cold water around the world, affecting climate. The ocean has absorbed over 90% of the extra heat from global warming.

A carbon sink takes in more carbon than it releases; a carbon source releases more than it takes in.

🌊Oceans: absorb about a quarter of human CO₂ emissions
🌲Forests: store carbon in wood and soil
🟫Peatlands and wetlands: store huge amounts of carbon; Canada has vast peatlands like the Hudson Bay Lowlands
🧊Permafrost: frozen carbon that can become a source as it thaws

Remember: high albedo cools, low albedo warms; oceans store heat and carbon.

Key words

  • albedo: the fraction of sunlight a surface reflects
  • feedback loop: a change that causes effects which increase or decrease the change
  • thermohaline circulation: global ocean currents driven by temperature and salt
  • carbon source: something that releases more carbon than it absorbs

See real-life examples

Hudson Bay Lowlands

Northern Ontario's Hudson Bay Lowlands hold one of the world's largest peatlands, storing billions of tonnes of carbon.

Wildfires

Large forest fires turn forests from carbon sinks into carbon sources as stored carbon burns.

Snow keeps cold

Fresh snow reflects most sunlight, which is why snowy days can stay cold even when sunny.
