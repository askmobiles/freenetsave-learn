Learn what you need to know

Electromagnetic (EM) waves are waves of electric and magnetic fields. They need no medium and all travel at the speed of light, 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s, in a vacuum.

From longest wavelength (lowest frequency, lowest energy) to shortest wavelength (highest frequency, highest energy):

📻Radio waves: radio, TV, cell phones
🍳Microwaves: cooking, Wi-Fi, radar
🌡️Infrared: heat, remote controls, night-vision cameras
🌈Visible light: red (longest) to violet (shortest)
🕶️Ultraviolet: sunburn, vitamin D, sterilizing
🦴X-rays: medical images, airport scanners
☢️Gamma rays: cancer treatment, from nuclear reactions

wave equation: c = f × λ (speed = frequency × wavelength)

Example: a radio station at 100 MHz (1.00 × 10⁸ Hz) has λ = (3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ (1.00 × 10⁸) = 3.00 m.

Higher-energy waves (UV, X-rays, gamma) are ionizing and can damage cells, so exposure is limited.

Remember: Radio, Microwave, Infrared, Visible, UV, X-ray, Gamma, from long to short.

Key words

  • wavelength: the distance between two wave crests (λ)
  • frequency: how many waves pass a point per second, in hertz
  • ionizing radiation: high-energy radiation that can damage cells
  • infrared: EM waves we feel as heat

See real-life examples

Remote controls

Your TV remote sends infrared pulses. A phone camera can often see the flashing that your eyes cannot.

The UV Index

Environment Canada reports the UV Index. At 3 or higher, wear sunscreen and a hat.

Radio telescopes

The CHIME radio telescope in BC detects mysterious fast radio bursts from distant galaxies.
