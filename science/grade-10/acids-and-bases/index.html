Acids and bases

Properties of acids and bases, the pH scale, indicators and neutralization. Grade 10 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 10 · Chemistry: Chemical Reactions

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Acids release hydrogen ions (H⁺) in water. They taste sour, turn blue litmus red, react with active metals to make hydrogen gas, and react with carbonates to make CO₂. Examples: HCl (hydrochloric acid), H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid), CH₃COOH (acetic acid, vinegar).

Bases release hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in water. They taste bitter, feel slippery, and turn red litmus blue. Examples: NaOH (sodium hydroxide), Ca(OH)₂, ammonia.

The pH scale runs 0–14. Each step is a factor of 10: pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4 and 100 times more acidic than pH 5.

🧪Litmus: red in acid, blue in base
🌸Phenolphthalein: colourless in acid, pink in base
🌈Universal indicator: a colour for every pH
🥬Red cabbage juice: a natural indicator

Neutralization: acid + base → salt + water, for example HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O. Uses: antacids, liming acidic lakes and soils, treating spills.

Remember: acids → H⁺, bases → OH⁻, and each pH step is ×10.

Key words

  • acid: a substance that releases H⁺ ions in water
  • base: a substance that releases OH⁻ ions in water
  • indicator: a substance that changes colour with pH
  • neutralization: an acid and base reacting to make salt and water

See real-life examples

Acid rain in Sudbury

Sulfur dioxide from smelters once made rain around Sudbury very acidic. Cleaner smelters and liming have helped lakes recover.

Pool chemistry

Swimming pools are kept at about pH 7.4 to protect swimmers' eyes and skin.

Ocean acidification

The ocean absorbs CO₂, which lowers its pH and makes it harder for shellfish to build shells.
