Digestion, circulation and breathing

How the digestive, circulatory and respiratory systems work together.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 10 science.

Learn what you need to know

Digestive system: food is broken down mechanically (teeth, stomach churning) and chemically (enzymes). Mouth → esophagus → stomach (acid and pepsin) → small intestine (most digestion and absorption, through villi) → large intestine (absorbs water) → rectum. The liver makes bile; the pancreas makes enzymes.

Respiratory system: air flows through the nose, trachea, bronchi and bronchioles to the alveoli, tiny air sacs surrounded by capillaries. Oxygen diffuses into the blood; carbon dioxide diffuses out. The diaphragm contracts to pull air in.

Circulatory system: the heart has four chambers. Blood from the body enters the right atrium → right ventricle → lungs (to pick up oxygen) → left atrium → left ventricle → body. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins return it (with valves), and capillaries exchange materials with cells.

🔴Red blood cells carry oxygen with hemoglobin
⚪White blood cells fight infection
🟡Platelets help blood clot
💧Plasma carries nutrients, wastes and hormones

Together, these systems deliver nutrients and oxygen to every cell and remove wastes.

Remember: digestion supplies nutrients, lungs supply oxygen, blood delivers both.

Key words

  • villi: finger-like folds in the small intestine that absorb nutrients
  • alveoli: tiny air sacs where gases are exchanged in the lungs
  • hemoglobin: the iron-containing protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen
  • enzyme: a protein that speeds up chemical reactions like digestion

See real-life examples

Blood pressure checks

A blood pressure cuff measures the pressure in your arteries when the heart beats and when it rests.

Asthma

In asthma, bronchioles narrow and make breathing hard. Inhalers relax the muscles around them.

Surface area

If the villi in your small intestine were spread flat, they would cover an area about the size of a badminton court.
