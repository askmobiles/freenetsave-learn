Learn what you need to know

Atoms gain, lose or share valence electrons to reach a stable, full outer shell.

⚡Ionic compound: a metal gives electrons to a non-metal; ions of opposite charge attract (NaCl, MgO)
🤝Molecular (covalent) compound: non-metals share electrons (H₂O, CO₂, CH₄)

Ions: metals lose electrons to form positive ions (Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺). Non-metals gain electrons to form negative ions (Cl⁻, O²⁻, N³⁻). Ion charge follows the group: group 1 is +1, group 2 is +2, group 16 is −2, group 17 is −1.

Writing ionic formulas: the total charge must equal zero. Use the criss-cross method: Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻ → MgCl₂. Al³⁺ and O²⁻ → Al₂O₃.

Naming ionic compounds: metal name + non-metal with "-ide": sodium chloride, magnesium oxide. Polyatomic ions are groups that act as one ion, like hydroxide (OH⁻), carbonate (CO₃²⁻), sulfate (SO₄²⁻) and nitrate (NO₃⁻).

Naming molecular compounds: use prefixes: mono (1), di (2), tri (3), tetra (4), penta (5). CO₂ is carbon dioxide; N₂O₄ is dinitrogen tetroxide; CO is carbon monoxide.

Remember: metal + non-metal = ionic (charges cancel); non-metal + non-metal = molecular (use prefixes).

Key words

  • ion: an atom that has gained or lost electrons
  • ionic compound: a compound of positive and negative ions
  • molecular compound: a compound where non-metals share electrons
  • polyatomic ion: a group of atoms with an overall charge

See real-life examples

Table salt

Sodium chloride (NaCl) is an ionic compound. Its ions form a cube-shaped crystal.

Baking soda

Baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO₃, which contains a polyatomic ion.

Carbon monoxide alarms

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a deadly molecular gas with no smell. Ontario law requires CO alarms in homes with fuel-burning appliances.
