Learn what you need to know

A magnet pulls (attracts) things made of iron or steel, like paper clips, nails and fridge doors. It does not pull wood, plastic, glass, paper or most coins.

Every magnet has two ends called poles: a north pole (N) and a south pole (S). The pull is strongest at the poles.

🧲N and S attract (pull together)
↔️N and N repel (push apart)
↔️S and S repel (push apart)

A magnet can pull through paper, water and even your hand. The area around a magnet where it pulls is its magnetic field.

The Earth is like a giant magnet. A compass needle is a tiny magnet that always points north, so people use it to find their way.

Remember: opposite poles attract, same poles repel. Magnets pull iron and steel.

Key words

  • magnet: an object that pulls iron and steel
  • pole: an end of a magnet, north or south
  • attract: pull together
  • repel: push apart
  • compass: a tool with a magnet needle that points north

See real-life examples

Fridge magnets

Fridge magnets stick because fridge doors are made of steel.

Floating magnets

Put two ring magnets on a pencil with the same poles facing. The top one floats because they repel.

Recycling

Recycling centres use giant magnets to pull steel cans out of the trash.
