Animal body coverings and groups

Fur, feathers, scales and shells, and how they help. Grade 3 science.

⭐ Extra topic · Ontario teaches this in Grade 2 (Growth and Changes in Animals)

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Animals have different body coverings. Each covering helps the animal live where it lives.

🦊Fur or hair: keeps mammals warm
🦅Feathers: help birds fly and stay warm
🐍Dry scales: protect reptiles and keep in water
🐟Wet scales: help fish glide through water
🐢Shells: armour for turtles, snails and crabs
🐸Moist skin: amphibians can breathe through it

Coverings also help animals hide. This is called camouflage. A snowshoe hare turns white in winter. A frog's green skin blends in with leaves.

Some coverings warn others to stay away. A porcupine has sharp quills. A skunk's black and white fur says "do not bother me!"

Remember: the covering tells you the group, and it helps the animal survive.

Key words

  • body covering: what is on the outside of an animal's body
  • camouflage: colours or patterns that help an animal hide
  • quills: sharp, stiff spines, like a porcupine's
  • shell: a hard outer covering

See real-life examples

Ducks stay dry

Ducks spread oil from a gland onto their feathers. The oil keeps water off, so they stay warm and dry.

Turtle armour

A turtle can pull its head and legs inside its hard shell to stay safe.

Arctic fox

The Arctic fox has thick white fur in winter and brown fur in summer, so it blends in all year.
