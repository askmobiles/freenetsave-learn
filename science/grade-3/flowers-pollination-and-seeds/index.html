Flowers, pollination and seeds

How flowering plants make seeds and how seeds travel. Grade 3 science.

⭐ Extra topic · also taught in England Year 3 and US Grade 3 (NGSS)

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Many plants grow flowers. A flower's job is to make seeds so new plants can grow.

🌸Petals: bright colours and smells attract insects
✨Stamen: makes yellow powder called pollen
🎯Pistil: in the middle; catches pollen and grows seeds
🍃Sepals: small green leaves that protected the bud

Pollination is when pollen moves from one flower to another. Bees, butterflies, birds, bats and the wind carry pollen. A bee visits a flower for sweet nectar, and pollen sticks to its fuzzy body.

After pollination, the flower makes seeds, often inside a fruit. Seeds travel to new places (this is called seed dispersal): blown by wind (dandelions, maple keys), carried by animals (burrs stick to fur, birds eat berries), or floating on water (coconuts).

The life cycle of a flowering plant: seed → seedling → adult plant → flower → pollination → seeds.

Remember: flowers make seeds; pollinators move pollen; seeds travel to grow new plants.

Key words

  • pollen: yellow powder made by flowers
  • pollination: moving pollen from flower to flower
  • nectar: a sweet drink inside flowers
  • seed dispersal: how seeds move to new places

See real-life examples

Maple keys

Maple trees make winged seeds called keys. They spin like helicopters as the wind carries them away.

Bees and apples

Apple orchards in Ontario need bees. Without pollination, the trees would make very few apples.

Burrs on socks

After a walk in a field, burrs stick to your socks. That is a plant using you to carry its seeds!
