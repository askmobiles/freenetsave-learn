Changes in materials

Changes you can undo and changes you cannot.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 3 science.

Learn what you need to know

Materials can change. Some changes can be reversed (undone). Some cannot.

🧊Melting ice: can be frozen again (reversible)
🧂Dissolving salt: water can dry up and leave the salt (reversible)
✂️Cutting paper: still paper, just smaller
🍳Cooking an egg: cannot be uncooked (not reversible)
🔥Burning wood: turns to ash and smoke (not reversible)
🔩Rusting iron: makes new reddish rust (not reversible)

A change that makes a new material, like burning, cooking or rusting, is hard or impossible to undo. Signs of a new material: a new colour, a smell, bubbles, heat or light.

Changing shape or size (cutting, folding, stretching) and changing state (melting, freezing) do not make a new material.

Remember: melt, freeze, dissolve, fold = can undo. Cook, burn, rust = cannot undo.

Key words

  • reversible: can be changed back
  • irreversible: cannot be changed back
  • rust: a reddish material made when iron gets wet
  • dissolve: mix into a liquid so it seems to disappear

See real-life examples

Toast

Bread turned into toast cannot become bread again. The brown colour and smell show a new material formed.

Chocolate

You can melt a chocolate bar and let it cool into a new shape. It is still chocolate.

A rusty bike

A bike left out in the rain gets orange rust. The iron has changed into a new material.
