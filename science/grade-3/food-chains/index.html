Food chains

Learn what you need to know

A food chain shows how energy passes from one living thing to the next when something is eaten.

🌿grass (producer)
🐇rabbit eats grass
🦊fox eats rabbit

Every food chain starts with a plant, called a producer, because it makes its own food from sunlight. Animals are consumers: they eat other living things.

🥬Herbivores eat only plants (rabbit, deer)
🥩Carnivores eat only animals (wolf, owl)
🍽️Omnivores eat plants and animals (bear, raccoon, people)
🍄Decomposers break down dead things (worms, mushrooms)

The arrows in a food chain point in the direction the energy goes: grass → rabbit → fox.

Remember: producer → herbivore → carnivore, and decomposers recycle what is left.

Key words

  • producer: a living thing that makes its own food, like a plant
  • consumer: an animal that eats other living things
  • predator: an animal that hunts other animals
  • prey: an animal that is hunted
  • decomposer: breaks down dead plants and animals

See real-life examples

A pond chain

Algae → tadpole → fish → heron. The heron eats the fish, which ate the tadpole, which ate the algae.

Ocean chain

Tiny plants called plankton → small fish → seal → polar bear.

Worms help

Earthworms eat dead leaves. Their waste makes the soil rich, so new plants can grow.
