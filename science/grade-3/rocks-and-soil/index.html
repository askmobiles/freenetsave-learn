Rocks and soil

Kinds of rocks and what we use them for. Grade 3 science.

Learn what you need to know

The Earth is made of rock. Rocks come in many colours, sizes and textures. Rocks are made of minerals.

Scientists sort rocks into three big families by how they formed:

🌋Igneous: formed from hot melted rock that cooled (granite, basalt)
🪨Sedimentary: layers of sand and mud pressed together (sandstone, limestone)
💎Metamorphic: changed by heat and pressure (marble, slate)

We test rocks by their properties: colour, hardness (can a nail scratch it?), texture (rough or smooth), and whether they have layers or crystals.

People use rocks every day: granite countertops, marble statues, slate roofs, limestone in cement, and gravel on roads.

Remember: igneous from fire, sedimentary from layers, metamorphic from change.

Key words

  • mineral: a natural solid that rocks are made of
  • igneous: rock made from cooled melted rock
  • sedimentary: rock made from layers pressed together
  • metamorphic: rock changed by heat and pressure

See real-life examples

Canadian Shield

Much of Canada sits on very old, hard igneous and metamorphic rock called the Canadian Shield.

Fossils in layers

Fossils are usually found in sedimentary rock, because shells and bones got buried in the layers of mud.

Pumice floats

Pumice is an igneous rock full of air bubbles. It is so light it can float on water.
