How plants grow and spread

Seeds, pollination and how seeds travel. Grade 3 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Most plants grow from seeds. A seed has a tiny baby plant and stored food inside a hard coat. When it gets water and warmth, it germinates (starts to grow).

Flowers make seeds. First, pollen must move from one flower to another. This is called pollination. Bees, butterflies, birds and the wind carry pollen.

Plants cannot walk, so seeds travel in clever ways:

💨Wind: dandelion fluff, maple "helicopters"
🐿️Animals: squirrels bury nuts; burrs stick to fur
🍒Eaten: birds eat berries and drop the seeds
🌊Water: coconuts float to new beaches
💥Pop: some pods burst and fling seeds

Some plants also grow new plants without seeds. A potato can sprout from its "eyes". Strawberries send out runners.

Remember: flower → pollination → seed → seed travels → new plant.

Key words

  • germinate: when a seed starts to grow
  • pollen: yellow powder that flowers need to make seeds
  • pollination: moving pollen from flower to flower
  • seed dispersal: how seeds are spread to new places

See real-life examples

Maple keys

Maple seeds have wings. When they fall, they spin like helicopters and float away from the tree.

Burrs on socks

After a walk in a field, burrs may stick to your socks. You carry the seeds to a new place!

Bees and apples

Apple farmers bring beehives to their orchards. Without bees, there would be very few apples.
