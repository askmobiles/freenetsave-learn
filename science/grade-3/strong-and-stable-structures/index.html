Strong and stable structures

What makes buildings and bridges strong and steady. Grade 3 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

A structure holds things up or holds things in. A strong structure can carry a load (weight) without breaking. A stable structure does not tip over.

🔺Triangles: the strongest shape for frames
🏛️Columns: hold up roofs
🌉Arches: spread the load outward
🧱Wide base: keeps it stable

Triangles are used in bridges, towers and roofs because a triangle does not bend out of shape when pushed. A square can squash into a diamond.

Structures are more stable when the heavy parts are low and the base is wide. That is why the Eiffel Tower and the CN Tower are wide at the bottom.

Engineers test structures to see how much load they can hold before they bend or break.

Remember: triangles for strength, a wide base for stability.

Key words

  • load: the weight a structure has to hold
  • stable: does not tip over
  • engineer: a person who designs structures
  • arch: a curved shape that holds weight

See real-life examples

CN Tower

The CN Tower in Toronto has a wide, heavy base made of concrete, so it stays stable in strong winds.

Paper bridge

A flat sheet of paper between two books sags. Fold it like a fan (zigzag) and it can hold coins!

Stone arches

Old stone bridges use arches. The stones press against each other and hold heavy loads for hundreds of years.
