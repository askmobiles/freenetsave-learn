How plants make food

Learn what you need to know

Animals have to find food. Plants make their own! Making food from sunlight is called photosynthesis (say: foto-SIN-thuh-sis).

Plants need three things to make food:

☀️sunlight
💧water, taken in by the roots
🌬️carbon dioxide, a gas from the air, taken in by the leaves

The green colour in leaves is called chlorophyll. It catches sunlight. The plant turns the water and air into sugar, which is its food. It also gives off oxygen, the gas we breathe.

Almost all food on Earth starts with plants. A cow eats grass. You drink the cow's milk. The energy in the milk came from the Sun through the grass.

Remember: sunlight + water + carbon dioxide → sugar (food) + oxygen.

Key words

  • photosynthesis: how plants make food using sunlight
  • chlorophyll: the green colouring in leaves that catches sunlight
  • carbon dioxide: a gas in the air that plants take in
  • oxygen: the gas plants give off that we breathe

See real-life examples

Pale grass

When a board lies on grass for a week, the grass under it turns yellow. Without sunlight, it cannot make chlorophyll or food.

Fall leaves

In fall, trees stop making chlorophyll. The green fades, and the yellow and orange colours underneath show.

Bubbles in a pond

On a sunny day you may see tiny bubbles on pond plants. That is oxygen they are giving off.
