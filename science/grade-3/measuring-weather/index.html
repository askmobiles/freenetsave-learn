Weather and how we measure it

Thermometers, rain gauges, wind vanes and forecasts. Grade 3 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Meteorologists are scientists who study weather. They use tools to measure it and make forecasts.

🌡️Thermometer: measures temperature
🧪Rain gauge: measures how much rain fell
🧭Wind vane: shows wind direction
🌀Anemometer: measures wind speed
📉Barometer: measures air pressure
🛰️Satellites: take pictures of clouds

Temperature in Canada is measured in degrees Celsius (°C). Water freezes at 0 °C. A warm summer day is about 25 °C.

Clouds give clues. Fluffy white cumulus clouds mean fair weather. Dark, tall cumulonimbus clouds can bring thunderstorms. Thin, wispy cirrus clouds are high in the sky.

Remember: thermometer = temperature, rain gauge = rain, wind vane = direction.

Key words

  • meteorologist: a scientist who studies weather
  • forecast: a guess about future weather based on data
  • precipitation: water falling from clouds: rain, snow, sleet, hail
  • Celsius: the temperature scale used in Canada

Weather app

The weather app on a phone uses data from weather stations and satellites to show tomorrow's forecast.

Make a rain gauge

Put a straight-sided jar outside with a ruler taped to it. After rain, read how many millimetres fell.

Thunderstorm clouds

On a hot afternoon, tall dark clouds may grow quickly. Soon there may be thunder, lightning and heavy rain.
