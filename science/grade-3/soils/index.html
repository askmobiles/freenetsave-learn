Soils

What soil is made of, kinds of soil, and why it matters. Grade 3 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 3 · Soils in the Environment

Learn what you need to know

Soil is the loose top layer of the ground. It is a mixture of tiny bits of rock, humus (rotted plants and animals), water and air.

🏖️Sand: big grains, water drains fast
🏺Clay: tiny sticky grains, holds lots of water
🌱Loam: a mix of sand, clay and humus; best for gardens

Soil has layers. The topsoil is dark and full of humus and living things. Below it is the subsoil, and under that is solid bedrock.

Many living things live in soil: earthworms, ants, beetles, bacteria and fungi. Worms dig tunnels that let air and water in.

We can protect soil by planting trees and cover crops so rain and wind do not wash or blow it away, and by adding compost.

Remember: soil = rock bits + humus + water + air. Loam is best for plants.

Key words

  • humus: dark, rotted plant and animal matter in soil
  • loam: rich soil that mixes sand, clay and humus
  • topsoil: the top, darkest layer of soil
  • compost: rotted food scraps and leaves that feed soil

See real-life examples

Jar test

Shake soil and water in a jar and let it sit. Sand settles at the bottom, then silt, then clay. Humus floats on top.

Composting

Banana peels, apple cores and leaves go in a compost bin. In a few months they turn into dark, rich soil.

Clay pots

Potters use clay soil because it is sticky and can be shaped, then baked hard.
