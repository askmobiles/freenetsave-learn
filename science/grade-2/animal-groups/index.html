Animal groups

Mammals, birds, fish, reptiles, amphibians and insects. Grade 2 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Scientists sort animals into groups. Animals in the same group share body parts and ways of living.

🐻Mammals: fur or hair, babies drink milk
🐦Birds: feathers, wings, lay eggs
🐟Fish: scales, fins, breathe with gills
🐢Reptiles: dry scales, most lay eggs
🐸Amphibians: smooth wet skin, start life in water
🐞Insects: 6 legs, 3 body parts

You are a mammal! People have hair, and babies drink milk.

Some animals surprise us. A whale lives in the sea but it is a mammal, not a fish. It breathes air and feeds its baby milk. A bat flies but it is a mammal, not a bird.

Remember: look at the body covering first: fur, feathers, scales or wet skin.

Key words

  • mammal: has hair or fur and feeds its babies milk
  • reptile: has dry scaly skin, like a snake or turtle
  • amphibian: lives in water and on land, like a frog
  • insect: a small animal with 6 legs

See real-life examples

A whale is not a fish

Whales come up to the surface to breathe air through a blowhole. Their babies drink milk. So whales are mammals.

Bats

Bats have fur and feed their babies milk. They fly with wings made of skin, not feathers.

Spider or insect?

A spider has 8 legs, so it is not an insect. Insects have 6 legs.
