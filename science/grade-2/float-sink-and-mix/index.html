Float, sink and mix

What floats, what sinks, and what dissolves. Grade 2 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 2 · Properties of Liquids and Solids

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

When you put something in water, it may float on top or sink to the bottom.

🦆Floats: rubber duck
🍃Floats: leaf
🪵Floats: wood
🪨Sinks: rock
🔑Sinks: key
🪙Sinks: coin

Things that are heavy for their size, like a rock, usually sink. Things that are light for their size, like a cork, usually float. Shape matters too: a flat ball of clay sinks, but clay shaped like a boat floats.

Some things dissolve in water. Sugar and salt seem to disappear when you stir them in. Sand does not dissolve. It sinks to the bottom.

Remember: light for their size floats, heavy for their size sinks; sugar dissolves, sand does not.

Key words

  • float: stay on top of the water
  • sink: go down to the bottom
  • dissolve: mix into water so you cannot see it any more
  • mixture: two or more things mixed together

See real-life examples

Boats

Huge steel ships float because their shape holds a lot of air inside.

Lemonade

When you stir sugar into lemonade, the sugar dissolves. You cannot see it, but you can taste it.

Sand in water

Shake a jar of sand and water. When you stop, the sand sinks to the bottom again. It does not dissolve.
