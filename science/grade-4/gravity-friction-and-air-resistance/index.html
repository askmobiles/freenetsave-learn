Gravity, friction and air resistance

Learn what you need to know

A force is a push or a pull. Three forces act on us all the time.

🌍Gravity: pulls everything down toward the centre of the Earth
🛷Friction: slows things when surfaces rub together
🪂Air resistance: air pushing against moving things, slowing them down

Gravity is why a ball falls and why we stay on the ground. The Moon has weaker gravity, so astronauts can jump very high there. Your weight is the pull of gravity on you.

Friction happens when surfaces rub. Rough surfaces have more friction than smooth ones. Friction can be helpful (shoe grip, bike brakes) or a problem (it wears out parts and makes heat).

Air resistance (drag) pushes against things moving through air. A large surface catches more air. A parachute has lots of air resistance so the person falls slowly. Water resistance works the same way in water.

Remember: gravity pulls down; friction and air resistance slow things down.

Key words

  • gravity: the force that pulls things toward the Earth
  • weight: how hard gravity pulls on something
  • friction: a force that slows things when surfaces rub
  • air resistance: air pushing against a moving object

See real-life examples

Winter sand

In winter, cities spread sand on icy sidewalks. Sand adds friction so people do not slip.

Parachutes

A skydiver opens a parachute. Its big shape catches lots of air, so the skydiver lands gently.

Rubbing hands

Rub your hands together fast. They get warm. Friction makes heat.
