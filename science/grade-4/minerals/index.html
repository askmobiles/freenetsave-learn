Minerals

What minerals are and how we identify them. Grade 4 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

A mineral is a natural, non-living solid with its own crystal shape. Rocks are made of one or more minerals.

Geologists identify minerals with tests:

🎨Colour: what it looks like
✏️Streak: the colour of its powder when rubbed on a white tile
✨Lustre: shiny like metal, glassy, or dull
💎Hardness: what it can scratch, on a scale from 1 (talc) to 10 (diamond)
🧊Crystal shape: cubes, columns or flat sheets
🧲Magnetism: some, like magnetite, attract magnets

Mohs hardness scale: a mineral can scratch any mineral with a lower number. Your fingernail is about 2.5, a copper coin about 3.5, a steel nail about 5.5.

We use minerals every day: quartz in glass and watches, graphite in pencils, halite as table salt, gypsum in drywall, and metals like iron, copper, gold and nickel. Canada mines many minerals.

Remember: test colour, streak, lustre, hardness and crystal shape to identify a mineral.

Key words

  • mineral: a natural, non-living solid with a crystal shape
  • streak: the colour of a mineral's powder
  • lustre: how a mineral reflects light: shiny, glassy or dull
  • hardness: how easily a mineral can be scratched

See real-life examples

Fool's gold

Pyrite looks like gold, but its streak is greenish black, while real gold's streak is yellow. The streak test catches it.

Pencil "lead"

The grey in a pencil is graphite, a soft mineral (hardness 1–2) that leaves a dark streak on paper.

Sudbury, Ontario

Sudbury is famous for its nickel mines. Nickel goes into coins and stainless steel.
