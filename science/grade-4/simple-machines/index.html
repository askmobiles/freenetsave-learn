Simple machines

Levers, wheels, inclined planes, wedges, screws and pulleys. Grade 4 science.

Learn what you need to know

A machine is anything that makes work easier. Simple machines have few or no moving parts. They help us lift, move, cut or hold things using less force.

🪚Lever: a bar that turns on a point (seesaw, crowbar)
🛞Wheel and axle: a wheel turning on a rod (doorknob, bike)
🛝Inclined plane: a ramp (wheelchair ramp, slide)
🪓Wedge: a sharp edge that splits (axe, knife)
🔩Screw: a ramp wrapped around a post (jar lid, bolt)
🏗️Pulley: a wheel with a rope (flagpole, crane)

A lever has three parts: the fulcrum (the point it turns on), the load (what you move) and the effort (your push or pull). The closer the load is to the fulcrum, the easier it is to lift.

Many tools are compound machines, made of two or more simple machines. Scissors are two levers plus two wedges.

Remember: lever, wheel and axle, inclined plane, wedge, screw and pulley.

Key words

  • simple machine: a tool with few parts that makes work easier
  • fulcrum: the point a lever turns on
  • load: the object being moved
  • effort: the force you use

See real-life examples

Seesaw

A seesaw is a lever. A small child can lift a bigger one if the bigger one sits closer to the middle.

Ramps

It is easier to push a wheelchair up a long ramp than to lift it up the stairs. The ramp is an inclined plane.

Can opener

A can opener uses a wedge (the blade), a lever (the handles) and a wheel and axle (the turning knob).
