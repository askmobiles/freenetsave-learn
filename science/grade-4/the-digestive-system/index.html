The digestive system

Learn what you need to know

Digestion is how your body breaks food into tiny pieces called nutrients that your blood can carry to your cells.

👄Mouth: teeth chew and saliva starts breaking down food
🧵Esophagus: a tube that squeezes food down to the stomach
🫃Stomach: churns food with strong juices
🌀Small intestine: nutrients pass into the blood (about 6 metres long!)
💧Large intestine: takes water back out of leftover waste
🚽Waste leaves the body

Food takes about a day or more to travel through your whole digestive system.

Fibre from fruits, vegetables and whole grains helps food move through. Drinking water helps too.

Remember: mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine.

Key words

  • digestion: breaking food down so the body can use it
  • saliva: spit, which starts breaking down food
  • esophagus: the tube from your mouth to your stomach
  • nutrients: the useful parts of food your body needs

See real-life examples

Chew well

Chewing breaks food into small pieces, which makes digestion easier. That is why we should not gulp food.

Bread tastes sweet

Chew plain bread for a long time and it starts to taste sweet. Saliva is turning starch into sugar.

Hiccups and burps

Burps happen when air swallowed with food comes back up from the stomach.
