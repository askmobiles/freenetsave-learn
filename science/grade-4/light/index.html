Learn what you need to know

Light is a form of energy. It travels in straight lines, very fast: about 300,000 kilometres every second.

Light can be reflected (bounced). Smooth, shiny surfaces like mirrors reflect light well. That is how you see yourself.

Light can be refracted (bent) when it passes from one material into another, like from air into water. A straw in a glass of water looks bent because of refraction.

White light is a mix of all colours. A prism or raindrops can split it into the colours of the rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet.

🪞reflect: bounce off
🥤refract: bend
🌈disperse: split into colours
🧱absorb: soak up

We see an object's colour because it reflects that colour of light. A red apple reflects red light and absorbs the rest.

Remember: light travels straight, reflects off mirrors, bends in water, and white light holds every colour.

Key words

  • reflection: light bouncing off a surface
  • refraction: light bending as it passes into another material
  • prism: a clear shape that splits white light into colours
  • absorb: soak up

See real-life examples

Rainbow

After a rain shower, sunlight passes through raindrops. Each drop bends and splits the light into a rainbow.

Periscope

A periscope uses two mirrors to reflect light around a corner. Submarines use them to see above the water.

Black shirt in summer

A black shirt absorbs most light, so it gets warmer in the sun than a white shirt, which reflects light.
