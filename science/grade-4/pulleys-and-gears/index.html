Pulleys and gears

How pulleys and gears change force, speed and direction. Grade 4 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 4 · Machines and Their Mechanisms

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

A pulley is a wheel with a groove for a rope. It can change the direction of a force: you pull down and the load goes up.

⬇️Fixed pulley: stays in place, changes direction (flagpole)
⬆️Movable pulley: moves with the load, halves the force needed
🏗️Pulley system: several pulleys together make heavy lifting easy (cranes)

Gears are wheels with teeth. When two gears touch, turning one turns the other, in the opposite direction.

A big gear turning a small gear makes the small gear spin faster. A small gear turning a big gear makes the big gear turn slower but with more force. Bikes use gears to make pedalling up hills easier.

Gears can also be joined by a chain (like a bike) so they turn in the same direction.

Remember: pulleys change direction and reduce force; touching gears turn opposite ways.

Key words

  • pulley: a wheel with a rope used to lift things
  • gear: a wheel with teeth that turns another gear
  • fixed pulley: a pulley that stays in one place
  • movable pulley: a pulley that moves with the load

See real-life examples

Flagpole

At school, you pull the rope down and the flag goes up. A fixed pulley at the top changes the direction.

Bike gears

On a steep hill, cyclists switch to a low gear. Each pedal turn is easier, but the bike goes slower.

Clock

Inside a wind-up clock, many gears of different sizes turn the hour, minute and second hands at different speeds.
