Habitats and communities

Food webs, communities and how people affect habitats. Grade 4 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 4 · Habitats and Communities

Learn what you need to know

A habitat is where a living thing gets the food, water, shelter and space it needs. All the living things in one habitat make up a community.

Living things in a community depend on each other. Food chains connect into a food web, which shows many feeding links at once.

🌿Producers: plants make food from sunlight
🐰Primary consumers: eat plants
🦊Secondary consumers: eat animals
🍄Decomposers: break down dead things

If one part of a food web changes, the others are affected. If wolves are removed, deer may increase and eat too many young trees.

People change habitats by building, farming, polluting and cutting forests. We also help by protecting parks, planting native plants and cleaning up. Animals at risk of disappearing are called endangered.

Remember: a community is all the living things in a habitat, linked in a food web.

Key words

  • community: all the living things in one area
  • food web: many food chains linked together
  • endangered: at risk of disappearing forever
  • native: naturally belongs in a place

See real-life examples

Pond community

A pond holds algae, cattails, insects, frogs, fish, turtles and herons. Each one depends on others for food or shelter.

Monarch butterflies

Monarch caterpillars only eat milkweed. When people plant milkweed, monarch numbers can recover.

Yellowstone wolves

When wolves returned to Yellowstone Park, deer and elk numbers dropped, and young trees along rivers grew back.
