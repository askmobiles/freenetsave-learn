Heat

Learn what you need to know

Heat is energy that moves from warmer things to cooler things. Heat always flows from hot to cold, never the other way.

🍳Conduction: heat moves through touching objects (a hot pan handle)
♨️Convection: warm liquids and gases rise and cool ones sink (boiling water, a heater warming a room)
☀️Radiation: heat travels as rays, even through empty space (sunshine, a campfire)

Conductors like metal let heat move through them easily. Insulators like wood, plastic, wool, air and foam slow heat down. That is why pot handles are plastic and winter coats are puffy.

Heat makes most things expand (get a little bigger) and cold makes them contract. Bridges have gaps so they can expand on hot days.

Remember: heat moves from hot to cold by conduction, convection and radiation.

Key words

  • conduction: heat moving through touching objects
  • convection: heat carried by moving liquids or gases
  • radiation: heat travelling as rays
  • insulator: a material that slows the flow of heat

See real-life examples

Hot chocolate spoon

A metal spoon in hot chocolate quickly gets hot at the handle. Heat travels up the metal by conduction.

Hot air balloon

Burners heat the air inside a balloon. Hot air rises (convection), lifting the balloon.

Campfire

You feel warm in front of a campfire even though the air between you is cooler. Heat reaches you by radiation.
