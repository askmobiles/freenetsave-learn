Earth, Sun and Moon

Rotation, revolution, seasons and Moon phases. Grade 4 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

The Earth rotates (spins) on its axis once every 24 hours. This gives us day and night.

The Earth also revolves (travels) around the Sun. One trip takes about 365 days, which is one year.

The Earth's axis is tilted. When the northern half is tilted toward the Sun, we get summer: longer days and more direct sunlight. When it tilts away, we get winter.

🌍Earth spins once a day
☀️Earth goes around the Sun once a year
🌙The Moon goes around Earth in about a month
🌗The Moon has phases because we see different amounts of its lit side

The Moon does not make light. Half of it is always lit by the Sun. As the Moon travels around Earth, we see more or less of the lit half: new moon, crescent, quarter, gibbous and full moon.

Remember: rotation = day and night, revolution = a year, tilt = seasons.

Key words

  • rotate: spin around an axis
  • revolve: travel around another object
  • axis: an imaginary line through the Earth from pole to pole
  • orbit: the path one object takes around another

See real-life examples

Summer in Canada

In June, Toronto gets about 15 hours of daylight. In December, only about 9. That is the tilt at work.

Opposite seasons

When it is winter in Canada, it is summer in Australia, because the southern half is tilted toward the Sun.

Leap year

A trip around the Sun takes about 365 and a quarter days. Every four years we add February 29 to catch up.
