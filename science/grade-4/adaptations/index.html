Adaptations

Learn what you need to know

An adaptation is a body part or a behaviour that helps a living thing survive in its habitat. Adaptations develop over many, many generations.

Physical adaptations are body parts:

🦆Webbed feet for swimming
🦅Sharp talons and beak for hunting
🐻‍❄️Thick fur and fat for cold
🌵Spines and a thick stem to store water
🦒Long neck to reach high leaves
🦎Colours that blend in (camouflage)

Behavioural adaptations are things an animal does: birds migrate south, bears hibernate, wolves hunt in packs, and opossums play dead.

Some animals copy the look of a dangerous animal. This is called mimicry. The harmless viceroy butterfly looks like the bad-tasting monarch, so birds leave it alone.

Remember: body-part adaptations and behaviour adaptations both help living things survive.

Key words

  • adaptation: a feature or behaviour that helps a living thing survive
  • camouflage: colours or patterns that help a living thing hide
  • mimicry: looking like another, more dangerous living thing
  • hibernate: go into a deep sleep for the winter

See real-life examples

Snowshoe hare

The snowshoe hare has big furry feet that work like snowshoes, and its fur turns white in winter so foxes cannot spot it.

Beavers

Beavers have orange teeth that never stop growing, so they can chew trees all their lives. Their flat tails steer when they swim.

Maple trees

In fall, maple trees drop their leaves. This saves water during the frozen winter. It is a plant adaptation.
