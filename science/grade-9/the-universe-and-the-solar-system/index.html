The universe and the solar system

Scale of the universe, how the solar system formed, and its members. Grade 9 science.

Learn what you need to know

The universe is enormous. Distances are measured in special units:

🌍Astronomical unit (AU): the average Earth–Sun distance, about 150 million km
💫Light-year (ly): the distance light travels in a year, about 9.5 trillion km

From smallest to largest: planet → solar system → galaxy (our Milky Way, with hundreds of billions of stars) → galaxy groups and clusters → the observable universe, with billions of galaxies.

The solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust (a nebula). Most of the material formed the Sun; the rest formed a spinning disc that clumped into planets.

The terrestrial planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are small and rocky. The Jovian planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) are gas and ice giants. Beyond Neptune lie the Kuiper belt with dwarf planets like Pluto, and the distant Oort cloud, the source of many comets.

Remember: AU for the solar system, light-years for stars and galaxies.

Key words

  • astronomical unit: the average distance from Earth to the Sun
  • light-year: the distance light travels in one year
  • nebula: a cloud of gas and dust in space
  • Kuiper belt: a region of icy bodies beyond Neptune

See real-life examples

Looking back in time

Light from the Andromeda Galaxy takes about 2.5 million years to reach us. We see it as it was long ago.

Neptune is far

Neptune is about 30 AU from the Sun. Sunlight takes over 4 hours to reach it.

Pluto

Pluto lives in the Kuiper belt. In 2006 it was reclassified as a dwarf planet because it has not cleared its orbit.
