Static and current electricity

Charges, charging by friction, contact and induction, and electric current. Grade 9 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 9 · Physics: Electricity

Learn what you need to know

All matter contains charges. Objects become charged when electrons move from one object to another. Protons do not move.

➕➕Like charges repel
➕➖Opposite charges attract
⚪➕Charged objects attract neutral objects

Three ways to charge an object:

🧶Friction: rubbing transfers electrons (balloon on hair)
🤝Contact (conduction): touching a charged object shares charge; same sign
🧲Induction: a charged object brought near rearranges charges without touching; opposite sign when grounded

The electrostatic series lists materials from those that hold electrons weakly to strongly. When rubbed, the one lower on the list gains electrons and becomes negative.

Static electricity stays in one place until discharged (a spark or lightning). Current electricity is a continuous flow of electrons in a closed circuit, measured in amperes (A). Grounding safely sends extra charge into the Earth.

Remember: only electrons move; like charges repel, opposites attract.

Key words

  • electrostatic series: a list ranking materials by how strongly they hold electrons
  • induction: charging without touching by rearranging charges
  • grounding: connecting to the Earth to remove extra charge
  • ampere: the unit of electric current

See real-life examples

Lightning rods

Tall buildings like the CN Tower have lightning rods that safely carry lightning to the ground. The CN Tower is struck dozens of times a year.

Gas station safety

Static sparks can ignite gas vapour, so people should touch metal on the car before touching the gas nozzle.

Photocopiers

Photocopiers and laser printers use static charge to stick toner powder to paper.
