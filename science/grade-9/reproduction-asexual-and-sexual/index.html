Reproduction: asexual and sexual

How living things make new living things: copying cells with mitosis, mixing genes with meiosis, and how humans develop.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 9 science.

Learn what you need to know

Cells come from cells. Before a cell divides, it copies its DNA, the instructions found in its chromosomes. Human body cells have 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs.

🔁Asexual reproduction: one parent; offspring are identical copies (clones).
🔀Sexual reproduction: two parents; offspring get a mix of genes and are all different.

Kinds of asexual reproduction: binary fission (bacteria split in two), budding (yeast and hydra grow a bud), spores (mushrooms and moulds), and vegetative reproduction (strawberry runners, potato tubers, cuttings). It is fast and needs no partner, but all the offspring can be wiped out by the same disease.

Mitosis makes two identical cells for growth and repair. Meiosis makes sex cells (sperm and egg) with only 23 chromosomes. At fertilization, sperm and egg join to make a zygote with 46 chromosomes. Sexual reproduction is slower but gives variation, which helps a species survive change.

Human development: the zygote divides into an embryo, then a fetus. The placenta passes food and oxygen from the mother. A baby is usually born after about 38 to 40 weeks. Plants reproduce sexually too: pollen carries the male cells to the flower's ovule.

Remember: mitosis = 2 identical cells (46 chromosomes in humans); meiosis = 4 different sex cells (23 chromosomes).

Key words

  • mitosis: cell division that makes two identical cells
  • meiosis: cell division that makes sex cells with half the chromosomes
  • zygote: the first cell made when sperm and egg join
  • clone: a living thing that is an exact genetic copy of its parent

See real-life examples

Garden cuttings

A piece of a spider plant or geranium in water grows roots: a clone of the parent.

Bread mould

Mould spreads on old bread by spores.

Siblings

Brothers and sisters look different because sexual reproduction mixes genes.
