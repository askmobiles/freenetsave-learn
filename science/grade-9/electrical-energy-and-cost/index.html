Electrical energy and cost

Power, kilowatt-hours, calculating the cost of electricity, and efficiency. Grade 9 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 9 · Physics: Electricity

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Power is the rate at which energy is used, measured in watts (W). 1000 W = 1 kilowatt (kW).

power (W) = voltage (V) × current (A)

Electrical energy used depends on power and time:

energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h)

Utilities charge by the kilowatt-hour (kWh).

cost = energy (kWh) × price per kWh

Example: a 1500 W heater (1.5 kW) running 4 hours uses 1.5 × 4 = 6 kWh. At $0.10 per kWh, that costs $0.60.

Ontario uses time-of-use pricing: electricity costs more at peak times (weekday daytime) and less overnight and on weekends. EnerGuide and ENERGY STAR labels help compare appliance efficiency. Efficiency = useful energy out ÷ energy in × 100%.

Remember: kW × h = kWh, and kWh × price = cost.

Key words

  • power: the rate of using energy, in watts
  • kilowatt-hour: energy used by a 1 kW device in 1 hour
  • time-of-use pricing: electricity prices that change with the time of day
  • ENERGY STAR: a label for very energy-efficient products

See real-life examples

Laundry at night

In Ontario, running the dryer at night or on the weekend usually costs less because of time-of-use pricing.

Phantom power

TVs, game consoles and chargers can use power even when "off". This standby power can add up over a year.

LED savings

A 10 W LED gives about as much light as a 60 W old bulb, using about one sixth of the energy.
