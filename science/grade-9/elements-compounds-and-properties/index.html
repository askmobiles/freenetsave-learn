Elements, compounds and properties

Classifying matter, physical and chemical properties, and WHMIS safety. Grade 9 science.

Learn what you need to know

Matter can be classified:

⚪Element: one kind of atom (copper, oxygen)
🧪Compound: two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio (H₂O, NaCl, CO₂)
🥗Mechanical mixture: parts visible
🧂Solution: one part dissolved, looks uniform

Physical properties can be observed without changing the substance: colour, lustre, state, melting point, boiling point, density, hardness, solubility, conductivity, malleability (can be hammered thin) and ductility (can be drawn into wire).

Chemical properties describe how a substance reacts to form new substances: flammability, reaction with acid, reaction with water or oxygen, and toxicity.

In the lab, WHMIS (Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System) symbols warn about hazards: flammable, corrosive, toxic, oxidizer, compressed gas and more. Always read labels and the Safety Data Sheet (SDS).

Remember: physical properties describe; chemical properties predict reactions.

Key words

  • compound: two or more elements chemically combined
  • physical property: a feature observed without changing the substance
  • chemical property: how a substance reacts to form new substances
  • WHMIS: Canada's system of hazard labels for chemicals

See real-life examples

Sodium chloride

Sodium is a reactive metal and chlorine is a poisonous gas, but combined they make table salt. A compound has new properties.

Copper wire

Copper is ductile (drawn into wire) and conducts electricity, which is why house wiring is copper.

WHMIS labels

Bleach shows the corrosive symbol. Never mix bleach with ammonia cleaners, which makes a toxic gas.
