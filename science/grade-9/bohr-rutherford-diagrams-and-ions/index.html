Bohr-Rutherford diagrams and ions

Learn what you need to know

A Bohr-Rutherford diagram shows the nucleus (protons and neutrons) in the middle and the electrons in shells around it.

Fill shells from the inside out: up to 2 electrons in the first shell, 8 in the second, 8 in the third (for the first 20 elements). Sodium (11 electrons) is 2, 8, 1.

➕Protons = atomic number
⚪Neutrons = mass number − atomic number
➖Electrons = protons (in a neutral atom)
🔵Valence electrons = electrons in the outer shell

Atoms want a full outer shell, like the noble gases. Metals lose electrons and become positive ions (cations): Na → Na⁺. Non-metals gain electrons and become negative ions (anions): Cl → Cl⁻.

Remember: the group (column) tells you the charge: group 1 → +1, group 2 → +2, group 16 → −2, group 17 → −1.

Key words

  • valence electrons: electrons in the outermost shell
  • ion: an atom that has gained or lost electrons and has a charge
  • cation: a positive ion, formed when an atom loses electrons
  • anion: a negative ion, formed when an atom gains electrons

See real-life examples

Table salt

Salt is made of sodium ions (Na⁺) and chloride ions (Cl⁻) held together by their opposite charges.

Neon signs

Neon already has a full outer shell (2, 8), so it does not react. That is why noble gases are safe in lights.

Sports drinks

Electrolytes in sports drinks are ions such as Na⁺, K⁺ and Cl⁻ that help nerves and muscles work.
