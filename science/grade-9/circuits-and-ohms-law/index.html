Circuits and Ohm's law

Current, voltage, resistance, Ohm's law and series versus parallel circuits. Grade 9 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 9 · Physics: Electricity

Learn what you need to know

Three key quantities describe a circuit:

🔋Voltage (V): the energy given to each charge; measured in volts with a voltmeter connected in parallel
➡️Current (I): the flow of charge; measured in amperes (A) with an ammeter connected in series
🧱Resistance (R): how much a part opposes current; measured in ohms (Ω)

Ohm's law: V = I × R

Example: a 12 V battery and a 4 Ω resistor give a current of I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.

In a series circuit there is one path: the current is the same everywhere, the voltages add up to the source voltage, and total resistance R = R₁ + R₂ + ….

In a parallel circuit there are several paths: each branch gets the full source voltage, and the branch currents add up to the total current. Adding branches lowers total resistance.

Remember: V = IR; series = same current; parallel = same voltage.

Key words

  • voltage: the electrical energy per unit of charge
  • resistance: opposition to the flow of current
  • ohm: the unit of resistance
  • load: a device that uses electrical energy

See real-life examples

House wiring

Homes are wired in parallel so each appliance gets 120 V and works on its own.

Breakers

Too many appliances on one parallel circuit draw too much current. A circuit breaker trips to prevent overheating.

Thin wires

A long, thin wire has more resistance than a short, thick one. That is why extension cords for heaters must be thick.
