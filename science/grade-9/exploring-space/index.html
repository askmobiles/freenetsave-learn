Learn what you need to know

We explore space with telescopes and spacecraft. Optical telescopes see visible light; radio telescopes detect radio waves; space telescopes like the James Webb Space Telescope (launched 2021, with a Canadian guidance sensor) see infrared light above Earth's blurry atmosphere.

Satellites orbit Earth for communication, GPS, weather forecasting and Earth observation. Probes and rovers explore other planets.

🦾Canadarm, Canadarm2 and Dextre: Canadian robotics on the Space Shuttle and ISS
👩‍🚀Canadian astronauts: Marc Garneau (first, 1984), Roberta Bondar (first Canadian woman, 1992), Chris Hadfield (ISS commander, 2013)
🌙Artemis II (April 2026): Jeremy Hansen became the first Canadian to travel around the Moon
🛰️RADARSAT satellites watch Canada's ice, forests and floods

Space is dangerous: no air, extreme temperatures, radiation, and microgravity that weakens bones and muscles. Space junk is a growing problem.

Space research gives us spinoffs: better satellites, medical imaging, water filters, cordless tools and more.

Remember: telescopes, satellites, probes and people all explore space, and Canada plays a big role.

Key words

  • satellite: an object that orbits a planet
  • microgravity: the near-weightless condition in orbit
  • space probe: an uncrewed spacecraft sent to explore
  • spinoff: an everyday product that came from space research

See real-life examples

Chris Hadfield

Chris Hadfield commanded the ISS in 2013 and shared life in space with millions through videos and music.

James Webb

The James Webb Space Telescope uses a Canadian Fine Guidance Sensor to point precisely at distant galaxies.

Artemis II

In April 2026, Canadian astronaut Jeremy Hansen flew around the Moon on Artemis II, the first crewed Moon mission since 1972.
