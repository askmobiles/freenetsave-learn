Atoms and the periodic table

Subatomic particles, atomic number, mass number and Bohr-Rutherford diagrams. Grade 9 science.

Learn what you need to know

An atom has a tiny, dense nucleus containing protons (+) and neutrons (no charge), surrounded by electrons (−) in energy levels (shells).

🔢Atomic number = number of protons (and electrons in a neutral atom)
⚖️Mass number = protons + neutrons
➖Neutrons = mass number − atomic number

Example: sodium has atomic number 11 and mass number 23, so it has 11 protons, 11 electrons and 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

In a Bohr-Rutherford diagram, electrons fill shells: up to 2 in the first shell, 8 in the second, 8 in the third (for the first 20 elements). Electrons in the outer shell are valence electrons; they decide how an element reacts.

Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons. Group 1 (alkali metals) have 1 and are very reactive; group 17 (halogens) have 7; group 18 (noble gases) have a full shell and are stable.

Remember: protons = atomic number; neutrons = mass number − atomic number; shells hold 2, 8, 8.

Key words

  • atomic number: the number of protons in an atom
  • mass number: the number of protons plus neutrons
  • valence electron: an electron in the outer shell
  • isotope: atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons

See real-life examples

Rutherford in Montreal

Ernest Rutherford did important atomic research at McGill University in Montreal before discovering the nucleus.

Sodium and water

Sodium has 1 valence electron and reacts violently with water. That is why it is stored in oil.

Neon signs

Neon has a full outer shell, so it does not react. Electricity makes it glow red-orange in signs.
