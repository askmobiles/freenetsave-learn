Ecosystems and energy flow

Learn what you need to know

An ecosystem includes all biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) factors in an area. Energy enters almost every ecosystem as sunlight, captured by producers through photosynthesis.

🌱Producers (autotrophs): make their own food
🐇Primary consumers: herbivores
🦊Secondary consumers: eat herbivores
🦅Tertiary consumers: eat other carnivores
🍄Decomposers and detritivores: break down dead matter

Energy flows one way through an ecosystem: Sun → producers → consumers → lost as heat. Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed to the next, because organisms use energy for life processes (cellular respiration) and lose it as heat.

Matter, unlike energy, is recycled. Decomposers return carbon, nitrogen and other nutrients to the soil and air.

Ecologists show this with pyramids of energy, numbers and biomass. Food webs with many links are more stable than simple food chains.

Remember: energy flows one way and is lost as heat; matter cycles.

Key words

  • autotroph: an organism that makes its own food
  • heterotroph: an organism that must eat other organisms
  • trophic level: a feeding level in an ecosystem
  • biomass: the total mass of living things at a level

See real-life examples

Boreal forest

Canada's boreal forest: spruce and moss (producers) → snowshoe hare → lynx. Lynx numbers rise and fall with hare numbers.

Deep-sea vents

Some deep-ocean ecosystems get energy from chemicals, not sunlight. Bacteria there are producers using chemosynthesis.

Eating lower

About 10 times more people can be fed by eating grain directly than by feeding that grain to animals.
