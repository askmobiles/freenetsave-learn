Physical and chemical changes

Evidence of chemical change, word equations and conservation of mass. Grade 9 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 9 · Chemistry: The Nature of Matter

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

In a physical change, the substance stays the same (melting, dissolving, cutting). In a chemical change (chemical reaction), new substances with new properties form.

🎨Colour change
🫧Gas produced (bubbles)
🌡️Energy released or absorbed (heat, light)
🧊Precipitate (a solid) forms
👃New odour

These are evidence of a chemical change. A reaction is written as a word equation: reactants → products.

methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

The law of conservation of mass (Antoine Lavoisier): in a chemical reaction, the total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants. Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed. If a gas escapes, an open container seems to lose mass, but the mass is still conserved.

Reactions can be exothermic (release energy, like burning) or endothermic (absorb energy, like an instant cold pack).

Remember: reactants → products, and mass is conserved.

Key words

  • reactant: a substance that changes in a chemical reaction
  • product: a new substance made in a chemical reaction
  • exothermic: releasing energy
  • endothermic: absorbing energy

See real-life examples

Cold packs

An instant cold pack gets cold when you squeeze it because the reaction inside is endothermic.

Campfire

Burning wood is exothermic, releasing heat and light. The ash weighs less because gases escape into the air.

Statue of Liberty

The copper Statue of Liberty turned green from a slow chemical reaction with air and water.
