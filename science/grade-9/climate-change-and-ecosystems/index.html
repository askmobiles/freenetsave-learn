Climate change and ecosystems

Learn what you need to know

Sunlight warms the Earth. The Earth gives off heat (infrared), and greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄) and water vapour trap some of it. This natural greenhouse effect keeps Earth about 33 °C warmer than it would be.

Burning fossil fuels, cutting forests and raising cattle add extra greenhouse gases. More heat is trapped, so average temperatures rise. This is climate change.

🧊Arctic sea ice and glaciers melt
🌊Sea level rises
🔥More heat waves and wildfires
🐻‍❄️Polar bears lose hunting ice
🦟Species move north; ticks spread
🪸Warmer, more acidic oceans harm coral

Mitigation means reducing emissions (clean electricity, electric transit, saving energy, planting trees). Adaptation means preparing for change (flood barriers, heat plans, drought-resistant crops).

Weather vs climate: weather is day to day; climate is the average over 30 years or more.

Key words

  • greenhouse effect: gases in the atmosphere trapping heat near Earth
  • greenhouse gas: a gas such as CO₂ or methane that traps heat
  • climate: the long-term average weather of a region
  • mitigation: actions that reduce greenhouse gas emissions

See real-life examples

Canada's North

The Arctic is warming about three to four times faster than the global average, melting permafrost and sea ice.

Wildfire smoke

Hotter, drier summers have made wildfire seasons longer in Canada, sending smoke to cities far away.

Your choices

Taking transit, eating less beef and switching off lights all cut greenhouse gases.
