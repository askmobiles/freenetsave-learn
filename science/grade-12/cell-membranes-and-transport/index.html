Cell membranes and transport

The fluid mosaic model, diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion, active transport and bulk transport.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

The cell membrane follows the fluid mosaic model: a phospholipid bilayer (hydrophilic heads outside, hydrophobic tails inside) with proteins floating in it. Cholesterol keeps fluidity steady, and glycoproteins and glycolipids act as markers for cell recognition.

➡️Simple diffusion: small non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂) move down their concentration gradient
💧Osmosis: water moves across a membrane from low solute to high solute concentration
🚪Facilitated diffusion: channel or carrier proteins help ions and glucose move down the gradient, no ATP
⚡Active transport: carrier proteins pump substances against the gradient using ATP (sodium–potassium pump)

Tonicity: in a hypotonic solution water enters the cell (animal cells may burst; plant cells become turgid). In a hypertonic solution water leaves (cells shrink; plant cells plasmolyze). In an isotonic solution there is no net movement.

Bulk transport moves large particles in vesicles: endocytosis brings material in (phagocytosis = "cell eating", pinocytosis = "cell drinking", receptor-mediated endocytosis) and exocytosis releases material out, as when nerve cells release neurotransmitters.

The sodium–potassium pump moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in for each ATP, keeping the membrane potential needed by nerve and muscle cells.

Remember: passive = down the gradient, no ATP; active = against the gradient, uses ATP.

Key words

  • fluid mosaic model: a bilayer of phospholipids with proteins that can move within it
  • osmosis: diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane
  • hypotonic: having a lower solute concentration than the cell
  • endocytosis: taking material into a cell by folding the membrane into a vesicle

See real-life examples

Salt on roads and lawns

Too much road salt makes soil water hypertonic, so roots lose water and grass dies near Canadian roads.

IV drips

Hospital IV saline is isotonic (0.9% NaCl) so red blood cells neither swell nor shrink.

White blood cells

Phagocytes swallow bacteria by phagocytosis, a type of endocytosis.
