The nervous and endocrine systems

Neurons, nerve impulses, synapses, the brain, reflexes and hormones.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

The nervous system sends fast electrical messages. The central nervous system (CNS) is the brain and spinal cord; the peripheral nervous system (PNS) is all the nerves outside them.

🧠Neuron parts: dendrites receive, cell body, axon sends, myelin speeds it up
⚡Resting potential about −70 mV; action potential: Na⁺ rushes in, then K⁺ flows out
🔁All-or-none: a neuron fires fully or not at all
🧪Synapse: neurotransmitters (like acetylcholine) cross the gap

A reflex arc (sensory neuron → interneuron in the spinal cord → motor neuron) lets you pull your hand from a hot stove before your brain even feels the pain.

The autonomic system runs automatic jobs: sympathetic = "fight or flight" (faster heart), parasympathetic = "rest and digest".

The endocrine system sends slower chemical messages (hormones) through the blood. The pituitary is the "master gland", controlled by the hypothalamus. Others: thyroid (metabolism), adrenal glands (adrenaline, cortisol), pancreas (insulin), and the ovaries and testes.

Remember: nerves = fast and short; hormones = slower and longer-lasting.

Key words

  • neuron: a nerve cell
  • action potential: the electrical signal that travels along a neuron
  • synapse: the gap between two neurons
  • hormone: a chemical messenger carried in the blood

A hot stove

Before a big exam

Adrenaline makes your heart race and hands sweat: the sympathetic "fight or flight" response.

Concussions in hockey

A hit to the head can injure brain tissue, which is why Canadian sports now have strict concussion rules.
