Learn what you need to know

Organic compounds are based on carbon. Carbon forms four bonds, so it can make chains, branches and rings. Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen.

➖Alkanes: single bonds only, CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (methane, ethane, propane)
🟰Alkenes: at least one C=C double bond, CₙH₂ₙ (ethene)
≡Alkynes: a C≡C triple bond, CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (ethyne)
⬡Aromatics: a benzene ring (C₆H₆)

Prefixes give the number of carbons: meth 1, eth 2, prop 3, but 4, pent 5, hex 6. Isomers have the same formula but different structures.

🍷Alcohol: –OH (ethanol)
🍋Carboxylic acid: –COOH (ethanoic acid = vinegar)
🍌Ester: –COO– (fruity smells)
🐟Amine: –NH₂; Ketone: C=O in the middle; Aldehyde: C=O at the end

Reactions: addition (alkenes add H₂, Br₂, H₂O across the double bond), substitution (alkanes swap H for a halogen), esterification (acid + alcohol → ester + water), combustion (hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O) and polymerization (many monomers → a polymer such as polyethylene).

Remember: -ane single, -ene double, -yne triple. Acid + alcohol → ester + water.

Key words

  • hydrocarbon: a compound of only carbon and hydrogen
  • functional group: a group of atoms that gives a compound its reactions
  • isomer: same formula, different structure
  • polymer: a large molecule made of many repeating units

See real-life examples

Barbecue fuel

Propane (C₃H₈) is an alkane; it burns with oxygen to make carbon dioxide and water.

Fruit flavours

Many candy flavours are esters, such as isoamyl acetate, which smells like banana.

Plastic bags

Polyethylene is made by joining thousands of ethene molecules in addition polymerization.
