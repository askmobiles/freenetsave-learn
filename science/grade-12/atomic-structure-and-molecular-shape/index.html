Atomic structure and molecular shape

Learn what you need to know

In the quantum mechanical model, electrons occupy orbitals, regions where they are likely to be found. Each orbital holds up to 2 electrons with opposite spins.

🔵s: 1 orbital, 2 electrons (sphere)
🎀p: 3 orbitals, 6 electrons (dumbbells)
🍀d: 5 orbitals, 10 electrons
🌸f: 7 orbitals, 14 electrons

Electron configurations fill lowest energy first (aufbau): 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d… For example O = 1s² 2s² 2p⁴. Hund's rule: fill each orbital in a set singly before pairing.

VSEPR theory: electron pairs around a central atom spread out as far as possible. 4 pairs, no lone pairs → tetrahedral (CH₄, 109.5°); 3 bonds + 1 lone pair → trigonal pyramidal (NH₃); 2 bonds + 2 lone pairs → bent (H₂O); 2 pairs → linear (CO₂, 180°); 3 pairs → trigonal planar (BF₃, 120°).

A molecule is polar if it has polar bonds that do not cancel (H₂O); CO₂ has polar bonds but is non-polar because it is symmetrical. Intermolecular forces: London dispersion (all molecules), dipole–dipole (polar), and hydrogen bonding (H on N, O or F) — the strongest, which explains water's high boiling point.

Remember: s2 p6 d10 f14. Shape depends on bonds AND lone pairs.

Key words

  • orbital: a region where an electron is likely to be found
  • electron configuration: how electrons are arranged in orbitals
  • VSEPR: theory that electron pairs repel to set a molecule's shape
  • hydrogen bond: a strong attraction between H on N, O or F and another N, O or F

See real-life examples

Ice floats

Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules in an open pattern in ice, so ice is less dense than liquid water and lakes freeze from the top.

Microwave ovens

Microwaves make polar water molecules spin back and forth, which heats food.

Fireworks

Electrons jumping between energy levels in metal atoms give fireworks their bright colours.
