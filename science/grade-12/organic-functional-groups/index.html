Organic functional groups

Alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, ethers, amines and amides: naming and properties.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

A functional group is the reactive part of an organic molecule. R stands for the rest of the carbon chain.

🍷Alcohol R–OH: suffix -ol (ethanol). Polar, H-bonds, higher boiling point
🍋Carboxylic acid R–COOH: -oic acid (ethanoic acid = vinegar). Weak acids
🌸Ester R–COO–R′: -yl -oate (ethyl ethanoate). Fruity smells; acid + alcohol
🐟Amine R–NH₂: -amine (methanamine). Weak bases; fishy smell

Aldehydes (R–CHO, -al, methanal) have C=O at the end of the chain; ketones (R–CO–R′, -one, propanone = acetone) have C=O inside the chain. Ethers (R–O–R′, methoxyethane) have an O between two carbons. Amides (R–CONH₂, -amide) join an acid and an amine; they link amino acids in proteins.

Properties: groups with O–H or N–H can hydrogen bond, so they have higher boiling points and dissolve better in water than hydrocarbons of similar size. Solubility drops as the carbon chain gets longer (the non-polar part grows).

Alcohols are classed as primary (OH carbon joined to 1 other C), secondary (2) or tertiary (3). Primary alcohols oxidize to aldehydes then carboxylic acids; secondary to ketones; tertiary do not oxidize easily.

Remember: -ol, -al, -one, -oic acid, -oate, -amine, -amide.

Key words

  • functional group: the reactive group of atoms in an organic molecule
  • carbonyl group: C=O, found in aldehydes and ketones
  • ester: a compound made from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol
  • hydrogen bonding: a strong attraction between an H on O or N and another O or N

See real-life examples

Nail polish remover

Acetone (propanone) is a ketone that dissolves nail polish.

Fruit flavours

Esters give the smell of bananas (pentyl ethanoate) and pineapple (ethyl butanoate).

Hand sanitizer

Most sanitizers use ethanol or isopropanol (propan-2-ol), both alcohols.
