Nuclear physics and energy

Learn what you need to know

A nucleus has protons and neutrons (nucleons) held together by the strong nuclear force, which is stronger than electric repulsion but acts only over tiny distances. Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

αAlpha decay: emits a helium nucleus (2p + 2n); mass number −4, atomic number −2; stopped by paper
βBeta-minus decay: a neutron becomes a proton and emits an electron; atomic number +1; stopped by aluminium
γGamma: high-energy photon, no change in mass or atomic number; needs thick lead or concrete
⏳Half-life: time for half of a radioactive sample to decay

The mass of a nucleus is a little less than the mass of its separate nucleons. This mass defect becomes the binding energy by E = mc². Iron-56 has the highest binding energy per nucleon, so heavy nuclei release energy by splitting and light nuclei by joining.

Fission: a neutron splits a heavy nucleus such as U-235 into two smaller nuclei, 2–3 neutrons and a lot of energy; the neutrons can start a chain reaction. Fusion: light nuclei like hydrogen join to make helium; it powers the Sun.

Ontario's CANDU reactors (Pickering, Darlington, Bruce) use natural uranium and heavy water to slow neutrons and cool the core. Nuclear power makes very little CO₂ but produces long-lived radioactive waste that must be stored safely.

Remember: in decay equations, mass numbers and charges must balance on both sides.

Key words

  • isotope: atoms of an element with different numbers of neutrons
  • half-life: the time for half of a radioactive sample to decay
  • mass defect: the missing mass when nucleons bind, turned into energy
  • chain reaction: neutrons from one fission cause more fissions

See real-life examples

Ontario's electricity

About half of Ontario's electricity comes from CANDU nuclear reactors.

Medical isotopes

Canada has been a major supplier of medical isotopes like cobalt-60 used in cancer treatment.

Carbon dating

Carbon-14's half-life of about 5730 years lets scientists date wood and bones.
