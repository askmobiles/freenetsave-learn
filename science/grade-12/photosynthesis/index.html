Photosynthesis

Learn what you need to know

Photosynthesis stores light energy in sugar: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. It happens in chloroplasts: thylakoid membranes (stacked in grana) surround the lumen, and the fluid around them is the stroma.

Pigments: chlorophyll a (main pigment), chlorophyll b and carotenoids (accessory pigments). They absorb mainly blue-violet and red light and reflect green. An absorption spectrum shows which colours a pigment absorbs; an action spectrum shows which colours drive photosynthesis.

🔆Photosystem II (P680): light excites electrons; water is split (photolysis) → O₂, H⁺, e⁻
⛓️Electron transport (plastoquinone → cytochrome b₆f → plastocyanin) pumps H⁺ into the lumen
🔆Photosystem I (P700): electrons re-energized → ferredoxin → NADP⁺ reductase makes NADPH
⚙️ATP synthase: H⁺ flows lumen → stroma and makes ATP (photophosphorylation)

Non-cyclic electron flow makes ATP, NADPH and O₂. Cyclic electron flow uses only photosystem I and makes extra ATP but no NADPH or O₂, balancing what the Calvin cycle needs.

1️⃣Fixation: rubisco joins CO₂ to RuBP (5C) → 2 × 3-PGA (3C)
2️⃣Reduction: ATP and NADPH turn 3-PGA into G3P
3️⃣Regeneration: ATP rebuilds RuBP from G3P
🧮3 CO₂ → 1 net G3P, using 9 ATP and 6 NADPH; one glucose needs 6 turns: 18 ATP, 12 NADPH

Photorespiration: in hot, dry weather stomata close, CO₂ falls and rubisco grabs O₂ instead, wasting energy. C₄ plants (corn, sugar cane) first fix CO₂ with PEP carboxylase in mesophyll cells and pass it to bundle-sheath cells. CAM plants (cacti, pineapple) open stomata at night and store CO₂ as acids for daytime.

Limiting factors: light intensity, CO₂ concentration and temperature. Raising one helps only until another factor limits the rate.

Remember: light reactions (thylakoid) make ATP, NADPH and O₂; the Calvin cycle (stroma) uses them to fix CO₂. Rubisco is the key enzyme.

Key words

  • photolysis: splitting water using light energy
  • rubisco: the enzyme that fixes CO₂ in the Calvin cycle
  • photophosphorylation: making ATP using light energy and chemiosmosis
  • photorespiration: rubisco fixing O₂ instead of CO₂, wasting energy

See real-life examples

Ontario corn fields

Corn is a C₄ plant, which helps it grow fast in hot Ontario summers.

Greenhouses

Some Ontario greenhouse growers add CO₂ to the air to speed up photosynthesis in tomatoes and peppers.

Fall colours

When chlorophyll breaks down in autumn, the yellow and orange carotenoids show in maple and birch leaves.
