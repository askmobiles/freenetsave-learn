The wave nature of light

Learn what you need to know

Huygens' principle: every point on a wavefront acts as a source of new wavelets. This explains diffraction: waves spread out after passing an opening. Diffraction is strongest when the opening is about the same size as the wavelength.

Interference: when waves overlap, crests meeting crests add (constructive, bright) and crests meeting troughs cancel (destructive, dark). Bright fringes happen where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths (mλ); dark fringes where it is a half number ((m + ½)λ).

🔦Young's double slit (1801): fringe spacing Δy = λL ÷ d
🌈Diffraction grating: d sinθ = mλ, very sharp bright lines, used in spectrometers
🔍Single slit: wide central bright band; first dark band at w sinθ = λ
🫧Thin films (soap, oil): reflections from top and bottom interfere, making colours

Polarization: light is a transverse wave, so its vibrations can be filtered to one direction. Polarizing sunglasses block glare from water and roads, which is partly polarized by reflection. LCD screens also use polarizers.

These effects showed that light behaves as a wave. Maxwell later showed light is an electromagnetic wave travelling at c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. Wavelengths of visible light run from about 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red).

Remember: bright = path difference mλ; dark = (m + ½)λ. Δy = λL/d; grating d sinθ = mλ.

Key words

  • interference: the combining of overlapping waves
  • diffraction: the spreading of waves past edges or through openings
  • path difference: the difference in distance travelled by two waves
  • polarization: limiting a transverse wave's vibrations to one direction

See real-life examples

Soap bubbles

Swirling colours come from thin-film interference as the bubble's thickness changes.

CDs and DVDs

The closely spaced tracks act like a diffraction grating, splitting light into a rainbow.

Polarized sunglasses

They block horizontally polarized glare from lakes and wet roads, helpful for fishing and driving.
