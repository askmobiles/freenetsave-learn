Cellular respiration

Learn what you need to know

Aerobic cellular respiration releases energy from glucose: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy. It is an oxidation: glucose loses electrons (carried by NAD⁺ and FAD) and oxygen gains them.

1️⃣Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate; net 2 ATP + 2 NADH; no O₂ needed
2️⃣Pyruvate oxidation (matrix): each pyruvate → acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH
3️⃣Krebs cycle (matrix): per glucose, 2 turns make 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂ and 4 CO₂
4️⃣Electron transport chain + chemiosmosis (inner membrane): most of the ATP

In glycolysis and the Krebs cycle, ATP is made by substrate-level phosphorylation (a phosphate is moved straight onto ADP). In the ETC, ATP is made by oxidative phosphorylation.

Electron transport chain: NADH gives electrons to complex I, FADH₂ to complex II. Electrons pass through ubiquinone (Q), complex III, cytochrome c and complex IV to oxygen, the final electron acceptor, forming water. The energy pumps H⁺ into the intermembrane space.

Chemiosmosis (Peter Mitchell): H⁺ flow back into the matrix through ATP synthase, which spins and makes ATP. Each NADH gives about 3 ATP and each FADH₂ about 2 ATP (newer estimates: 2.5 and 1.5), so one glucose gives about 36–38 ATP (about 30–32 by newer counts).

🍞Alcohol fermentation (yeast): pyruvate → ethanol + CO₂
💪Lactic acid fermentation (muscle): pyruvate → lactate
🔁Fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue; only 2 ATP per glucose
☠️Cyanide blocks complex IV; DNP lets H⁺ leak, so energy becomes heat (an uncoupler)

Fats and proteins can also be used: fatty acids are broken into acetyl-CoA by β-oxidation, and amino acids are deaminated (the amino group removed) before entering the Krebs cycle.

Remember: glycolysis → pyruvate oxidation → Krebs → ETC. O₂ is the final electron acceptor; ATP synthase uses the H⁺ gradient.

Key words

  • substrate-level phosphorylation: making ATP by moving a phosphate directly onto ADP
  • oxidative phosphorylation: making ATP using energy from the electron transport chain
  • chemiosmosis: making ATP as H⁺ flows through ATP synthase
  • final electron acceptor: the molecule at the end of the ETC; oxygen in aerobic respiration

See real-life examples

Cyanide poisoning

Cyanide stops complex IV, so cells cannot use oxygen and ATP production collapses within minutes.

Brown fat in babies

Babies have brown fat with a protein that lets H⁺ leak back, making heat instead of ATP to keep them warm.

Marathon runners

At high effort, muscles add lactic acid fermentation for quick ATP when oxygen cannot keep up.
