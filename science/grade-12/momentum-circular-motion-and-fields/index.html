Momentum, circular motion and fields

Learn what you need to know

Momentum p = mv (kg·m/s). An impulse J = FΔt = Δp changes momentum. In a closed system, total momentum is conserved in every collision.

🎱Elastic collision: momentum and kinetic energy both conserved
🚗Inelastic collision: momentum conserved, some kinetic energy lost
🧲Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together
🛡️Airbags increase Δt, so the force is smaller for the same Δp

An object moving in a circle at steady speed still accelerates toward the centre. Centripetal acceleration a = v²/r, and the net force F = mv²/r points to the centre. There is no outward "centrifugal force" in an inertial frame.

Gravitational field: F = Gm₁m₂/r² (Newton's law of gravitation), g = GM/r². Electric field: F = kq₁q₂/r² (Coulomb's law); like charges repel. Both are inverse-square laws: double the distance → ¼ the force.

A magnetic field pushes on moving charges and currents: F = qvB sinθ or F = BIL sinθ. Use a right-hand rule for the direction. Charged particles in a uniform magnetic field move in circles.

Remember: p = mv, F = mv²/r, and inverse-square: 2× the distance gives ¼ the force.

Key words

  • momentum: mass times velocity
  • impulse: force times time, equal to the change in momentum
  • centripetal force: the net force toward the centre of a circle
  • inverse-square law: force falls with the square of distance

See real-life examples

Hockey hits

When a heavy player hits a lighter one, momentum is shared, which is why the lighter player flies back faster.

Satellites

A satellite orbits because gravity provides exactly the centripetal force needed for its speed and radius.

MRI machines

Strong magnetic fields in MRI scanners are used to make images of the inside of the body.
