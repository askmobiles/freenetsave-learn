Electrochemistry

Learn what you need to know

Oxidation is losing electrons; reduction is gaining electrons (OIL RIG). They always happen together in a redox reaction. The substance oxidized is the reducing agent; the one reduced is the oxidizing agent.

Oxidation numbers track electrons: elements alone are 0, O is usually −2, H usually +1, and the numbers in a compound add up to its charge.

🔋Galvanic (voltaic) cell: spontaneous redox makes electricity
🅰️Anode: oxidation happens here
🅲Cathode: reduction happens here ("red cat")
🌉Salt bridge: lets ions move to keep charges balanced

Cell voltage: E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode, using standard reduction potentials. A positive E°cell means the reaction is spontaneous.

Electrolysis uses electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous reaction, such as splitting water or electroplating. Corrosion (rusting) is the oxidation of iron; paint, galvanizing with zinc and sacrificial anodes protect it.

Remember: OIL RIG. An Ox, Red Cat: oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode.

Key words

  • oxidation: losing electrons
  • reduction: gaining electrons
  • anode: the electrode where oxidation happens
  • electrolysis: using electricity to drive a chemical reaction

See real-life examples

Phone batteries

Lithium-ion batteries are galvanic cells that are recharged by running them as electrolytic cells.

Salted roads

Road salt in Canadian winters speeds up the rusting of cars, a redox reaction.

Gold-plated jewellery

A thin layer of gold is put on cheaper metal by electroplating.
