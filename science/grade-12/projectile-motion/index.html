Projectile motion

Motion in two dimensions: launching things sideways and at angles, and vector components.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

A projectile moves under gravity alone (ignoring air). Split its motion into two independent parts: horizontal and vertical.

➡️Horizontal: no acceleration, steady speed, x = vₓt
⬇️Vertical: accelerates down at 9.8 m/s², like a dropped ball
⏱️Time links them: both parts take the same time
🎯The path is a parabola

A vector launched at angle θ with speed v has components vₓ = v cos θ and v_y = v sin θ. At the top of the path the vertical speed is 0, but the horizontal speed is unchanged.

Useful equations (vertical, with a = −9.8 m/s²): v = v₀ + at, d = v₀t + ½at², v² = v₀² + 2ad. On level ground the range is greatest at a 45° launch angle, and angles that add to 90° (such as 30° and 60°) give the same range.

A ball dropped and a ball thrown sideways from the same height land at the same time, because their vertical motions are the same.

Remember: horizontal = steady speed; vertical = falls at 9.8 m/s². Use time to connect them.

Key words

  • projectile: an object moving under gravity only after being launched
  • component: one part of a vector, like its horizontal part
  • range: how far a projectile travels horizontally
  • trajectory: the path a projectile follows

See real-life examples

Basketball shots

A free throw follows a parabola; players adjust the angle and speed so the path ends in the hoop.

Water from a hose

The stream of water traces a parabola; aiming near 45° sends it farthest.

Ski jumping

Jumpers leave the ramp moving sideways and fall at the same rate as anything dropped.
