The kidneys and excretion

How the nephron filters blood: filtration, reabsorption, secretion, water balance, ADH and kidney problems.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

The excretory system removes wastes such as urea (made in the liver from extra amino acids), extra water and salts. Path: kidneys → ureters → bladder → urethra. Each kidney has about one million nephrons.

1️⃣Filtration: blood pressure pushes water, glucose, salts and urea from the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule (not cells or proteins)
2️⃣Reabsorption: the proximal tubule takes back glucose, amino acids, most salts and water
3️⃣Loop of Henle: makes the medulla salty so water can be drawn out later
4️⃣Secretion and collecting duct: H⁺, K⁺ and drugs are added; ADH controls how much water leaves the collecting duct

ADH (from the pituitary) makes the distal tubule and collecting duct more permeable to water, so more water is kept and urine is concentrated. Alcohol and caffeine block ADH, so you make more urine. Aldosterone makes the kidney keep Na⁺, and water follows.

The kidneys also balance blood pH (secreting H⁺, keeping bicarbonate) and blood pressure. Problems: kidney stones, diabetes (glucose in urine), kidney failure treated by dialysis or a transplant.

Remember: filter → reabsorb → secrete → concentrate. Glucose in urine is a warning sign of diabetes.

Key words

  • nephron: the filtering unit of the kidney
  • glomerulus: a ball of capillaries where blood is filtered
  • urea: a nitrogen waste made by the liver
  • dialysis: a machine treatment that filters blood when kidneys fail

See real-life examples

Organ donation

Ontario residents can register as organ donors; kidneys are the most often transplanted organ.

Urine tests

Doctors test urine for glucose, protein or blood to spot diabetes or kidney damage.

Drinking coffee

Caffeine reduces ADH, so you need the washroom more often.
