Relativity and nuclear energy

Learn what you need to know

Einstein's special relativity (1905) has two ideas: the laws of physics are the same for everyone moving at steady velocity, and the speed of light (c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s) is the same for all observers.

⏳Time dilation: moving clocks run slow
📏Length contraction: moving objects are shorter along their motion
⚡Nothing with mass can reach the speed of light
⚖️E = mc²: mass and energy are equivalent

The nucleus has protons and neutrons held by the strong nuclear force. A nucleus's mass is slightly less than the sum of its parts; this mass defect is the binding energy (E = mc²).

Radioactive decay: alpha (helium nucleus, stopped by paper), beta (electron, stopped by aluminium), gamma (high-energy light, reduced by thick lead or concrete). The half-life is the time for half of a sample to decay.

Fission splits heavy nuclei (uranium) and powers nuclear reactors, like Ontario's CANDU reactors at Pickering, Darlington and Bruce. Fusion joins light nuclei (hydrogen into helium) and powers the Sun.

Remember: fission = split (reactors); fusion = join (stars). E = mc² explains why so much energy comes from so little mass.

Key words

  • time dilation: moving clocks running slower than clocks at rest
  • half-life: the time for half of a radioactive sample to decay
  • fission: splitting a heavy nucleus into smaller ones
  • fusion: joining light nuclei into a heavier one

See real-life examples

GPS satellites

GPS clocks must be corrected for relativity every day, or your phone's location would drift by kilometres.

CANDU reactors

About half of Ontario's electricity comes from nuclear fission in CANDU reactors, a Canadian design.

Carbon dating

Scientists use carbon-14's half-life of about 5730 years to find the age of old bones and wood.
