Organic reactions and polymers

Learn what you need to know

🔥Combustion: hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O (+ energy)
➕Addition: atoms add across a C=C or C≡C (hydrogenation, halogenation, hydration)
🔄Substitution: one atom swaps for another, e.g. alkane + Cl₂ → chloroalkane + HCl (needs UV light)
➖Elimination: a small molecule is removed to make a double bond, e.g. alcohol → alkene + H₂O

Addition tests for unsaturation: orange bromine water turns colourless when shaken with an alkene, but not with an alkane. Markovnikov's rule: when HX adds to an uneven alkene, the H goes to the carbon that already has more H.

Condensation joins two molecules and releases a small one (usually water): acid + alcohol → ester + water. Hydrolysis is the reverse. Oxidation adds O or removes H (alcohol → aldehyde → acid); reduction does the opposite.

Polymers are long chains of monomers. Addition polymers come from alkene monomers: ethene → polyethylene, propene → polypropylene, chloroethene → PVC. Condensation polymers release water as they form: polyesters (PET bottles), polyamides (nylon, proteins), and natural ones like starch and cellulose.

Plastics are cheap and strong but last hundreds of years. Canada has banned some single-use plastics; recycling codes (1 = PET, 2 = HDPE, 5 = PP) tell sorters what each plastic is. Biodegradable polymers such as PLA are made from plants.

Remember: addition = no by-product; condensation = water (or another small molecule) given off.

Key words

  • addition reaction: a reaction where atoms add across a multiple bond
  • condensation reaction: two molecules join and release a small molecule like water
  • monomer: a small repeating unit of a polymer
  • polyamide: a polymer joined by amide links, such as nylon

See real-life examples

Margarine

Vegetable oils are hydrogenated (an addition reaction) to make them more solid.

Water bottles

PET is a polyester; the 1 inside the recycling triangle means PET.

Nylon jackets

Nylon is a polyamide condensation polymer, first made in 1935.
