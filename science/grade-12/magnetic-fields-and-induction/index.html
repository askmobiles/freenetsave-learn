Magnetic fields and electromagnetic induction

Magnetic fields around wires and coils, right-hand rules, forces on charges and currents, motors, induction and generators.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

Magnetic field lines go from the north pole to the south pole outside a magnet. A current in a straight wire makes circular field lines around it (Oersted, 1820). A coil of wire (solenoid) makes a field like a bar magnet; an iron core makes it a strong electromagnet.

✊Right-hand rule for a wire: thumb along the current, fingers curl with the field
🧲Force on a moving charge: F = qvB sinθ (zero if moving parallel to the field)
🔌Force on a current-carrying wire: F = BIL sinθ
🔄A charged particle moving across a uniform field moves in a circle: r = mv ÷ (qB)

Motors: the force on a current in a magnetic field makes a coil turn; a commutator keeps it turning. Mass spectrometers use r = mv/(qB) to separate ions by mass.

Electromagnetic induction (Faraday): a changing magnetic field through a coil induces a voltage and current. Lenz's law: the induced current flows so its own magnetic field opposes the change that caused it. Generators, transformers, induction stoves and wireless chargers all use induction.

Earth has a magnetic field that guides compasses and shields us from charged particles from the Sun. When these particles spiral into the field near the poles, they make the northern lights (aurora borealis) seen across Canada.

Remember: moving charges make magnetic fields; magnetic fields push on moving charges; changing fields make currents.

Key words

  • solenoid: a long coil of wire that makes a magnetic field when current flows
  • electromagnetic induction: making a current by changing a magnetic field
  • Lenz's law: an induced current opposes the change that caused it
  • tesla (T): the unit of magnetic field strength

See real-life examples

Northern lights

Charged particles from the Sun spiral along Earth's magnetic field and glow high above northern Canada.

Electric cars

Electric motors turn wheels using forces on currents in magnetic fields, and act as generators when braking.

Hydro power

Ontario's hydroelectric generators at Niagara spin coils in magnetic fields to induce current.
