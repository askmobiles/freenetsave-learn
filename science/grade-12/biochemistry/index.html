Learn what you need to know

Living things are built from four main kinds of large biological molecules (macromolecules). Most are polymers: long chains made by joining small units called monomers.

🍞Carbohydrates: monomer is a monosaccharide (glucose). Energy and structure (starch, glycogen, cellulose)
🧈Lipids: fats, oils, phospholipids, steroids. Long-term energy and cell membranes
🥩Proteins: monomer is an amino acid. Enzymes, muscles, transport, antibodies
🧬Nucleic acids: monomer is a nucleotide. DNA and RNA store genetic information

Dehydration synthesis joins monomers and releases water. Hydrolysis breaks polymers apart by adding water. Digestion is hydrolysis.

A protein's shape decides its job. Its levels of structure are primary (amino acid order), secondary (α-helix, β-sheet), tertiary (3-D fold) and quaternary (several chains together). Heat, strong acids or bases can denature a protein by unfolding it.

Enzymes are protein catalysts. They lower the activation energy of a reaction. The substrate fits into the active site (induced fit). A competitive inhibitor blocks the active site; a non-competitive inhibitor binds elsewhere and changes the shape.

Remember: dehydration builds, hydrolysis breaks. Enzymes are not used up, and work best at their own ideal temperature and pH.

Key words

  • monomer: a small unit that joins with others to make a polymer
  • hydrolysis: breaking a bond by adding water
  • active site: the part of an enzyme where the substrate binds
  • denature: to lose shape and function, as when a protein is heated

See real-life examples

Lactose intolerance

People who make little lactase enzyme cannot fully break down the milk sugar lactose, so it causes stomach upset.

A fever

A very high body temperature can start to denature enzymes, which is why high fevers are dangerous.

Bread and pasta

Amylase in saliva starts breaking starch into sugars, so a cracker tastes slightly sweet if you chew it for a while.
