The nervous system

Learn what you need to know

The nervous system is split into the CNS (brain and spinal cord) and the PNS. The PNS has a somatic part (voluntary skeletal muscles) and an autonomic part: sympathetic ("fight or flight") and parasympathetic ("rest and digest").

A neuron has dendrites (receive), a cell body, an axon (sends) and axon terminals. A myelin sheath made by Schwann cells insulates the axon; the gaps (nodes of Ranvier) let the impulse jump (saltatory conduction), which is much faster.

🔋Resting potential: about −70 mV, kept by the Na⁺/K⁺ pump (more Na⁺ outside, more K⁺ inside)
⚡Depolarization: threshold (about −55 mV) reached, Na⁺ gates open, Na⁺ rushes in, inside becomes positive (+40 mV)
↩️Repolarization: K⁺ gates open, K⁺ flows out; a short hyperpolarization follows, then the refractory period
🎯All-or-none: an impulse either fires fully or not at all; stronger stimuli fire more often, not bigger

At a synapse, the impulse opens Ca²⁺ channels; vesicles release neurotransmitters (acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin) by exocytosis. They cross the synaptic cleft and bind receptors on the next neuron. Enzymes such as cholinesterase then break them down.

A reflex arc: receptor → sensory neuron → interneuron in the spinal cord → motor neuron → effector. Brain parts: cerebrum (thinking, senses, movement), cerebellum (balance and coordination), medulla oblongata (breathing, heart rate), hypothalamus (links nervous and endocrine systems).

Remember: Na⁺ in = depolarize, K⁺ out = repolarize. Myelin makes impulses jump and travel faster.

Key words

  • action potential: the quick reversal of charge that travels along an axon
  • myelin sheath: a fatty insulating layer around some axons
  • synaptic cleft: the small gap between two neurons
  • neurotransmitter: a chemical messenger released at a synapse

See real-life examples

Multiple sclerosis

In MS the immune system damages myelin, so nerve impulses slow down or are blocked.

Touching a hot stove

A reflex arc through the spinal cord pulls your hand away before your brain even feels the pain.

Dental freezing

Local anaesthetics block Na⁺ channels, so pain signals cannot travel along the nerves.
