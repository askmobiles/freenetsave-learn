Protein synthesis

Learn what you need to know

Gene expression follows the central dogma: DNA → RNA → protein. A gene is a DNA sequence that codes for a polypeptide (or a functional RNA).

1️⃣Initiation: RNA polymerase binds the promoter (TATA box in eukaryotes)
2️⃣Elongation: RNA polymerase reads the template strand 3′→5′ and builds mRNA 5′→3′
3️⃣Termination: a terminator sequence ends transcription
✂️Processing (eukaryotes): 5′ cap, poly-A tail, introns spliced out by spliceosomes

The genetic code: 64 codons (3 bases each), 61 code for amino acids and 3 are stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA). AUG codes for methionine and starts translation. The code is redundant (several codons per amino acid) but not ambiguous, and it is nearly universal.

Translation at the ribosome: the small subunit binds the mRNA at AUG; tRNA with matching anticodons bring amino acids to the A, P and E sites; peptide bonds form; the ribosome moves one codon at a time until a stop codon, when a release factor frees the polypeptide.

Prokaryotes have no nucleus, so transcription and translation can happen at the same time; their genes usually have no introns. Eukaryotic mRNA is processed in the nucleus before export.

Remember: transcription (nucleus) makes mRNA; translation (ribosome) makes protein. Codon on mRNA, anticodon on tRNA.

Key words

  • promoter: the DNA sequence where RNA polymerase binds to start transcription
  • intron: a non-coding part of a gene removed during RNA processing
  • anticodon: three tRNA bases that pair with an mRNA codon
  • ribosome: the rRNA and protein machine that builds polypeptides

See real-life examples

Antibiotics

Antibiotics like streptomycin attack bacterial ribosomes, which differ from human ribosomes.

Alternative splicing

By joining exons in different ways, one human gene can make several different proteins.

Insulin from bacteria

Because the genetic code is universal, bacteria can read a human insulin gene and make human insulin.
