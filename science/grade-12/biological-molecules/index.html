Biological molecules

Learn what you need to know

Biological molecules are built mostly from C, H, O, N, P and S. Their functional groups decide how they react: hydroxyl (–OH), carbonyl (C=O), carboxyl (–COOH), amino (–NH₂), phosphate (–PO₄) and sulfhydryl (–SH).

Large molecules (macromolecules) are polymers of monomers. Dehydration synthesis (condensation) joins monomers and releases water; hydrolysis splits them by adding water.

🍬Carbohydrates: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose), disaccharides (sucrose, lactose, maltose), polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin); glycosidic bonds
🧈Lipids: triglycerides (glycerol + 3 fatty acids, ester bonds), phospholipids, steroids, waxes; saturated vs unsaturated
🥩Proteins: 20 amino acids joined by peptide bonds; primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary structure
🧬Nucleic acids: nucleotides (phosphate, sugar, base) joined by phosphodiester bonds

Saturated fatty acids have no C=C double bonds and are solid at room temperature (butter); unsaturated fats have C=C bonds that put kinks in the chain, so they are liquid (olive oil). Trans fats are artificially straightened unsaturated fats linked to heart disease.

Protein shape is held by hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions and disulfide bridges (between cysteines). Heat, pH changes or salts can denature a protein.

Remember: condensation builds, hydrolysis breaks. Glycosidic (sugars), ester (fats), peptide (proteins), phosphodiester (nucleic acids).

Key words

  • functional group: a cluster of atoms that gives a molecule its properties
  • glycosidic bond: the bond joining two sugars
  • ester bond: the bond joining glycerol and a fatty acid
  • disulfide bridge: a covalent S–S bond between two cysteines in a protein

See real-life examples

Hair perms

A perm breaks and reforms disulfide bridges in hair keratin to change its shape.

Frying an egg

Heat denatures egg-white proteins, which turn from clear to white and cannot go back.

Insects and crabs

Their hard outer skeletons are made of chitin, a polysaccharide with nitrogen.
