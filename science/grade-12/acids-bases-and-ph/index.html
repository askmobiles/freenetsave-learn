Acids, bases and pH

Brønsted–Lowry acids and bases, pH and pOH, Ka and Kb, buffers, titration curves and indicators.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

Brønsted–Lowry: an acid donates H⁺ and a base accepts H⁺. When an acid gives up H⁺ it becomes its conjugate base: HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻. Water can act as either (amphiprotic).

Strong acids (HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, HClO₄) ionize 100%. Weak acids (CH₃COOH, HF) ionize only a little; their strength is given by Ka = [H₃O⁺][A⁻] ÷ [HA]. Bigger Ka = stronger acid. For bases, Kb. For a conjugate pair, Ka × Kb = Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C.

🔢pH = −log[H₃O⁺]; pOH = −log[OH⁻]
➕pH + pOH = 14 at 25 °C
🍋pH < 7 acidic; 7 neutral; > 7 basic
🔟Each pH unit = 10× change in [H₃O⁺]

A buffer (a weak acid with its conjugate base, like CH₃COOH/CH₃COO⁻) resists pH change when small amounts of acid or base are added. Blood is buffered by H₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻ at about pH 7.4.

In a titration, a base of known concentration is added from a burette until the equivalence point (moles acid = moles base). An indicator changes colour at the endpoint. Strong acid + strong base: equivalence at pH 7. Weak acid + strong base: above 7 (use phenolphthalein). Strong acid + weak base: below 7 (use methyl orange).

Remember: acids give H⁺, bases take H⁺. pH + pOH = 14. Buffers = weak acid + conjugate base.

Key words

  • conjugate base: what remains after an acid donates H⁺
  • Ka: the acid ionization constant
  • buffer: a solution that resists changes in pH
  • equivalence point: where moles of acid and base are exactly equal in a titration

See real-life examples

Antacids

Tablets with CaCO₃ or Mg(OH)₂ neutralize extra stomach acid.

Acid rain

SO₂ and NOₓ from burning fuels form acids that harmed lakes in Ontario.

Swimming pools

Pool owners test pH and keep it near 7.4 so chlorine works and eyes do not sting.
