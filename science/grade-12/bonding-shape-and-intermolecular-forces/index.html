Bonding, molecular shape and intermolecular forces

Ionic, covalent and metallic bonding, Lewis structures, VSEPR shapes, polarity and intermolecular forces.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

Electronegativity difference (ΔEN) sets the bond type: about 0–0.4 non-polar covalent, 0.5–1.7 polar covalent, above 1.7 mostly ionic. Metallic bonds are positive ions in a "sea" of moving electrons.

🧂Ionic solids (NaCl): high melting point, brittle, conduct when melted or dissolved
💎Network covalent (diamond, SiO₂): very hard, very high melting point
🧊Molecular solids (ice, sugar): low melting point, do not conduct
🔩Metallic solids: shiny, malleable, conduct as solids

VSEPR: electron pairs around a central atom push apart. 2 bonding groups = linear (180°); 3 = trigonal planar (120°); 4 = tetrahedral (109.5°). Lone pairs push harder: NH₃ is trigonal pyramidal (107°), H₂O is bent (104.5°). 5 = trigonal bipyramidal; 6 = octahedral.

A molecule is polar if it has polar bonds that do not cancel because of its shape. CO₂ is linear, so its dipoles cancel (non-polar); H₂O is bent, so it is polar.

Intermolecular forces, weakest to strongest: London dispersion (all molecules; stronger with more electrons), dipole–dipole (polar molecules), hydrogen bonding (H bonded to N, O or F, attracted to another N, O or F). Stronger forces mean higher boiling points. "Like dissolves like": polar dissolves polar.

Remember: shape decides polarity; polarity decides forces; forces decide boiling point and solubility.

Key words

  • electronegativity: how strongly an atom attracts shared electrons
  • VSEPR: theory that electron pairs repel to set molecular shape
  • dipole: a molecule or bond with a positive and a negative end
  • London dispersion force: a weak attraction from temporary dipoles in all molecules

See real-life examples

Ice floats

Hydrogen bonds hold ice in an open structure, so it is less dense than liquid water and lakes freeze from the top.

Oil and vinegar

Oil is non-polar and vinegar is mostly water (polar), so they separate.

Geckos

Geckos stick to walls using millions of tiny hairs and London dispersion forces.
