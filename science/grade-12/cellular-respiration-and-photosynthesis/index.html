Cellular respiration and photosynthesis

How cells make ATP from glucose, and how plants capture light energy to make glucose.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 12 science.

Learn what you need to know

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the cell's energy currency. Breaking off a phosphate (ATP → ADP + P) releases energy for work.

Aerobic cellular respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (about 36–38 ATP).

1️⃣Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate, 2 ATP, no oxygen needed
2️⃣Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix): CO₂ released, NADH and FADH₂ made
3️⃣Electron transport chain (inner membrane): most ATP made; O₂ is the final electron acceptor and forms water
🍞No oxygen: fermentation makes lactic acid (muscles) or ethanol + CO₂ (yeast), only 2 ATP

Photosynthesis in chloroplasts: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. The light-dependent reactions (thylakoids) split water, release O₂ and make ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle (stroma) uses them to fix CO₂ into sugar.

The two processes are a cycle: the products of one are the reactants of the other. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light and reflects green.

Remember: respiration releases energy from glucose; photosynthesis stores light energy in glucose.

Key words

  • ATP: the molecule cells use to carry energy
  • glycolysis: splitting glucose into pyruvate in the cytoplasm
  • Calvin cycle: light-independent reactions that make sugar from CO₂
  • fermentation: making ATP without oxygen

See real-life examples

Sore muscles

During hard exercise, muscles run short of oxygen and use lactic acid fermentation for quick ATP.

Bread rising

Yeast ferments sugar into ethanol and CO₂; the gas bubbles make dough rise.

Fall leaves

In autumn, chlorophyll breaks down, so the yellow and orange pigments in maple leaves show.
