Homeostasis

Learn what you need to know

Homeostasis is keeping the inside of the body steady while the outside changes. It uses a receptor (senses change), a control centre (often the brain) and an effector (muscle or gland that responds).

Most systems use negative feedback: the response reverses the change. Positive feedback makes a change bigger, as in childbirth contractions (oxytocin) or blood clotting.

🌡️Temperature: hypothalamus; sweating and dilated vessels cool; shivering and narrowed vessels warm
🍬Blood sugar: insulin lowers it (cells take up glucose); glucagon raises it (liver releases glucose)
💧Water: ADH makes kidneys keep water; less ADH = more urine
🫘Kidneys: the nephron filters blood, reabsorbs what is needed and secretes waste

In the nephron: filtration at the glomerulus, reabsorption of glucose, salts and water in the tubules, and secretion of extra wastes. Urine collects in the collecting duct.

Type 1 diabetes: the pancreas makes little or no insulin. Type 2 diabetes: cells do not respond well to insulin. Insulin was first used to treat diabetes in Toronto in 1922 (Banting and Best).

Remember: negative feedback = back to normal. Insulin in, glucagon out.

Key words

  • homeostasis: keeping a stable internal environment
  • negative feedback: a response that reverses a change
  • insulin: hormone that lowers blood sugar
  • nephron: the filtering unit of the kidney

See real-life examples

Winter in Canada

Shivering in the cold is muscle contraction that makes heat to keep your core near 37 °C.

A hot hockey game

Players sweat a lot; evaporating sweat carries heat away, and thirst and ADH help replace the water lost.

Insulin pumps

Many people with type 1 diabetes wear a pump that delivers insulin through a small tube under the skin.
