Biotechnology

Learn what you need to know

Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sequences (often palindromes, like GAATTC for EcoRI). Many leave sticky ends that can pair with matching ends from another DNA cut by the same enzyme. DNA ligase seals the pieces.

Recombinant DNA: a gene (for example human insulin) is cut out and inserted into a plasmid (small circular bacterial DNA, the vector). Bacteria take up the plasmid (transformation) and make the protein. Antibiotic-resistance genes on the plasmid help select bacteria that took it up.

🔥PCR: denature at ~95 °C, anneal primers at ~55 °C, extend with Taq polymerase at ~72 °C; DNA doubles each cycle
⚡Gel electrophoresis: DNA is negative, so it moves to the + end; small pieces move farthest
🔤DNA sequencing: reads the order of bases (Sanger method, newer high-speed methods)
✂️CRISPR-Cas9: a guide RNA leads the Cas9 enzyme to cut a chosen DNA sequence to edit genes

Uses: insulin and vaccines made by bacteria and yeast, DNA fingerprinting in forensics and paternity, genetic testing, GMO crops (Bt corn, Golden Rice), gene therapy, and cloning.

Ethics: who owns genetic information, privacy of DNA tests, safety and labelling of GMOs, fairness of access to gene therapy, and limits on editing human embryos. In Canada, editing human embryo DNA that could be passed on is banned by law.

Remember: cut (restriction enzyme) → paste (ligase) → copy (PCR or bacteria) → check (gel electrophoresis).

Key words

  • restriction enzyme: an enzyme that cuts DNA at a specific sequence
  • plasmid: a small circular piece of bacterial DNA used as a vector
  • PCR: polymerase chain reaction, which makes millions of copies of DNA
  • sticky ends: short single-stranded overhangs left after some cuts

See real-life examples

Insulin for diabetes

Most insulin used today is human insulin made by bacteria or yeast using recombinant DNA.

COVID-19 tests

PCR tests copy tiny amounts of viral genetic material until there is enough to detect.

Crime labs

DNA fingerprinting compares band patterns on a gel to match a sample to a person.
