Population dynamics

Learn what you need to know

A population is all the members of one species in an area. Density = number ÷ area. Change in population = (births + immigration) − (deaths + emigration).

📈Exponential growth: J-shaped curve, no limits
🏔️Logistic growth: S-shaped curve that levels off at carrying capacity (K)
🚧Density-dependent limits: food, disease, competition, predation
🌪️Density-independent limits: fire, floods, cold snaps

Carrying capacity (K) is the largest population the environment can support over time. Species that grow fast with many offspring are r-selected (insects, weeds); species with few offspring and much care are K-selected (whales, elephants, humans).

Interactions: predation (lynx and snowshoe hare cycles in Canada), competition, and symbiosis — mutualism (+/+), commensalism (+/0) and parasitism (+/−).

Human population passed 8 billion in 2022. The demographic transition model shows birth and death rates falling as countries develop. Age pyramids show if a population is growing, stable or shrinking.

Remember: J-curve = no limits; S-curve = levels off at K.

Key words

  • carrying capacity: the largest population an environment can support
  • limiting factor: something that keeps a population from growing
  • mutualism: a relationship where both species benefit
  • exponential growth: growth that speeds up as the population gets bigger

See real-life examples

Lynx and hare

In Canada's boreal forest, lynx and snowshoe hare numbers rise and fall in about 10-year cycles.

Zebra mussels

With no natural predators, invasive zebra mussels grew explosively in the Great Lakes.

Deer in winter

Deep snow and little food limit deer numbers each winter in northern Ontario.
