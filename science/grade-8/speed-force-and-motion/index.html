Speed, force and motion

Learn what you need to know

Speed is how far something travels in a certain time.

speed = distance ÷ time

A car that goes 300 km in 3 hours has an average speed of 100 km/h. Velocity is speed in a direction (100 km/h north). Acceleration is a change in velocity: speeding up, slowing down or turning.

A force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons (N). Sir Isaac Newton described three laws of motion:

1️⃣First law (inertia): an object stays still or keeps moving in a straight line unless a force acts on it
2️⃣Second law: force = mass × acceleration; more force gives more acceleration, more mass gives less
3️⃣Third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction

Friction is a force that slows motion between surfaces. Balanced forces cause no change in motion; unbalanced forces cause acceleration.

Remember: speed = distance ÷ time, and F = m × a.

Key words

  • velocity: speed in a certain direction
  • acceleration: a change in speed or direction
  • inertia: the tendency of an object to keep doing what it is doing
  • newton: the unit used to measure force

See real-life examples

Seat belts

When a car stops suddenly, your body keeps moving forward because of inertia. A seat belt supplies the force that stops you.

Rocket launch

A rocket pushes hot gas down; the gas pushes the rocket up. That is Newton's third law.

Hockey

A hard slap shot puts a big force on a light puck, so it accelerates very quickly: F = m × a.
