Waves, light and sound

Wave properties, the electromagnetic spectrum, and how sound travels.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 science.

Learn what you need to know

A wave carries energy from one place to another without carrying matter.

⛰️Crest: the top of a wave; trough: the bottom
📏Wavelength: distance from crest to crest
📶Amplitude: height of the wave; more amplitude = more energy (louder, brighter)
🔁Frequency: waves per second, in hertz (Hz)

Sound is a mechanical wave: it needs a material (air, water, solid) to travel through. It cannot travel in empty space. Sound moves about 343 m/s in air, faster in water and faster still in solids. Higher frequency = higher pitch.

Light is an electromagnetic wave: it can travel through empty space, at 300 000 km/s. Visible light is a small part of the electromagnetic spectrum: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays.

Light can be reflected (bounce off a mirror), refracted (bend as it enters water or glass) or absorbed.

Remember: sound needs matter; light does not. Light is much faster.

Key words

  • wavelength: the distance from one crest to the next
  • amplitude: the height of a wave, related to its energy
  • frequency: the number of waves per second
  • refraction: the bending of light as it passes into a new material

See real-life examples

Thunder and lightning

You see lightning before you hear thunder because light is far faster than sound. Count the seconds and divide by 3 to estimate the distance in km.

A straw in water

A straw in a glass of water looks bent. Light refracts as it passes from water to air.

Ultrasound

Doctors use high-frequency sound waves (ultrasound) to see babies before they are born.
