Diffusion and osmosis

How materials move into and out of cells.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 science.

Learn what you need to know

Cells must take in nutrients and oxygen and get rid of wastes. These materials cross the cell membrane, which is selectively permeable: it lets some things through and blocks others.

Diffusion is the movement of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration, until they are evenly spread. Oxygen diffuses from your lungs into your blood this way.

Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane. Water moves toward the side with more dissolved substances (less water).

🥬Wilted celery in fresh water: water moves in, celery becomes crisp
🥒Cucumber in salt: water moves out, it shrinks into a pickle
🫐Raisin in water: swells up as water moves in

Diffusion and osmosis are passive: they need no energy from the cell. Moving materials against the gradient (low to high) needs energy, called active transport.

Remember: diffusion = particles spread high to low; osmosis = water crossing a membrane.

Key words

  • diffusion: particles moving from high to low concentration
  • osmosis: water moving across a selectively permeable membrane
  • selectively permeable: letting only some substances through
  • concentration gradient: a difference in concentration between two areas

See real-life examples

Salting roads

Salt on icy roads kills grass beside the road. Salty soil pulls water out of the plant roots by osmosis.

Pickles

Cucumbers soaked in salty brine lose water by osmosis and become pickles.

Breathing

In your lungs, oxygen diffuses from the air sacs (high oxygen) into your blood (low oxygen).
