Body systems working together

How the body's systems cooperate to keep you alive and balanced.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 science.

Learn what you need to know

No body system works alone. Systems constantly work together to keep conditions inside your body steady. This balance is called homeostasis.

🫁Respiratory + circulatory: lungs load oxygen into blood; the heart pumps it to every cell
🍽️Digestive + circulatory: nutrients from the intestines enter the blood and travel to cells
🧠Nervous + muscular + skeletal: brain sends signals, muscles pull bones to move
🚽Excretory (kidneys) + circulatory: kidneys filter wastes out of the blood
🛡️Immune + circulatory: white blood cells travel in blood to fight germs
🧪Endocrine: glands release hormones into the blood to control growth and energy

Example: when you exercise, your muscles need more oxygen and make more heat. Your brain makes you breathe faster and your heart beat faster, and your skin sweats to cool you down. Everything works together.

When one system fails, others are affected. That is why healthy habits matter for the whole body.

Remember: body systems cooperate to keep your inside conditions balanced (homeostasis).

Key words

  • homeostasis: keeping steady conditions inside the body
  • hormone: a chemical messenger carried in the blood
  • excretory system: the kidneys and bladder, which remove wastes
  • endocrine system: glands that make hormones

See real-life examples

Exercise

During a soccer game your heart rate rises, you breathe harder and you sweat. Many systems respond at once.

Insulin

After you eat, your pancreas releases the hormone insulin to keep blood sugar steady. Insulin was discovered in Toronto in 1921 by Banting and Best.

Shivering

When you are cold, your nervous system makes your muscles shiver to produce heat.
