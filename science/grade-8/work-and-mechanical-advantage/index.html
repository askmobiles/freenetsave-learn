Work and mechanical advantage

Learn what you need to know

In science, work is done when a force moves an object over a distance.

work (J) = force (N) × distance (m)

Work is measured in joules (J). Lifting a 10 N box up 2 m does 20 J of work. If the object does not move, no work is done, even if you push hard!

Machines make work easier. Mechanical advantage (MA) tells how much a machine multiplies your force:

MA = output force (load) ÷ input force (effort)

If you lift a 600 N load with only 200 N of effort, the MA is 3. A machine cannot reduce the total work; it trades a smaller force for a longer distance.

Efficiency = (work output ÷ work input) × 100%. Friction always makes real machines less than 100% efficient.

Remember: W = F × d, and MA = load ÷ effort.

Key words

  • work: force times the distance an object moves
  • joule: the unit of work and energy
  • mechanical advantage: how many times a machine multiplies your force
  • effort force: the force you put into a machine

See real-life examples

Car jack

A car jack has a big mechanical advantage. A small push on the handle lifts a heavy car a little at a time.

Ramps for wheelchairs

A long ramp lets a wheelchair user go up a few steps with less force, by going a longer distance.

Pushing a wall

Pushing a wall all day makes you tired, but in science you did no work, because the wall did not move.
