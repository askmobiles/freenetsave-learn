Systems in action

Inputs, outputs, efficiency, and how systems affect people and the environment. Grade 8 science.

Learn what you need to know

A system is a group of parts that work together to do a job. A bicycle, a school, a power grid and your body are all systems.

📥Input: what goes into the system (energy, materials, effort)
⚙️Process: what the system does
📤Output: what comes out (motion, products, results)
🗑️Side effects: waste, heat, noise or pollution

Mechanical systems use machines and moving parts. Social systems use people and rules, like a school or a transit system.

Efficiency compares useful output to total input:

efficiency = (useful output ÷ total input) × 100%

No system is 100% efficient. Some energy is always lost, usually as heat from friction. A car engine is only about 25–30% efficient; an electric motor can be over 85%.

When designing systems, we must also think about their effects on society and the environment.

Remember: input → process → output, and efficiency is never 100%.

Key words

  • system: a group of parts that work together to do a job
  • input: what goes into a system
  • output: what a system produces
  • efficiency: how much of the input becomes useful output

See real-life examples

A bicycle

Your pedalling (input) turns gears and wheels (process) and moves you forward (output). Some energy is lost to friction.

TTC subway

The TTC is a system: trains, tracks, drivers, schedules and riders all work together to move people.

LED vs old bulbs

An old light bulb turns only about 5% of its energy into light; the rest is heat. An LED is far more efficient.
