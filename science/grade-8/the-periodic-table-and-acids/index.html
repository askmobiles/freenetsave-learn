The periodic table and acids

How the periodic table is organized, and acids, bases and the pH scale.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 8 science.

Learn what you need to know

The periodic table organizes all the elements by their atomic number (the number of protons). Dmitri Mendeleev made the first useful table in 1869.

↔️Rows are called periods
↕️Columns are called groups or families; elements in a group act alike
🪙Metals: left side, shiny, conduct heat and electricity
💨Non-metals: right side, often gases or dull solids
🎈Noble gases: far right column; very unreactive (helium, neon)

Acids taste sour and turn blue litmus paper red (lemon juice, vinegar). Bases feel slippery, taste bitter and turn red litmus blue (soap, baking soda).

The pH scale goes from 0 to 14. Below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral (pure water), and above 7 is basic. When an acid and a base mix, they neutralize each other and make water and a salt.

Remember: pH below 7 = acid, 7 = neutral, above 7 = base.

Key words

  • atomic number: the number of protons in an atom of an element
  • group: a column of elements with similar properties
  • pH scale: a 0 to 14 scale of how acidic or basic something is
  • neutralize: to cancel out an acid with a base

See real-life examples

Stomach acid

Your stomach makes strong acid (pH about 2) to digest food. Antacid tablets are bases that neutralize extra acid.

Bee and wasp stings

A bee sting is acidic, so some people use baking soda paste. A wasp sting is basic, so some use vinegar.

Acid rain

Pollution from burning coal made acid rain that harmed lakes in Ontario. Cleaner power plants have reduced it.
