Genes and inheritance

Learn what you need to know

You look like your family because of inheritance: traits are passed from parents to children.

Instructions for your traits are stored in DNA, inside the nucleus of every cell. DNA is coiled into chromosomes. Humans have 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs: one of each pair from each parent. A gene is a section of DNA that codes for one trait.

Different versions of a gene are called alleles. A dominant allele (written with a capital letter, B) shows its trait even if there is only one copy. A recessive allele (b) only shows when there are two copies (bb).

Gregor Mendel discovered these patterns in the 1860s by breeding pea plants. A Punnett square predicts the chances of a child's traits.

Example: if both parents are Bb, the children could be BB, Bb, Bb or bb. That is a 3 in 4 (75%) chance of showing the dominant trait and 1 in 4 (25%) of the recessive trait.

Remember: genes are on chromosomes made of DNA; dominant hides recessive.

Key words

  • DNA: the molecule that carries genetic instructions
  • gene: a part of DNA that codes for a trait
  • dominant: an allele that shows its trait with only one copy
  • recessive: an allele that shows only when there are two copies

See real-life examples

Pea plants

Mendel crossed tall and short pea plants and found the short trait skipped a generation, then came back.

Eye colour

Eye colour is controlled by several genes, so two brown-eyed parents can sometimes have a blue-eyed child.

Identical twins

Identical twins come from one fertilized egg, so they have the same DNA.
