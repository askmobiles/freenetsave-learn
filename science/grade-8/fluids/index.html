Learn what you need to know

A fluid is any substance that can flow. Both liquids and gases are fluids, because their particles can move past each other.

Viscosity is how thick a fluid is, or how much it resists flowing. Honey and ketchup have high viscosity; water and juice have low viscosity. Heating a liquid usually lowers its viscosity: warm syrup pours faster.

Flow rate is how much fluid passes a point in a certain time, like litres per minute.

🩸Blood flows through your body
🚿Water flows in pipes to your home
🛢️Oil flows through pipelines
✈️Air flows around airplane wings
🏭Factories mix and move fluids

Fluids are also compressible or not: gases can be squeezed into a smaller space, but liquids barely can. That difference matters in tires (gas) and car brakes (liquid).

Remember: fluids flow; viscosity tells how easily.

Key words

  • fluid: a substance that can flow, like a liquid or a gas
  • viscosity: how much a fluid resists flowing
  • flow rate: the amount of fluid that passes a point in a certain time
  • compressible: able to be squeezed into a smaller space

See real-life examples

Maple syrup

Maple syrup is thick. Warming it lowers its viscosity, so it pours easily onto pancakes.

Motor oil

Car engines use oil with the right viscosity: thin enough to flow in cold Canadian winters, thick enough to protect the engine.

Ketchup bottles

Ketchup is so viscous that squeeze bottles were invented to help it flow.
