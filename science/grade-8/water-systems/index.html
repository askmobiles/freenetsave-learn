Water systems

Earth's water: oceans, ice, groundwater, watersheds, and protecting it. Grade 8 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

About 71% of Earth's surface is water, but about 97% of it is salt water in the oceans. Only about 3% is fresh water, and most of that is frozen in glaciers and ice caps. Less than 1% is easy for us to use.

Groundwater is water that soaks into the ground and fills spaces in soil and rock. Layers of rock that hold groundwater are called aquifers. Many rural Canadians get drinking water from wells into aquifers.

A watershed (drainage basin) is all the land that drains into one river, lake or ocean. Anything spilled in a watershed can end up in the water downstream.

🌊Oceans: 97% of Earth's water, affect climate and weather
🧊Glaciers and ice caps: store most fresh water
🏞️Great Lakes: about 20% of the world's fresh surface water
🚰Water treatment makes water safe to drink

Threats to water include pollution, overuse, and climate change melting glaciers. We protect water by conserving it, keeping chemicals out of drains, and protecting wetlands.

Remember: only a tiny part of Earth's water is fresh and easy to use, so protect it.

Key words

  • groundwater: water stored underground in soil and rock
  • aquifer: an underground layer of rock that holds water
  • watershed: all the land that drains into one body of water
  • glacier: a huge, slow-moving mass of ice

See real-life examples

The Great Lakes

The Great Lakes hold about 20% of the world's fresh surface water and give drinking water to tens of millions of people.

Walkerton, 2000

In Walkerton, Ontario, polluted well water made thousands of people sick. It led to much stricter drinking water rules.

Storm drains

Storm drains often flow straight to rivers and lakes, so pouring paint or oil down them pollutes the water.
