Pressure and hydraulics

Learn what you need to know

Pressure is how much force acts on a certain area.

pressure = force ÷ area

Pressure is measured in pascals (Pa); 1 Pa = 1 N/m². Kilopascals (kPa) are common: car tires are about 220 kPa. The same force on a smaller area makes more pressure. That is why knives are sharp and snowshoes are wide.

Pascal's law: pressure applied to a fluid in a closed container is passed equally to every part of the fluid.

Hydraulic systems use liquids (usually oil) to multiply force. A small force on a small piston creates pressure that pushes a large piston with a much bigger force. Excavators, car brakes and dentist chairs use hydraulics.

Pneumatic systems use compressed air or gas instead. Air brakes on buses, nail guns and jackhammers are pneumatic.

Remember: P = F ÷ A, and Pascal's law lets hydraulics multiply force.

Key words

  • pressure: force acting on a certain area
  • pascal: the unit of pressure, one newton per square metre
  • hydraulic system: a system that uses a liquid to transmit force
  • pneumatic system: a system that uses a gas to transmit force

See real-life examples

Snowshoes

Snowshoes spread your weight over a large area, so the pressure is low and you do not sink into deep snow.

Excavators

An excavator's arm is moved by hydraulic cylinders filled with oil, letting it lift tonnes of dirt.

Bus doors and brakes

TTC buses use pneumatic (air) systems for their brakes and doors. That hissing sound is compressed air.
