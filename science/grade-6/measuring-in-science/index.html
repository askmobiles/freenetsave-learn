Learn what you need to know

Scientists all over the world use the same units, called the SI system (metric system). It is based on 10s, which makes converting easy.

📏Length: metre (m); 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm; 1 km = 1000 m
⚖️Mass: gram (g); 1 kg = 1000 g
🧪Volume: litre (L); 1 L = 1000 mL
🌡️Temperature: degrees Celsius (°C)
⏱️Time: second (s)

Choose the right tool: a ruler or measuring tape for length, a balance or scale for mass, a graduated cylinder for liquid volume, a thermometer for temperature, and a stopwatch for time.

Read scales carefully: look straight at the scale (not from an angle), read a liquid at the bottom of its curve (the meniscus), and always write the unit. "5" means nothing; "5 cm" is a measurement.

Accurate means close to the true value. Measuring several times and finding the average gives a better result.

Remember: right tool, read carefully, always write the unit.

Key words

  • SI units: the metric units used by scientists worldwide
  • mass: the amount of matter in an object
  • volume: the amount of space something takes up
  • average: the total divided by the number of measurements

See real-life examples

Kitchen science

Recipes use millilitres and grams. A kitchen scale and measuring cup are science tools too.

Running a race

Race times are measured with stopwatches, often to a hundredth of a second.

Fever check

A thermometer showing 38.5 °C tells a parent their child has a fever.
