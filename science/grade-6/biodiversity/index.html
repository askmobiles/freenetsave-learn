Learn what you need to know

Biodiversity means the variety of all living things in a place: plants, animals, fungi and tiny living things too.

High biodiversity makes an ecosystem stronger. If one food source disappears, animals can eat something else. Nature also gives us food, medicine, clean air and clean water.

🏗️Habitat loss: building, farming, logging
🏭Pollution of air, water and soil
🌡️Climate change
🐟Overhunting and overfishing
🌿Invasive species, like zebra mussels

These threats can make species endangered or even extinct, gone forever. In Canada, the monarch butterfly, the beluga whale and the woodland caribou are at risk.

We protect biodiversity with parks, laws, planting native plants, cleaning up pollution and not releasing pets into the wild.

Remember: more variety of life means a healthier, stronger ecosystem.

Key words

  • biodiversity: the variety of living things in a place
  • endangered: at risk of dying out
  • extinct: gone forever, with none left alive
  • invasive species: a living thing brought to a new place where it harms native life

See real-life examples

Zebra mussels

Zebra mussels came to the Great Lakes on ships. They spread fast and crowd out native mussels.

Monarch butterflies

Monarchs need milkweed plants. People plant milkweed in gardens to help them.

National parks

Canada has over 35 national parks that protect habitats for plants and animals.
