Flight

The four forces of flight and how planes, birds and helicopters fly. Grade 6 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 6 · Flight

Learn what you need to know

Anything that flies must deal with four forces:

⬆️Lift: pushes up, made by wings
⬇️Weight: gravity pulls down
➡️Thrust: pushes forward, made by engines or flapping
⬅️Drag: air pushes back and slows you down

To take off, lift must be greater than weight. To speed up, thrust must be greater than drag.

Wings are shaped and tilted so air moving over them is pushed downward. Pushing air down pushes the wing up. This is lift. The faster the air flows, the more lift.

Smooth, pointed shapes are streamlined: they cut through air with less drag. Birds, jets and paper airplanes all use this idea. The Wright brothers made the first powered flight in 1903.

Remember: lift beats weight to rise; thrust beats drag to go forward.

Key words

  • lift: the upward force made by wings
  • thrust: the forward force that moves a flying object
  • drag: air resistance that slows a moving object
  • streamlined: shaped to move through air or water easily

See real-life examples

Canada Goose

Geese fly in a V shape. Each bird rides the lift from the bird ahead, saving energy.

Helicopters

A helicopter's spinning blades are wings that go round and round to make lift.

Paper airplanes

Folding a sharp nose and flat wings makes a paper plane glide farther with less drag.
