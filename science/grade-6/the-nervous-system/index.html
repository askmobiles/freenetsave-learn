The nervous system

Learn what you need to know

The nervous system is your body's control and message system. It has three main parts:

🧠Brain: the control centre for thinking, memory and movement
🦴Spinal cord: a thick bundle of nerves in your backbone that carries messages
〰️Nerves: long cells that carry signals all over the body

Nerve cells are called neurons. Signals travel along them very fast, up to about 100 metres per second.

The brain has parts with different jobs. The cerebrum handles thinking and senses, the cerebellum handles balance and coordination, and the brain stem controls breathing and heartbeat.

A reflex is a fast, automatic reaction, like pulling your hand from a hot stove. The spinal cord sends the "move!" message before your brain even feels the pain.

Protect your nervous system: wear a helmet, get enough sleep, and avoid drugs and alcohol.

Remember: senses send signals to the brain; the brain sends commands back through the spinal cord and nerves.

Key words

  • neuron: a nerve cell that carries signals
  • spinal cord: the bundle of nerves inside the backbone
  • reflex: a fast, automatic reaction
  • cerebellum: the part of the brain that controls balance

See real-life examples

Knee reflex

When a doctor taps below your knee, your leg kicks without you deciding. That is a reflex.

Riding a bike

Your cerebellum keeps you balanced while your cerebrum decides where to go.

Concussions

A hard hit to the head can cause a concussion, a brain injury. Helmets help prevent them in hockey and cycling.
