Rocks and the rock cycle

Learn what you need to know

There are three main kinds of rock:

🌋Igneous: forms when melted rock (magma or lava) cools; granite, basalt, pumice
🏖️Sedimentary: forms when layers of sand, mud and shells are pressed together; sandstone, limestone, shale
💎Metamorphic: forms when heat and pressure change a rock; marble, slate, gneiss

Rocks are always slowly changing in the rock cycle:

Weathering breaks rocks into sediment → sediment is pressed into sedimentary rock → heat and pressure change it into metamorphic rock → it melts into magma → magma cools into igneous rock → and it can weather again.

Fossils are found mostly in sedimentary rock, because plants and animals get buried in the layers.

Remember: igneous from cooling, sedimentary from layers, metamorphic from heat and pressure.

Key words

  • igneous rock: rock formed when melted rock cools
  • sedimentary rock: rock formed from pressed layers of sediment
  • metamorphic rock: rock changed by heat and pressure
  • rock cycle: the slow process by which rocks change from one kind to another

See real-life examples

Canadian Shield

The Canadian Shield, under much of Ontario, has some of the oldest igneous and metamorphic rock on Earth.

Niagara Escarpment

The Niagara Escarpment shows layers of sedimentary rock built up in an ancient sea.

Floating rock

Pumice is an igneous rock full of air bubbles. It is so light it can float on water.
