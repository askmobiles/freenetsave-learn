Sorting living things

Learn what you need to know

There are millions of kinds of living things. To study them, scientists sort them into groups by their features. This is called classification.

The biggest groups are kingdoms. Each kingdom is split into smaller and smaller groups, down to a single species.

🐻Animals: eat food, most can move
🌳Plants: make their own food from sunlight
🍄Fungi: mushrooms, mould and yeast; absorb food
🦠Bacteria: tiny single cells
🫧Protists: mostly single cells, like amoebas and algae

Every species gets a two-part scientific name in Latin, like Ursus americanus for the black bear. This way, scientists all over the world use the same name.

A dichotomous key helps you identify a living thing by answering yes-or-no questions, one step at a time.

Remember: scientists sort living things by their features, from kingdom down to species.

Key words

  • classification: sorting living things into groups
  • kingdom: the largest group of living things
  • species: one kind of living thing that can have young together
  • dichotomous key: a chart of two-choice questions used to identify living things

See real-life examples

Same name everywhere

A "gopher" means different animals in different places, but a scientific name means only one species.

Mushrooms are not plants

Mushrooms cannot make their own food from sunlight. That is why fungi have their own kingdom.

Field guides

Bird watchers use keys in field guides: "Is the head red? Yes or no?" to name a bird.
