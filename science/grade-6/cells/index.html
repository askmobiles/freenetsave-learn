Learn what you need to know

Every living thing is made of cells, the smallest units of life. Some living things, like bacteria, are just one cell. You are made of trillions of cells.

Most cells are too small to see without a microscope. Robert Hooke first saw cells in 1665 when he looked at thin slices of cork.

🧠Nucleus: the control centre, holds the instructions (DNA)
🧱Cell membrane: thin skin that controls what goes in and out
💧Cytoplasm: jelly that fills the cell
⚡Mitochondria: release energy from food
🫙Vacuole: stores water and wastes

Plant cells have three extra parts: a stiff cell wall for support, green chloroplasts that make food from sunlight, and one big vacuole. Animal cells have no cell wall and no chloroplasts.

Remember: all living things are made of cells; plant cells add a cell wall and chloroplasts.

Key words

  • cell: the smallest unit of life
  • nucleus: the control centre of a cell
  • cell membrane: the thin layer that controls what enters and leaves a cell
  • chloroplast: the green part of a plant cell that makes food

See real-life examples

Onion skin

Under a school microscope, a thin layer of onion skin shows rows of brick-shaped plant cells with cell walls.

Cheek cells

Gently scraping the inside of your cheek gives animal cells. They are rounder and have no cell wall.

Healing a cut

When you scrape your knee, your skin cells divide to make new cells and close the cut.
