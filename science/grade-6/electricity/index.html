Learn what you need to know

Static electricity is charge that builds up on a surface. Rubbing a balloon on your hair moves electrons, so the balloon sticks to the wall. Lightning is a giant static spark.

Current electricity is a steady flow of electrons through a circuit. It powers lights and devices.

🔗Series circuit: one path; if one bulb breaks, all go out
🔀Parallel circuit: many paths; one bulb can break and the others stay on
✅Conductors: copper, aluminum, water
⛔Insulators: plastic, rubber, glass, wood

Electricity can be made from many sources: hydro (moving water), wind, solar, nuclear, and burning fossil fuels. Most generators spin a magnet inside coils of wire.

We measure electrical energy use in kilowatt-hours. Turning off lights and unplugging chargers saves energy and money.

Remember: static stays put; current flows. Houses use parallel circuits.

Key words

  • static electricity: electric charge that builds up on a surface
  • current electricity: a steady flow of electrons in a circuit
  • series circuit: a circuit with only one path
  • parallel circuit: a circuit with more than one path

See real-life examples

Socks and carpets

Walking in socks on carpet builds static charge. Touching a doorknob gives a tiny shock.

Holiday lights

Older holiday lights were in series, so one bad bulb made the whole string go dark.

Niagara Falls

The power stations at Niagara Falls use falling water to spin generators and make electricity.
