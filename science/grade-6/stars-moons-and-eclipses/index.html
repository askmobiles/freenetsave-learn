Stars, moons and eclipses

How stars work, moons in the solar system, and solar and lunar eclipses. Grade 6 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 6 · Space

Learn what you need to know

A star is a huge ball of hot gas that makes its own light and heat. Our Sun is a medium-sized star. Other stars look tiny because they are very far away.

Stars have different colours: blue stars are hottest, then white, yellow (like our Sun), orange, and red stars are coolest. Patterns of stars are called constellations, like the Big Dipper.

A moon is a natural object that orbits a planet. Earth has one Moon. Jupiter and Saturn have dozens.

🌑Solar eclipse: the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth, blocking the Sun
🌕Lunar eclipse: Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, and Earth's shadow covers the Moon

Never look straight at the Sun, even during an eclipse. Use special eclipse glasses or a pinhole viewer.

Remember: solar eclipse = Moon blocks the Sun; lunar eclipse = Earth's shadow on the Moon.

Key words

  • star: a huge ball of hot gas that makes its own light
  • constellation: a pattern of stars in the sky
  • solar eclipse: when the Moon blocks the Sun
  • lunar eclipse: when Earth's shadow falls on the Moon

See real-life examples

April 2024 eclipse

On April 8, 2024, a total solar eclipse crossed southern Ontario, Quebec and the Maritimes. Day turned dark for a few minutes.

Blood Moon

During a total lunar eclipse, the Moon looks red because sunlight bends through Earth's air onto it.

The Big Dipper

The Big Dipper's two end stars point to Polaris, the North Star, which helps people find north.
