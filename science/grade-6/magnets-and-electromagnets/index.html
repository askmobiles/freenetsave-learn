Magnets and electromagnets

Learn what you need to know

A magnet attracts iron, nickel and cobalt. Every magnet has a north pole and a south pole. Like poles repel; opposite poles attract.

The space around a magnet where its force acts is the magnetic field. Iron filings sprinkled near a magnet show the field lines, going from north to south.

Earth is a giant magnet. A compass needle is a small magnet that lines up with Earth's magnetic field and points north. Earth's magnetic north pole is in the Canadian Arctic region.

An electromagnet is made by wrapping wire around an iron nail and sending electric current through it. It is a magnet you can turn on and off!

🔁More coils of wire = stronger
🔋More current = stronger
🔩An iron core = stronger

Electromagnets are used in scrapyard cranes, doorbells, speakers, electric motors and MRI machines.

Remember: like poles repel; electromagnets switch on and off with electricity.

Key words

  • magnetic pole: the end of a magnet where its force is strongest
  • magnetic field: the area around a magnet where its force acts
  • compass: a tool with a magnetic needle that points north
  • electromagnet: a magnet made by electric current in a coil of wire

See real-life examples

Scrapyard cranes

Huge electromagnets lift old cars. When the power is switched off, the cars drop.

Speakers and headphones

Speakers use an electromagnet and a permanent magnet to make a cone vibrate and create sound.

Northern navigation

Before GPS, explorers in Canada's North used compasses, but near the magnetic pole compasses become unreliable.
