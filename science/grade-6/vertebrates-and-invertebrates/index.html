Vertebrates and invertebrates

Learn what you need to know

Animals can be sorted by whether they have a backbone.

Vertebrates have a backbone. There are five groups:

🐟Fish: gills, scales, fins, live in water
🐸Amphibians: moist skin, live in water then on land
🦎Reptiles: dry scales, lay leathery eggs
🐦Birds: feathers, wings, lay hard-shelled eggs
🐻Mammals: hair or fur, feed babies milk

Invertebrates have no backbone. More than 95% of all animal species are invertebrates!

🐛Insects: 6 legs, 3 body parts
🕷️Arachnids: 8 legs (spiders, ticks)
🦀Crustaceans: hard shells (crabs, lobsters)
🪱Worms
🐌Molluscs: soft bodies, often shells (snails, octopuses)
🪼Jellyfish and sponges

Many invertebrates have an exoskeleton, a hard covering on the outside.

Remember: vertebrates have backbones; most animals are invertebrates.

Key words

  • vertebrate: an animal with a backbone
  • invertebrate: an animal without a backbone
  • exoskeleton: a hard covering on the outside of the body
  • mammal: a vertebrate with hair that feeds its young milk

See real-life examples

Octopus

An octopus has no bones at all, so it can squeeze through a hole the size of a coin.

Lobster

Atlantic lobsters have an exoskeleton. They must shed it (moult) to grow bigger.

Bats

Bats fly but are mammals, not birds. They have fur and feed their babies milk.
