Learn what you need to know

The Earth is made of layers, like a peach.

🥚Crust: thin rocky outer layer we live on
🔥Mantle: thick layer of hot rock that flows very slowly
🟠Outer core: liquid iron and nickel
⚪Inner core: solid iron and nickel, as hot as the Sun's surface

The crust is broken into giant pieces called tectonic plates. They float on the mantle and move a few centimetres a year, about as fast as your fingernails grow.

Where plates meet, we get earthquakes, volcanoes and mountains. The Rocky Mountains formed when plates pushed together.

Long ago, all the continents were joined in one supercontinent called Pangaea.

Remember: crust, mantle, outer core, inner core; moving plates cause earthquakes and volcanoes.

Key words

  • crust: the thin rocky outer layer of Earth
  • mantle: the thick layer of hot rock under the crust
  • core: the iron and nickel centre of Earth
  • tectonic plate: a giant moving piece of Earth's crust

See real-life examples

Iceland

Iceland sits where two plates pull apart, so it has many volcanoes and hot springs.

British Columbia

B.C. is near plate boundaries, so it has small earthquakes and is ready for big ones.

Puzzle continents

South America and Africa fit together like puzzle pieces. They were once joined in Pangaea.
