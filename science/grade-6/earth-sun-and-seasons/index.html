Earth, Sun and seasons

Why Earth has day, night, years and seasons.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 6 science.

Learn what you need to know

Earth rotates (spins) on its axis once every 24 hours. This gives us day and night.

Earth revolves (travels) around the Sun once every 365¼ days. That is one year. The extra quarter day adds up, so every 4 years we have a leap year with February 29.

Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5°. That tilt causes the seasons, not Earth's distance from the Sun.

☀️Summer: our half of Earth tilts toward the Sun; direct rays, long days
❄️Winter: our half tilts away; slanted rays, short days
🌷Spring and fall: neither half tilts toward the Sun; day and night are nearly equal

When it is summer in Canada, it is winter in Australia, because the other half of Earth tilts away.

Remember: rotation = day and night; revolution = a year; tilt = seasons.

Key words

  • rotate: to spin on an axis
  • revolve: to travel around another object
  • axis: an imaginary line through Earth from pole to pole
  • equinox: the day in spring or fall when day and night are about equal

See real-life examples

Longest day

The summer solstice, around June 21, is the longest day of the year in Canada.

Christmas in summer

In Australia, December is summer. Many people have Christmas at the beach.

Northern summer nights

In the Arctic, the Sun does not set for weeks in summer. This is called the midnight sun.
