Simple machines

Learn what you need to know

In Grade 4 you met the six simple machines. Now let's see how they help.

A machine gives mechanical advantage when it lets you use less force to move a load. But there is a trade-off: you must move a longer distance. A long ramp is easier to push up than a steep one, but you walk farther.

1️⃣First-class lever: fulcrum in the middle (seesaw, scissors)
2️⃣Second-class lever: load in the middle (wheelbarrow, nutcracker)
3️⃣Third-class lever: effort in the middle (tweezers, fishing rod, your forearm)

On a lever, the farther your effort is from the fulcrum, the less force you need. That is why long handles make jobs easier.

Machines can also change the direction of a force (a pulley) or increase speed (a bike's gears).

Remember: less force means more distance; that is the trade-off of every simple machine.

Key words

  • mechanical advantage: how much a machine multiplies your force
  • effort arm: the distance from the fulcrum to where you push
  • first-class lever: a lever with the fulcrum in the middle
  • trade-off: giving up one thing to get another

See real-life examples

Long wrench

A mechanic uses a long wrench to loosen a tight bolt. The long handle means less force is needed.

Wheelbarrow

A wheelbarrow is a second-class lever. The wheel is the fulcrum, the load is in the middle, and you lift the handles.

Your arm

Your forearm is a third-class lever. Your elbow is the fulcrum and your biceps pull between the elbow and your hand.
