Physical and chemical changes

Learn what you need to know

Matter can change in two ways.

A physical change changes the size, shape or state of a material, but it is still the same material. Cutting paper, melting ice, dissolving sugar and crushing a can are physical changes. Many can be reversed.

A chemical change makes a new substance with new properties. Burning, rusting, cooking, baking and digesting food are chemical changes. They are usually hard or impossible to reverse.

🎨New colour
👃New smell
🫧Gas bubbles
🔥Heat or light given off
🧊A solid forms in a liquid (precipitate)

These are the signs of a chemical change. If you see one, a new substance may have formed.

Remember: physical = same stuff, new look. Chemical = new stuff.

Key words

  • physical change: a change in size, shape or state, but not in what it is made of
  • chemical change: a change that makes a new substance
  • reversible: can be changed back
  • precipitate: a solid that forms when two liquids are mixed

See real-life examples

Baking soda volcano

Mixing baking soda and vinegar makes bubbles of a new gas, carbon dioxide. That is a chemical change.

Ice sculpture

An ice sculpture melts on a warm day. It is still water, just liquid now. That is a physical change.

Fireworks

Fireworks burn and give off light, heat, sound and smoke. Many new substances form, so it is a chemical change.
