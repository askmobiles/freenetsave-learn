Growing our food

Learn what you need to know

Almost all our food comes from farms. Canada grows wheat, canola, corn, soybeans, potatoes, apples and much more, and raises cattle, chickens, pigs and dairy cows.

🚜Prepare the soil: plough or loosen it
🌱Plant seeds at the right time and depth
💧Water: rain or irrigation
🧪Feed the soil: manure, compost or fertilizer
🐛Protect from weeds, pests and disease
🌾Harvest when ripe, then store safely

Crop rotation means growing different crops in a field each year. It keeps soil healthy and breaks pest cycles. Beans and peas even add nitrogen to the soil.

Science helps farmers: soil tests, weather forecasts, GPS tractors, greenhouses, and new crop varieties. Canadian scientists developed canola in the 1970s from rapeseed.

Farming must be sustainable: protect soil from erosion, use water wisely, keep fertilizer out of lakes, and protect bees and other pollinators.

Remember: healthy soil, water, sunlight and care grow our food.

Key words

  • crop: a plant grown by farmers for food or other uses
  • irrigation: watering crops with pipes, ditches or sprinklers
  • crop rotation: growing different crops in a field each year
  • harvest: gathering ripe crops

See real-life examples

Canola

Canola, a yellow-flowered crop, was developed by Canadian scientists. Its oil is used for cooking around the world.

Ontario greenhouses

Leamington, Ontario has huge greenhouses that grow tomatoes, peppers and cucumbers all year.

Prairie wheat

Saskatchewan and Alberta grow large amounts of wheat used to make bread and pasta.
