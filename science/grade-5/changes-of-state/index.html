Learn what you need to know

All matter is made of tiny particles that are always moving. How they move decides whether matter is a solid, liquid or gas.

🧊Solid: particles packed tight, only vibrate
💧Liquid: particles close but slide past each other
💨Gas: particles far apart, moving fast in all directions

Adding heat makes particles move faster. Removing heat slows them down. That causes changes of state:

🧊→💧Melting (solid to liquid)
💧→🧊Freezing (liquid to solid)
💧→💨Evaporation or boiling (liquid to gas)
💨→💧Condensation (gas to liquid)
🧊→💨Sublimation (solid straight to gas, like dry ice)

Water melts at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C.

Remember: add heat → particles speed up → solid to liquid to gas.

Key words

  • particle: a tiny piece of matter, too small to see
  • melting point: the temperature where a solid becomes a liquid
  • boiling point: the temperature where a liquid boils into gas
  • sublimation: a solid turning straight into a gas

See real-life examples

Dry ice

Dry ice is frozen carbon dioxide. It turns straight into a gas without melting, making spooky fog.

Frost on a window

On cold nights, water vapour turns straight into ice crystals on windows. That is frost.

Butter in a pan

Butter melts when the pan gets hot. Its particles start sliding around.
