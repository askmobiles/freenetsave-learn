Learn what you need to know

A property describes what a material is like. Engineers and designers pick materials by their properties.

💎Hardness: resists scratching
🧵Flexibility: bends without breaking
🏋️Strength: holds heavy loads
🪟Transparency: lets light through
🔥Heat conductivity: lets heat through
⚡Electrical conductivity: lets electricity through
💧Absorbency: soaks up liquids
🧲Magnetism: attracted to magnets

Matter also has properties you can measure: mass (how much matter), volume (how much space it takes up) and density (how tightly packed it is). Things denser than water sink; less dense things float.

Choosing the right material matters: towels must be absorbent, windows transparent, bridges strong, and pot handles poor heat conductors.

Remember: pick a material whose properties match the job.

Key words

  • property: a feature that describes a material
  • mass: the amount of matter in an object
  • volume: the amount of space an object takes up
  • density: how much mass is packed into a space

See real-life examples

Goalie gear

Hockey goalie pads are made of tough, light foam and plastic: strong enough to stop pucks, light enough to move fast.

Paper towels

Paper towels are designed to be very absorbent, so they soak up spills quickly.

Oil on water

Oil floats on water because it is less dense than water.
