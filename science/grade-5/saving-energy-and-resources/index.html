Learn what you need to know

We get energy from resources. Some can run out; some cannot.

☀️Renewable: solar, wind, water (hydro), geothermal, biomass
⛽Non-renewable: oil, natural gas, coal, uranium

Non-renewable resources took millions of years to form and will run out. Burning fossil fuels (oil, gas, coal) also releases carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change.

Renewable resources are replaced naturally. Ontario gets a lot of electricity from hydro (falling water) and nuclear power, plus wind and solar.

Conserving energy means using less: turn off lights, unplug chargers, use LED bulbs, take shorter showers, walk or bike, and set the heat a little lower. Remember the 3 Rs: reduce, reuse, recycle.

Remember: renewable resources come back; non-renewable ones run out. Use less of both.

Key words

  • renewable: can be replaced naturally
  • non-renewable: will run out and cannot be replaced quickly
  • fossil fuel: fuel made from ancient plants and animals: oil, gas, coal
  • conserve: use less to save something

Wind farms

LED bulbs

LED bulbs use about 75% less energy than old light bulbs and last much longer.

Solar panels on roofs

Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity. Some homes even send extra power back to the grid.
