Weather and climate

Learn what you need to know

Weather is what the air is like at one place and time: today it is rainy and 12 °C. Climate is the usual weather in a place over many years: Toronto has cold, snowy winters and warm summers.

Climate depends on:

🌐Latitude: places near the equator are warmer
⛰️Altitude: high mountains are colder
🌊Water: oceans and big lakes make winters milder and summers cooler
🌬️Wind and ocean currents

Canada has many climate regions: the mild, rainy West Coast, the dry Prairies, the cold Arctic, and the humid Great Lakes region.

Climate change is the long-term warming of Earth's climate. Burning fossil fuels adds greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide, which trap heat. Effects include melting Arctic ice, rising seas, and more extreme weather.

Remember: weather is today; climate is the long-term pattern.

Key words

  • weather: the air conditions at one place and time
  • climate: the usual weather of a place over many years
  • greenhouse gas: a gas that traps heat in the atmosphere
  • latitude: how far north or south of the equator a place is

See real-life examples

Vancouver vs Winnipeg

Vancouver is next to the ocean and has mild winters. Winnipeg is far from oceans and has very cold winters.

Arctic sea ice

Arctic sea ice is shrinking as the climate warms. Polar bears need sea ice to hunt seals.

Lake-effect snow

Cold winds blowing over the warmer Great Lakes pick up moisture and drop heavy snow on nearby towns.
