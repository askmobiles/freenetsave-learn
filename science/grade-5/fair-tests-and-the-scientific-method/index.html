Fair tests and the scientific method

Learn what you need to know

Scientists answer questions by doing investigations. The scientific method is a set of steps:

❓1. Ask a question
💡2. Make a hypothesis: a smart guess you can test
🧪3. Plan and do a fair test
📊4. Record and look at your results
✅5. Make a conclusion and share it

In a fair test, you change only one thing at a time. The thing you change is the independent variable. The thing you measure is the dependent variable. Everything else stays the same: these are the controlled variables.

Example: "Does the amount of sunlight change how tall a bean plant grows?" Change only sunlight. Measure height. Keep the same kind of seed, soil, water and pot.

Good scientists repeat tests, record results in tables and graphs, and stay safe: goggles, adult help with heat, and never tasting anything in a science test.

Remember: change one thing, measure one thing, keep everything else the same.

Key words

  • hypothesis: a smart guess that can be tested
  • fair test: a test where only one thing is changed
  • variable: something that can change in an investigation
  • conclusion: what you learned from the results

See real-life examples

Paper airplanes

To test which wing shape flies farthest, use the same paper, the same throw and the same room. Change only the wings.

Science fairs

Many Canadian students do projects for science fairs, using the scientific method to test their own questions.

Medicine tests

New medicines are tested fairly by comparing groups who get the medicine with groups who do not.
