Natural resources

Resources from nature and how to use them wisely. Grade 5 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 5 · Conservation of Energy and Resources

Learn what you need to know

A natural resource is something from nature that people use: water, air, soil, trees, minerals, fish, oil and sunlight.

🌲Forests: wood, paper, habitats
🐟Fish and seafood
⛏️Minerals and metals: nickel, gold, potash
🛢️Oil and natural gas
🌾Fertile soil for farming
💧Fresh water: Canada has about 20% of the world's fresh water

Canada is rich in natural resources. Many jobs come from forestry, mining, fishing, farming and energy.

Using resources has costs: mining and logging can harm habitats, and pollution can harm water and air. Sustainable use means meeting our needs today without using up what future generations need, for example by replanting trees, setting fishing limits and recycling metals.

Indigenous peoples have cared for the land for thousands of years, taking only what is needed and thinking of the next seven generations.

Remember: use natural resources sustainably so they last for the future.

Key words

  • natural resource: something from nature that people use
  • sustainable: used in a way that can last into the future
  • forestry: growing, managing and cutting forests
  • potash: a mineral used to make fertilizer

See real-life examples

Replanting forests

Forestry companies in Canada must replant trees after logging, so new forests can grow.

Atlantic cod

In 1992, too much fishing made cod nearly disappear off Newfoundland. A fishing ban was needed to let them recover.

Potash in Saskatchewan

Saskatchewan mines a large share of the world's potash, used to help crops grow.
