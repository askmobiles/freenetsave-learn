Breathing and blood

How the lungs and heart team up to deliver oxygen.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 5 science.

Learn what you need to know

Every cell in your body needs oxygen. The respiratory system brings it in, and the circulatory system delivers it.

👃Air enters your nose or mouth
🫁It travels down the windpipe (trachea) into your lungs
🫧Tiny air sacs (alveoli) pass oxygen into the blood
❤️The heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body
🔁Blood brings carbon dioxide back to the lungs to breathe out

The diaphragm is a dome-shaped muscle under your lungs. When it moves down, air rushes in. When it moves up, air is pushed out.

Blood travels in blood vessels: arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins carry it back, and tiny capillaries connect them. Your heart has four chambers and beats about 70–100 times a minute at rest.

Remember: lungs bring in oxygen; the heart pumps it out; arteries away, veins back.

Key words

  • diaphragm: the muscle under the lungs that helps you breathe
  • alveoli: tiny air sacs in the lungs
  • artery: a blood vessel that carries blood away from the heart
  • vein: a blood vessel that carries blood back to the heart

See real-life examples

Count your pulse

Count your pulse for 15 seconds and multiply by 4. Then do 30 jumping jacks and count again. It goes up!

Fogging a mirror

Breathe on a cold mirror. It fogs because the air you breathe out is warm and moist.

Blood donation

Donated blood can save lives after accidents or surgery. Canadian Blood Services collects it.
