Acids and bases: pH and neutralization

Learn what you need to know

An Arrhenius acid releases H⁺ ions in water (HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻). An Arrhenius base releases OH⁻ ions (NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻).

pH measures acidity: pH = −log[H⁺]. Below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, above 7 is basic. Each step of 1 is a 10× change: pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4.

🍋pH 2: lemon juice
☕pH 5: coffee
💧pH 7: pure water
🧼pH 10: soap
🧪pH 13: drain cleaner

Strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) ionize completely. Weak acids (vinegar's acetic acid) ionize only a little. Strong ≠ concentrated: a dilute strong acid can be less harmful than a concentrated weak one.

Neutralization: acid + base → salt + water. For titration, moles of H⁺ = moles of OH⁻ at the end point: c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ for 1:1 reactions.

Example: 25.0 mL of NaOH is neutralized by 20.0 mL of 0.100 mol/L HCl. c(NaOH) = (0.100 × 20.0) ÷ 25.0 = 0.0800 mol/L.

Key words

  • pH: a number from 0 to 14 showing how acidic or basic a solution is
  • strong acid: an acid that ionizes completely in water
  • neutralization: acid + base → salt + water
  • titration: adding a solution of known concentration to find an unknown concentration

See real-life examples

Antacids

Antacid tablets are weak bases that neutralize extra stomach acid.

Pool chemistry

Pool water is kept near pH 7.4 so it is safe for eyes and keeps chlorine working.

Acid rain

Sulfur and nitrogen oxides from burning fuels make rain acidic, harming lakes and forests.
