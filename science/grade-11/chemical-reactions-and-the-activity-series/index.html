Chemical reactions and the activity series

Learn what you need to know

SCH3U builds on the five reaction types. Now you predict whether a reaction happens and what forms.

Activity series (most to least reactive metals): K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Ni, Sn, Pb, (H), Cu, Ag, Au. A metal displaces any metal below it. Metals above hydrogen react with acids to make H₂ gas.

✅Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (Zn above Cu)
❌Cu + ZnSO₄ → no reaction (Cu below Zn)
🫧Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ (Mg above H)
❌Au + HCl → no reaction (gold is below H)

Double displacement happens if a precipitate, a gas or water forms. Use solubility rules: all nitrates and all Group 1 and ammonium compounds dissolve; most chlorides dissolve except those of Ag, Pb and Hg(I); most carbonates and hydroxides do not dissolve.

Net ionic equations show only the ions that change. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃ becomes Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s). Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ are spectator ions.

Halogens have their own series: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. A higher halogen displaces a lower one from its salt.

Key words

  • activity series: a list of metals from most to least reactive
  • spectator ion: an ion that is present but does not change in a reaction
  • net ionic equation: an equation showing only the particles that react
  • solubility rules: guidelines that predict which ionic compounds dissolve in water

See real-life examples

Galvanized steel

Zinc coatings protect steel because zinc reacts first, sacrificing itself before iron rusts.

Gold jewellery

Gold is at the bottom of the activity series, so it does not react with air, water or most acids.

Hard water

Calcium ions form a solid carbonate precipitate that builds up as scale in kettles.
