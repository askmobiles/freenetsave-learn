Genetics and Punnett squares

Learn what you need to know

Mendel's laws: the law of segregation says the two alleles for a gene separate when gametes form, so each gamete gets one. The law of independent assortment says genes for different traits (on different chromosomes) are sorted independently.

🧬Genotype: the alleles (TT, Tt, tt)
👁️Phenotype: the trait you see (tall, short)
🟰Homozygous: two identical alleles (TT or tt)
↔️Heterozygous: two different alleles (Tt)

Monohybrid cross Tt × Tt: genotype ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt; phenotype ratio 3 tall : 1 short.

Dihybrid cross TtYy × TtYy gives a phenotype ratio of 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. A test cross with a homozygous recessive reveals an unknown genotype.

🌸Incomplete dominance: blend (red × white snapdragons → pink)
🐄Codominance: both show (roan cattle; AB blood type)
🩸Multiple alleles: ABO blood types (IA, IB, i)
👦Sex-linked: genes on the X chromosome; red-green colour blindness is more common in males

Remember: Tt × Tt → 3 : 1; TtYy × TtYy → 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.

Key words

  • genotype: the alleles an organism has
  • phenotype: the trait that can be observed
  • heterozygous: having two different alleles for a gene
  • test cross: crossing with a homozygous recessive to find a genotype

See real-life examples

Blood types

Blood type AB shows codominance: both A and B markers appear on red blood cells.

Colour blindness

Because the gene is on the X chromosome, a son can inherit colour blindness from a mother who has normal vision.

Plant breeding

Canadian wheat breeders use crosses to combine traits like disease resistance and early ripening for short prairie summers.
