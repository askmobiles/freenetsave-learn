Plants: structure and growth

Learn what you need to know

Flowering plants are grouped as monocots and dicots:

🌾Monocots: one seed leaf, parallel leaf veins, scattered vascular bundles, flower parts in 3s, fibrous roots (grasses, corn, lilies)
🌳Dicots: two seed leaves, net-like veins, vascular bundles in a ring, flower parts in 4s or 5s, taproots (beans, maples, roses)

Transport: water moves up the xylem by root pressure and transpiration pull (cohesion and adhesion of water). Sugars move in the phloem from sources (leaves) to sinks (roots, fruits, growing tips).

Growth: primary growth at apical meristems makes plants taller and roots longer. Secondary growth from the vascular cambium makes stems wider, forming tree rings.

🌞Auxins: promote cell elongation; cause phototropism
📏Gibberellins: stem growth and seed germination
🍌Ethylene: fruit ripening
🛑Abscisic acid: closes stomata, seed dormancy

Tropisms are growth responses: phototropism (toward light), gravitropism (roots down, shoots up), thigmotropism (touch, like vines curling).

Remember: monocots in 3s, dicots in 4s or 5s; auxins bend plants toward light.

Key words

  • monocot: a flowering plant with one seed leaf
  • dicot: a flowering plant with two seed leaves
  • vascular cambium: tissue that makes stems grow wider
  • tropism: a growth response toward or away from a stimulus

See real-life examples

Tree rings

Each ring in a tree trunk is one year of secondary growth, so counting rings gives the tree's age.

Ripening bananas

Putting a banana with green fruit in a paper bag speeds ripening, because bananas release ethylene.

Houseplants

A plant on a windowsill bends toward the window. Auxin builds up on the shaded side, making those cells grow longer.
