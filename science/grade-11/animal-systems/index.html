Animal systems: digestion, circulation and gas exchange

How mammals break down food, move blood and exchange gases, and how other animals do it.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 science.

Learn what you need to know

Large animals need systems to bring food and oxygen to every cell and take wastes away. Three key systems work together.

🍎Digestive: break food into small molecules and absorb them
❤️Circulatory: pump blood carrying O₂, nutrients and wastes
🫁Respiratory: take in O₂ and remove CO₂

Digestion: mechanical (chewing, churning) and chemical (enzymes). Amylase starts on starch in the mouth, pepsin on proteins in the stomach, and lipase on fats in the small intestine. Villi in the small intestine give a huge surface for absorption.

Circulation: mammals have a four-chambered heart and double circulation: the right side pumps blood to the lungs, the left side to the body. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins return it, capillaries exchange materials.

Gas exchange happens in the alveoli: tiny air sacs with thin walls and many capillaries. Fish use gills; insects use tubes called tracheae; earthworms breathe through moist skin.

Theme: every exchange surface is large, thin and moist, with a good blood supply.

Key words

  • villi: finger-like folds in the small intestine that absorb nutrients
  • alveoli: tiny air sacs in the lungs where gas exchange happens
  • double circulation: blood passes through the heart twice: once for the lungs, once for the body
  • capillary: a tiny blood vessel where materials pass between blood and cells

See real-life examples

Running a race

Your breathing and heart rate rise together to deliver more oxygen to your muscles and remove CO₂.

Lactose intolerance

People who make little lactase cannot fully digest milk sugar, so gut bacteria ferment it.

Fish out of water

Gills collapse in air and their surface shrinks, so fish cannot get enough oxygen.
