Solutions and concentration

Solubility, molar concentration, dilution and water quality. Grade 11 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 11 · Chemistry (SCH3U): Solutions and Solubility

Learn what you need to know

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of a solute dissolved in a solvent. Water is a polar solvent, so "like dissolves like": polar and ionic solutes dissolve well in water; non-polar ones (oils) do not.

Solubility depends on temperature: most solids dissolve more in hot water; gases dissolve less in warm water (warm pop goes flat). A saturated solution holds the maximum solute.

molar concentration c = n ÷ V (mol/L)

Example: 0.50 mol of NaCl in 2.0 L of solution gives c = 0.50 ÷ 2.0 = 0.25 mol/L.

dilution: c₁V₁ = c₂V₂

Example: to make 500 mL of 0.20 mol/L from a 1.0 mol/L stock: V₁ = (0.20 × 500) ÷ 1.0 = 100 mL of stock, then add water to 500 mL.

Small concentrations use ppm (parts per million, mg/L in water). Drinking-water rules set limits such as for lead. Water treatment uses coagulation, filtration and disinfection.

Remember: c = n ÷ V, and c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ for dilution.

Key words

  • solubility: the maximum amount of solute that dissolves at a given temperature
  • molar concentration: moles of solute per litre of solution
  • dilution: adding solvent to lower concentration
  • ppm: parts per million; mg per litre of water

See real-life examples

Hospital IV bags

Saline IV bags hold about 0.9% sodium chloride, close to the salt concentration of blood.

Lead in water

Some older Canadian homes have lead pipes. Water testing checks that lead stays below safe limits.

Juice concentrate

Frozen juice is diluted with water using the same idea as c₁V₁ = c₂V₂.
