Work, energy and power

Learn what you need to know

work W = F × Δd (when force is along the motion)

Work is measured in joules (J). Pushing a box with 50 N for 4.0 m does 200 J of work.

kinetic energy E_k = ½mv² gravitational potential energy E_p = mgh

A 2.0 kg ball moving at 3.0 m/s has E_k = ½(2.0)(3.0)² = 9.0 J. Lifting a 10 kg box up 2.0 m gives E_p = 10 × 9.8 × 2.0 = 196 J.

Law of conservation of energy: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. A roller coaster turns E_p into E_k as it falls; some becomes heat and sound through friction.

power P = W ÷ Δt (watts) efficiency = useful output ÷ total input × 100%

Energy and society: Ontario generates electricity from nuclear, hydro, gas, wind and solar. Each has costs and benefits for the environment and society.

Remember: W = FΔd, E_k = ½mv², E_p = mgh, P = W ÷ Δt.

Key words

  • work: energy transferred by a force moving an object
  • kinetic energy: energy of motion
  • gravitational potential energy: energy stored by height
  • power: the rate of doing work

See real-life examples

Roller coasters

Behemoth at Canada's Wonderland climbs about 70 m, storing potential energy that becomes speed on the drop.

Stairs vs elevator

Running up stairs takes the same work as walking, but more power because it is done faster.

Brakes and heat

When a car brakes, its kinetic energy becomes heat in the brakes.
