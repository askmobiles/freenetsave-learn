Electricity and magnetism

Current, voltage and resistance in circuits, magnetic fields, motors, generators and transformers. Grade 11 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 11 · Physics (SPH3U): Electricity and Magnetism

Learn what you need to know

Current I = Q ÷ Δt (amperes), voltage (potential difference) V = E ÷ Q (volts), and Ohm's law V = IR. In series, R_T = R₁ + R₂; in parallel, 1/R_T = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ (Kirchhoff's laws: currents add at junctions; voltages add around a loop).

Moving charges create magnetic fields (Ørsted, 1820). Right-hand rules give the direction: grip the wire with the thumb along the current and fingers curl with the field. A coil (solenoid) makes a field like a bar magnet: an electromagnet.

🔄Motor principle: a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force; electric motors turn electrical energy into motion
⚡Electromagnetic induction (Faraday): a changing magnetic field induces a current; generators turn motion into electricity
🔌Transformer: Vₚ/Vₛ = Nₚ/Nₛ; steps voltage up or down using AC

Lenz's law: an induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change that caused it. Power plants make alternating current (AC); batteries supply direct current (DC).

Example: a transformer with 100 primary turns and 500 secondary turns steps 120 V up to 120 × 500 ÷ 100 = 600 V.

Remember: current makes magnetism (motors); changing magnetism makes current (generators).

Key words

  • electromagnetic induction: producing current with a changing magnetic field
  • solenoid: a coil of wire that acts like a magnet when current flows
  • transformer: a device that changes AC voltage
  • alternating current: current that changes direction back and forth

See real-life examples

Power grid

Ontario power stations step voltage up to as high as 500 000 V for transmission, then transformers step it down to 120/240 V for homes.

Electric vehicles

EV motors use the motor principle, and when braking they act as generators to recharge the battery (regenerative braking).

Induction stoves

An induction cooktop uses a changing magnetic field to induce currents that heat the metal pot directly.
