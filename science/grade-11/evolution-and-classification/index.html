Evolution and classification

Natural selection, evidence for evolution, speciation, and how scientists classify life.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 11 science.

Learn what you need to know

Natural selection (Darwin and Wallace): individuals vary; more are born than survive; those with advantageous heritable traits survive and reproduce more; over generations the population changes. Artificial selection is when humans choose the traits, as in dog and crop breeding.

Evidence for evolution: the fossil record, homologous structures (same bones, different jobs: human arm, bat wing), vestigial structures (the human appendix, whale hip bones), embryology, DNA comparisons, and observed changes like antibiotic resistance.

Speciation happens when populations become reproductively isolated (for example, by geography) and change until they can no longer interbreed.

Classification (taxonomy): Carl Linnaeus created binomial nomenclature (Genus species, like Homo sapiens). The levels are: Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species ("Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup").

🦠Domain Bacteria
🔥Domain Archaea (often extreme environments)
🧬Domain Eukarya: protists, fungi, plants, animals

Phylogenetic trees (cladograms) show evolutionary relationships, based on shared traits and DNA.

Remember: Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species.

Key words

  • homologous structure: a body part with the same basic structure in different species
  • vestigial structure: a reduced body part with little or no function
  • speciation: the formation of a new species
  • binomial nomenclature: the two-part scientific naming system
