Limiting reactant and percent yield

Stoichiometry: mole ratios, limiting and excess reactants, and percent yield. Grade 11 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 11 · Chemistry (SCH3U): Quantities in Chemical Reactions

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

A balanced equation gives the mole ratio of reactants and products. In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 2 mol of H₂ react with 1 mol of O₂ to make 2 mol of H₂O.

1️⃣Convert grams to moles (n = m ÷ M)
2️⃣Use the mole ratio from the balanced equation
3️⃣Convert moles back to grams (m = n × M)

The limiting reactant runs out first and decides how much product forms. The other is the excess reactant. Example: 4 mol H₂ and 3 mol O₂. 4 mol H₂ needs only 2 mol O₂, so H₂ is limiting; 4 mol H₂O form and 1 mol O₂ is left over.

The theoretical yield is the maximum product calculated. The actual yield is what you really get.

percent yield = actual yield ÷ theoretical yield × 100%

Yields are less than 100% because of incomplete reactions, side reactions and losses when transferring or filtering.

Remember: grams → moles → mole ratio → moles → grams; the limiting reactant sets the yield.

Key words

  • stoichiometry: calculating amounts of substances in reactions
  • limiting reactant: the reactant that runs out first
  • theoretical yield: the maximum possible amount of product
  • percent yield: actual ÷ theoretical × 100%

See real-life examples

Baking

If a recipe needs 2 eggs per cake and you have 6 eggs but flour for only 2 cakes, flour is the "limiting reactant".

Fertilizer plants

Ammonia factories use excess nitrogen or hydrogen on purpose to make sure the more costly reactant is fully used.

Medicines

Drug makers track percent yield closely; a few percent improvement can save huge amounts of money.
