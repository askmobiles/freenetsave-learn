Learn what you need to know

Taxonomy is the science of naming and grouping living things. Scientists group organisms by shared features and by how closely they are related (their evolutionary history).

From broad to specific: Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. Memory trick: "Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup".

🦠Bacteria: prokaryotes, no nucleus
🔥Archaea: prokaryotes, often in extreme places
🧬Eukarya: cells with a nucleus — protists, fungi, plants, animals

Binomial nomenclature gives every species a two-part Latin name: Genus species. Humans are Homo sapiens. The genus is capitalized; both words are in italics.

A dichotomous key identifies an organism by answering a series of two-choice questions. A cladogram is a branching diagram that shows shared ancestors.

Viruses are not in any domain: they are not made of cells and cannot reproduce on their own.

Key words

  • taxonomy: the science of classifying and naming organisms
  • binomial nomenclature: two-part scientific name: Genus species
  • dichotomous key: a tool of paired choices for identifying organisms
  • prokaryote: a single-celled organism without a nucleus

See real-life examples

Canada lynx

Lynx canadensis: the same genus as the bobcat (Lynx rufus), so they are closely related.

Mushrooms

Fungi were once grouped with plants, but they cannot photosynthesize and are actually closer to animals.

Hot springs

Many archaea live in boiling springs where almost nothing else survives.
