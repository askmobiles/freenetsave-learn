Motion

Displacement, velocity, acceleration, motion graphs and the kinematic equations. Grade 11 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 11 · Physics (SPH3U): Kinematics

Learn what you need to know

Scalars have only size (distance, speed, time). Vectors have size and direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration). Example: walking 5 m east then 5 m west gives a distance of 10 m but a displacement of 0 m.

average velocity v = Δd ÷ Δt acceleration a = Δv ÷ Δt

A car going from 0 to 20 m/s in 5.0 s has a = 20 ÷ 5.0 = 4.0 m/s².

Motion graphs: on a position–time graph, the slope is velocity. On a velocity–time graph, the slope is acceleration and the area under the graph is displacement.

1️⃣v₂ = v₁ + aΔt
2️⃣Δd = v₁Δt + ½aΔt²
3️⃣v₂² = v₁² + 2aΔd
4️⃣Δd = ½(v₁ + v₂)Δt

Objects in free fall near Earth accelerate at about 9.8 m/s² downward (ignoring air resistance).

Remember: slope of d–t = velocity; slope of v–t = acceleration; area of v–t = displacement.

Key words

  • displacement: change in position, with direction
  • velocity: rate of change of displacement
  • acceleration: rate of change of velocity
  • free fall: motion under gravity alone

See real-life examples

Highway speed

100 km/h is about 27.8 m/s. At that speed a car covers almost a football field every 4 seconds.

Reaction time

At 50 km/h, a driver looking at a phone for 2 seconds travels about 28 m without watching the road.

Drop tower

At Canada's Wonderland, drop rides let riders feel free fall, speeding up about 9.8 m/s every second.
