Forces and Newton's laws

Free-body diagrams, net force, weight, friction and Newton's three laws. Grade 11 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 11 · Physics (SPH3U): Forces

Learn what you need to know

A force is a push or pull, measured in newtons (N). A free-body diagram shows all the forces on one object as arrows.

⬇️Gravity (weight): F_g = mg, with g = 9.8 N/kg
⬆️Normal force: a surface pushing back, perpendicular to it
⬅️Friction: opposes motion; F_f = μF_N
🪢Tension: pull through a rope or cable
➡️Applied force: a push or pull

Newton's first law (inertia): an object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity unless a net force acts on it.

Newton's second law: F_net = m × a

Newton's third law: for every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force, acting on a different object.

Example: a 50 kg crate is pushed with 200 N; friction is 50 N. F_net = 200 − 50 = 150 N, so a = 150 ÷ 50 = 3.0 m/s². Its weight is 50 × 9.8 = 490 N.

Static friction keeps objects from starting to slide; kinetic friction acts on sliding objects and is usually smaller. μ is the coefficient of friction.

Remember: add the forces to find F_net, then use a = F_net ÷ m.

Key words

  • net force: the sum of all forces on an object
  • normal force: the perpendicular push of a surface
  • coefficient of friction: μ, a number describing how much two surfaces resist sliding
  • free-body diagram: a sketch showing all forces on one object

See real-life examples

Winter tires

Winter tires have softer rubber and deeper treads, increasing friction on snow and ice.

Seat belts

In a crash, inertia keeps your body moving; the seat belt supplies the net force to slow you safely.

Rockets

Rocket engines push exhaust gas down; the gas pushes the rocket up with an equal force.
