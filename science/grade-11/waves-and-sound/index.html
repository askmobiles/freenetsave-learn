Waves and sound

Wave types and properties, the speed of sound, interference, resonance and the Doppler effect. Grade 11 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 11 · Physics (SPH3U): Waves and Sound

Learn what you need to know

A wave carries energy without carrying matter. Transverse waves vibrate perpendicular to the direction of travel (waves on a string, light). Longitudinal waves vibrate parallel to it, making compressions and rarefactions (sound).

v = f × λ f = 1 ÷ T

Sound travels about 343 m/s in air at 20 °C (v ≈ 331 + 0.6T), faster in water (about 1500 m/s) and faster still in steel. A 343 Hz note in air has λ = 343 ÷ 343 = 1.0 m.

🔊Pitch depends on frequency; humans hear about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz
📢Loudness depends on amplitude, measured in decibels (dB); +10 dB is 10× the intensity
🦇Ultrasound: above 20 000 Hz; infrasound: below 20 Hz

Interference: waves that meet add up. Constructive (crests meet crests) makes bigger waves; destructive (crest meets trough) cancels, as in noise-cancelling headphones. Resonance happens when something is pushed at its natural frequency, making large vibrations.

The Doppler effect: a sound source moving toward you sounds higher in pitch; moving away, lower, as with a passing siren.

Remember: v = fλ; sound is longitudinal and needs a medium.

Key words

  • longitudinal wave: a wave that vibrates parallel to its direction of travel
  • decibel: the unit for sound intensity level
  • resonance: large vibrations when forced at a natural frequency
  • Doppler effect: a change in pitch due to a moving source

See real-life examples

Noise-cancelling headphones

They record outside noise and play the opposite wave, cancelling it by destructive interference.

Hearing protection

Long exposure above about 85 dB, like loud concerts or power tools, can permanently damage hearing.

Ambulance sirens

A siren sounds higher as it approaches and drops in pitch as it passes: the Doppler effect.
