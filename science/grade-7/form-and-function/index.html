Form and function

How a structure's shape and materials match its job. Grade 7 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 7 · Form, Function, and Design of Structures

Learn what you need to know

Form is the shape and size of a structure. Function is the job it does. Good design matches form to function.

🏛️Solid structures: made of one solid piece (a dam, a brick wall)
🦴Frame structures: a skeleton of parts joined together (a bike, a house frame, a tower)
🥚Shell structures: a thin outer layer holding its shape (an egg, a helmet, a tent)

Engineers think about loads, the centre of gravity (the point where weight seems to act), and the materials. A lower centre of gravity and a wide base make a structure more stable.

Structures can fail by bending, buckling, twisting or breaking. Engineers add a safety factor: they design structures to hold much more than the expected load.

Good design also thinks about cost, safety, how it looks, and its effect on the environment.

Remember: the shape and materials of a structure must suit its job.

Key words

  • form: the shape and size of a structure
  • function: the job a structure does
  • centre of gravity: the point where all the weight of an object seems to act
  • safety factor: designing a structure to hold more than it needs to

See real-life examples

CN Tower

The CN Tower is a tall frame and concrete structure with a wide base and heavy lower part, giving it a low centre of gravity.

Bike helmets

A bike helmet is a shell structure. Its curved, hard shell spreads a hit over a large area.

Kayaks

A kayak is long and narrow (form) so it moves fast and straight through water (function).
