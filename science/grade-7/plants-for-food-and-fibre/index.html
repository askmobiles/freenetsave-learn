Plants for food and fibre

How farmers grow plants for food, clothing and building, and how science helps crops grow well without harming the land.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 7 science.

Learn what you need to know

People use plants for food (wheat, canola, potatoes, apples) and for fibre: materials like cotton and flax for cloth, wood for lumber and paper, and hemp for rope.

🌱Plants need light, water, carbon dioxide, nutrients from the soil and the right temperature.
🧪Soil gives nutrients: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K). Fertilizer adds them back.
🌾Crops in Canada: wheat, canola, barley, oats, lentils, corn and soybeans.

Growing more food: farmers choose seed varieties bred for strong yields and short Canadian summers. Canola was developed in Canada in the 1970s from rapeseed by plant breeders.

Protecting crops: weeds, insects and diseases can cut harvests. Farmers use crop rotation (changing crops each year), pesticides and integrated pest management (using natural enemies and careful timing so fewer chemicals are needed).

Caring for the soil: no-till farming leaves old stalks in the field, so wind and water erode less soil and more moisture stays in the ground. Too much fertilizer can wash into lakes and cause algae blooms.

Sustainable farming means growing what we need today while keeping soil, water and living things healthy for the future.

Key words

  • fibre: a plant material used to make cloth, rope, paper or lumber
  • crop rotation: growing different crops in a field from year to year
  • no-till: planting without ploughing, to protect the soil
  • integrated pest management: controlling pests with several methods so fewer chemicals are needed
