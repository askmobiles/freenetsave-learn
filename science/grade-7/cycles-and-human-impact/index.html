Cycles and human impact

The water, carbon and nitrogen cycles, and how people change them. Grade 7 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 7 · Interactions in the Environment

Learn what you need to know

Matter on Earth is recycled again and again in natural cycles.

💧Water cycle: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection
🌳Carbon cycle: plants take in CO₂; animals, decomposers and burning release it
🌾Nitrogen cycle: bacteria in soil change nitrogen into forms plants can use

Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down dead things and return nutrients to the soil, keeping the cycles going.

Humans change these cycles. Burning fossil fuels adds extra carbon dioxide, causing climate change. Too much fertilizer washes into lakes and causes algae blooms that use up oxygen. Paving land stops rain from soaking in.

We can help by using less energy, planting trees, using less fertilizer, protecting wetlands and composting.

Remember: nature recycles water, carbon and nitrogen; human actions can upset the balance.

Key words

  • carbon cycle: the movement of carbon between air, living things, oceans and rocks
  • nitrogen cycle: the movement of nitrogen between air, soil and living things
  • decomposer: a living thing that breaks down dead matter
  • algae bloom: a fast overgrowth of algae caused by extra nutrients

See real-life examples

Lake Erie algae

Fertilizer runoff from farms has caused huge green algae blooms in Lake Erie, harming fish and drinking water.

Composting

A backyard compost bin lets decomposers turn food scraps into rich soil, returning nutrients to the ground.

Wetlands

Wetlands act like sponges. They soak up rain, slow floods and filter water.
