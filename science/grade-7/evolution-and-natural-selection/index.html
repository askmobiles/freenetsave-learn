Evolution and natural selection

How living things change over many generations, and the evidence. Grade 7 science.

⭐ Extra topic · also taught in BC Grade 7, England Year 6 and US middle school (NGSS)

Learn what you need to know

Living things in a species are not all the same. These differences are called variations, and many are passed from parents to young.

Natural selection, described by Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace, works like this:

👪1. Animals and plants have more young than can survive
🎨2. The young show variation
🏆3. Those with helpful traits for their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce
🔁4. Helpful traits become more common over many generations

Over very long periods, this process, called evolution, can produce new species. That explains the great diversity of life on Earth.

Evidence for evolution includes fossils (showing life changed over time), similar body structures in different animals, DNA comparisons, and changes we can watch today, like bacteria becoming resistant to antibiotics.

Remember: variation + survival of the best-suited + many generations = evolution.

Key words

  • variation: differences between members of a species
  • natural selection: the process where better-suited living things survive and reproduce more
  • evolution: the change in living things over many generations
  • adaptation: a trait that helps a living thing survive in its environment

See real-life examples

Darwin's finches

On the Galápagos Islands, finches have different beak shapes suited to the foods on each island.

Fossils in Alberta

Alberta's Royal Tyrrell Museum displays dinosaur fossils that show how life on Earth has changed over millions of years.

Antibiotic resistance

If people stop antibiotics early, the toughest bacteria can survive and multiply. That is natural selection in action.
