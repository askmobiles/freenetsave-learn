Photosynthesis and respiration

How plants make food, and how all living things release energy from it. Grade 7 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria make their own food. It happens in the chloroplasts, using the green pigment chlorophyll.

carbon dioxide + water + light energy → glucose (sugar) + oxygen

Plants take in carbon dioxide through tiny holes in their leaves called stomata, and water through their roots.

Cellular respiration is how living things (plants AND animals) release energy from food. It happens in the mitochondria.

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

Notice that the two processes are opposites. Photosynthesis stores energy in sugar; respiration releases it. Together they cycle carbon and oxygen between plants and animals.

Remember: photosynthesis makes food and oxygen; respiration uses them to release energy.

Key words

  • photosynthesis: the process plants use to make sugar from light, water and carbon dioxide
  • cellular respiration: the process cells use to release energy from sugar
  • chlorophyll: the green pigment that captures light
  • stomata: tiny openings in leaves that let gases in and out

See real-life examples

Fresh air from forests and oceans

Tiny algae in the ocean make about half of the oxygen we breathe.

Plants at night

At night, there is no light for photosynthesis, but plants keep doing respiration all the time.

Running

When you run, your muscle cells do respiration faster, so you breathe harder to get more oxygen.
