Heat and temperature

The difference between heat and temperature, and how we measure them.⁠​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌​​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌​​‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌‌​‌‌​​‌‌​​‌​‌⁠ Grade 7 science.

Learn what you need to know

Temperature measures how fast the particles in a substance are moving on average. Faster particles = higher temperature. We measure it with a thermometer in degrees Celsius (°C).

Heat (thermal energy) is the total energy of all the particles. It depends on the temperature and how much matter there is.

Example: a bathtub of warm water (40 °C) has more heat than a cup of boiling water (100 °C), because the bathtub has so many more particles.

🧊0 °C: water freezes
🌡️37 °C: normal body temperature
♨️100 °C: water boils (at sea level)
🥶−273 °C: absolute zero, the coldest possible

Different materials heat up at different rates. Water takes a lot of energy to warm up, so lakes stay cool in summer and warm in fall.

Remember: temperature is how hot; heat is how much total thermal energy.

Key words

  • temperature: a measure of how fast particles are moving on average
  • heat: the total thermal energy of all the particles
  • thermometer: a tool that measures temperature
  • absolute zero: the coldest possible temperature, about −273 °C

See real-life examples

Lake swimming

Lakes are still cold in June because water heats up slowly. By September, they are warmer than the air.

Iceberg vs match

An iceberg has more total heat than a burning match, because it has so much more matter, even though the match is hotter.

Fever

A thermometer reading above about 38 °C usually means a fever.
