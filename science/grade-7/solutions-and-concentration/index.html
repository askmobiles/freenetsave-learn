Solutions and concentration

Learn what you need to know

The concentration of a solution tells how much solute is dissolved in a certain amount of solvent. A concentrated solution has a lot of solute; a dilute solution has a little.

We can measure concentration in grams per litre (g/L). For example, 20 g of sugar in 1 L of water is 20 g/L.

Solubility is the most solute that can dissolve in a solvent at a certain temperature. When no more can dissolve, the solution is saturated. If more could still dissolve, it is unsaturated.

🔥Heating the solvent (for most solids)
🥄Stirring or shaking
🔨Crushing the solute into smaller pieces

These three things make solids dissolve faster. Heat also lets most solids dissolve more. But gases are the opposite: warm pop goes flat faster because less gas stays dissolved.

Remember: concentration = amount of solute ÷ amount of solution.

Key words

  • concentration: the amount of solute in a certain amount of solution
  • solubility: the most solute that can dissolve at a certain temperature
  • saturated: holding as much solute as it can
  • dilute: having only a little solute

See real-life examples

Iced tea

Sugar dissolves much faster in hot tea than in iced tea. That is why people make sweet tea hot, then cool it.

Warm pop

An open can of pop goes flat faster on a hot day because gas is less soluble in warm liquid.

Juice concentrate

Frozen juice concentrate is very concentrated. You add water to dilute it before drinking.
