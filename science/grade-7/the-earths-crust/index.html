The Earth's crust

Learn what you need to know

The crust is Earth's thin, rocky outer layer. There are two kinds: thick, light continental crust under the land, and thin, dense oceanic crust under the oceans.

The crust is made of rocks, and rocks are made of minerals. Minerals are identified by properties like colour, streak, lustre (shine), and hardness on the Mohs scale (1 talc, 10 diamond).

🏔️Mountain building: plates collide and push rock upward
🌋Volcanoes: magma rises through the crust
💥Earthquakes: stress builds and rock suddenly breaks along faults
🌊Weathering and erosion: water, wind and ice wear the crust down

The crust is always being built up by plate movement and volcanoes, and worn down by weathering and erosion.

Many resources come from the crust: metals, salt, gravel, oil and gas. Canada is a world leader in mining.

Remember: the crust is built up from inside and worn down from outside.

Key words

  • mineral: a natural solid with a definite chemical makeup and crystal structure
  • Mohs scale: a 1-to-10 scale of mineral hardness
  • fault: a crack in the crust where rocks move
  • lustre: how a mineral reflects light

See real-life examples

Sudbury Basin

The Sudbury Basin in Ontario was formed by a huge meteorite impact 1.8 billion years ago. Today it is a major nickel mine.

Niagara Falls

The falls are slowly moving upstream as water erodes the rock under them.

Mount Everest

The Himalayas are still rising as the Indian plate pushes into Asia.
