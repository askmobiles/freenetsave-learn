Heat transfer

Conduction, convection and radiation, and how we control heat flow. Grade 7 science.

📘 Ontario Grade 7 · Heat in the Environment

Learn what you need to know

Heat always moves from a warmer object to a cooler one. There are three ways heat moves:

🍳Conduction: heat moves through direct contact (a metal spoon in hot soup)
🌀Convection: heat moves by currents in liquids and gases (warm air rising, cool air sinking)
☀️Radiation: heat moves as invisible waves, even through empty space (sunlight, a campfire)

Conductors (metals) let heat pass easily. Insulators (wood, plastic, air, foam, wool) slow heat down.

Houses use insulation in walls and attics to keep heat in during winter. A thermos has a vacuum layer, which stops conduction and convection, and shiny walls, which reflect radiation.

Remember: conduction = touching; convection = currents; radiation = waves.

Key words

  • conduction: heat transfer by direct contact
  • convection: heat transfer by moving currents in liquids or gases
  • radiation: heat transfer by waves that can travel through space
  • insulator: a material that slows heat transfer

See real-life examples

Winter coats

A down coat traps lots of air. Trapped air is a great insulator, so your body heat stays in.

Baseboard heaters

Warm air rises from heaters by the floor, crosses the ceiling, cools and sinks: a convection current that heats the whole room.

Feeling the Sun

Sunlight crosses 150 million km of empty space to warm your face. That is radiation.
