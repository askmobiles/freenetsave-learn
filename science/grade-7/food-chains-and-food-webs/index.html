Food chains and food webs

How energy flows through an ecosystem, and the 10% rule. Grade 7 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

Energy enters an ecosystem as sunlight. Producers (plants and algae) capture it. Consumers get energy by eating.

🌱Producers: make their own food
🐇Primary consumers: herbivores that eat producers
🦊Secondary consumers: eat primary consumers
🦅Tertiary consumers: top predators
🍄Decomposers: break down dead things

A food chain shows one path of energy. A food web shows many connected food chains, which is more realistic.

Each step in a food chain is a trophic level. Only about 10% of the energy passes to the next level; the rest is used for living or lost as heat. That is why there are many more plants than wolves, shown in an energy pyramid.

Some poisons, like mercury, build up as they move up the food chain. This is biomagnification, so top predators get the most.

Remember: energy flows from producers to consumers, and only about 10% moves up each level.

Key words

  • producer: a living thing that makes its own food
  • trophic level: a feeding step in a food chain
  • energy pyramid: a diagram showing energy decreasing at each level
  • biomagnification: poison building up as it moves up a food chain

Fish advice

Why so few wolves

A forest needs a huge amount of plants and many deer to feed just one wolf pack.
