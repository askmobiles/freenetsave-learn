The particle theory

The five ideas that explain how matter behaves. Grade 7 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

The particle theory of matter is a model scientists use to explain how matter behaves. It has five main ideas:

1️⃣All matter is made of tiny particles
2️⃣Particles are always moving
3️⃣There are spaces between particles
4️⃣Particles attract each other
5️⃣Adding heat makes particles move faster and spread apart

The theory explains the states of matter: in solids, particles are close and strongly attracted; in liquids, they slide past each other; in gases, they are far apart and move freely.

It also explains diffusion (a smell spreading across a room as particles move) and thermal expansion (things getting bigger when heated). Bridges have expansion joints so they do not crack in summer heat.

Remember: particles are tiny, moving, attracted, with spaces between, and move faster when heated.

Key words

  • particle theory: a model that explains how matter behaves using tiny moving particles
  • diffusion: particles spreading from crowded areas to less crowded areas
  • thermal expansion: matter getting bigger when heated
  • model: a simple way to explain something we cannot see

See real-life examples

Bridge joints

Bridges have metal "teeth" called expansion joints. They leave room for the bridge to grow in summer heat.

Popcorn smell

When you make popcorn, the smell spreads through the house. That is diffusion.

Thermometers

The liquid in a thermometer rises when it gets warm because its particles spread apart.
