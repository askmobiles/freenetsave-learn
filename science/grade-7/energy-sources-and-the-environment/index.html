Energy sources and the environment

How we produce electricity and heat, and the impact of each source. Grade 7 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

People use energy for electricity, heating and transportation. Each energy source has benefits and costs for the environment.

⛽Fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas): reliable and cheap to use, but release greenhouse gases and air pollution, and will run out
☢️Nuclear: no greenhouse gases while running, but makes radioactive waste that must be stored safely
💧Hydroelectric: renewable and clean, but dams flood land and change rivers
💨Wind: renewable, but only works when windy; turbines can harm birds and bats
☀️Solar: renewable and quiet, but only works in daylight and needs space
🌋Geothermal: steady heat from underground, but only in certain places

Ontario's electricity comes mostly from nuclear and hydro, with some natural gas, wind and solar. Ontario closed its last coal power plant in 2014, which greatly reduced smog days.

Using energy efficiently (getting the same job done with less energy) helps no matter which source we use: LED lights, heat pumps, insulation and electric vehicles.

Remember: every energy source has trade-offs; using less energy is always good.

Key words

  • greenhouse gas: a gas that traps heat in the atmosphere
  • radioactive waste: leftover material from nuclear power that gives off harmful radiation
  • energy efficiency: doing the same job with less energy
  • trade-off: a balance of benefits and costs

See real-life examples

Coal phase-out

Ontario closed all its coal plants by 2014. Smog warning days in Toronto dropped from dozens a year to almost none.

Heat pumps

A heat pump moves heat from outside air into a house. It can use much less energy than an older furnace.

Solar farms

Large solar farms in southern Ontario turn sunlight into electricity for the grid.
