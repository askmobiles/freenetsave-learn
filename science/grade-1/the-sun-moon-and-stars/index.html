The Sun, Moon and stars

What we see in the sky in the day and at night. Grade 1 science.

1 Learn2 See3 Try4 Play5 Test6 Challenge

Learn what you need to know

The Sun is a star. It is a huge, hot ball of glowing gas. It is the closest star to Earth, so it looks big and bright.

The Moon is a big rock that goes around the Earth. It does not make its own light. It shines because sunlight bounces off it.

🌑new moon
🌓half moon
🌕full moon
🌗half moon again

The Moon seems to change shape over about a month. These shapes are called phases.

Stars are suns that are very, very far away. That is why they look like tiny dots. Some stars make shapes called constellations.

Remember: the Sun is a star, the Moon reflects sunlight, stars are far-away suns.

Key words

  • star: a huge ball of hot glowing gas
  • Moon: a big rock that goes around the Earth
  • phases: the shapes the Moon seems to have

See real-life examples

Never look at the Sun

The Sun is so bright it can hurt your eyes. Never look straight at it, even with sunglasses.

Moon in the day

Sometimes you can see the Moon in the daytime, pale in the blue sky.

The Big Dipper

On a clear night you can find seven stars shaped like a big spoon. It is called the Big Dipper.
